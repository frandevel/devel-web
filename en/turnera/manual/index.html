The Turnera manual

How Turnera is used day to day in a centre: booking, charging, and knowing what happened. It is written for the person at the front desk, not for the person who writes the software.

Getting help

If something does not work as it should, report it from the application itself: at the bottom right of every screen there is the round lifebuoy button. Every user has it, with no special permission, because whoever runs into the problem is usually the person at the front desk.

  1. Click the lifebuoy. Report an incident.
  2. In What happened, describe what you were doing, what you expected to happen and what happened instead. A sentence like «it doesn't work» does not let us reproduce the problem, so the application will ask you for a little more.
  3. Choose the urgency: Low if it can wait, Normal if it is annoying but you can work, High if something cannot be done and Blocking if the centre has stopped.
  4. If it helps, attach a screenshot or a file of up to 5 MB. It travels in the email and is not stored on the platform.
  5. Enter your name and the email address you want us to reply to. The phone number is optional, in case we ever needed to talk to you.
  6. Click Send. The application gives you an incident number: keep it in case you need to refer to it.

You do not need to look for technical details. We send, along with the incident, the screen you were on, your user, the Turnera version and the last error you got. If the error message had a reference of eight letters and digits, it reaches us too: with it we find exactly what happened.

The answer comes by email, to the one you entered. If the application says the incident has been recorded but the notice could not be sent, and it is urgent, write to us at soporte@devel.es with the incident number.

Is it a question about how to do something? It is probably in this manual. It opens from the application itself with User manual, at the bottom of the left-hand menu next to the version, and also from the incident window. It opens in the language you have chosen in Turnera.

1. Logging in and finding your way around

Turnera opens in the browser, with the centre's address and the username and password you were given. There is nothing to install and it works the same on the front desk computer, on a tablet or on a phone.

Each person has their own user account. This is not red tape: the application records who did each thing, and with a shared account that information is worth nothing at all. What is more, each user sees only their own part — the receptionist has no need to open the clinical record, and the physiotherapist has no need to see the till.

The menu on the left

At the top, with no heading, is what you use every day: Home, Agenda, Point of sale, Unpaid visits and Customers. Below, grouped, is what you set up now and then (catalogue and stock), the team under Staff (professionals, work calendar and working time) and what you only look up (reports and settings).

If one of those entries is missing for you it is not a fault: your user account does not have that permission. Whoever administers the centre can give it to you.

It may also be that the centre does not have that part of Turnera. Some centres only use Turnera for their staff (work calendar, clocking in and the working-time record), with no agenda or till; they do not see the rest, not even whoever manages the centre. To add a part, write to support.

At the top of the menu is the centre's logo, if one has been uploaded under Administration → Centre details; if not, Turnera's.

Sorting any list

Every list —customers, suppliers, services, products, stock, outstanding payments, reports…— is sorted by clicking the column header. Once, from lowest to highest or from A to Z; again, the other way round; and a third time goes back to the usual order. The arrow next to the title tells you which column the list is sorted by and in which direction.

The whole list is sorted, not just the page on screen: sort the customers by registration date and go to page two, and it carries on where the first one ended. Anything empty —a customer with no email, a product with no brand— always goes last, in both directions, which is where you look for it.

Which version am I on

Right at the bottom of the left-hand menu, under the active delegation, is the Turnera version this centre is running. It is the first thing you are asked for when something does not work, so it is worth knowing where it is before you need it. Hovering over it also says when it was built. It reads like this: 2026.9.262-bdc9dd2 is from 2026, month 9, the 262nd change of that month; what comes after the hyphen identifies the exact change, and it is what you should copy when asking for help.

Languages

Turnera speaks Spanish, English, German and Portuguese (from Portugal). Each person picks their own in the selector at the top and it stays set; anyone who does not pick one sees the centre's, which is changed in Administration → Preferences.

Whatever a customer is given or sent does not come out in the language of whoever presses the button, but in the customer's own: on their record, Edit → Language. That covers tickets, quotes, reports, consent forms, booking-portal emails and payment links. If the record says The centre's, it comes out in the centre's language. So a German physiotherapist works in Turnera in German and prints the ticket in Spanish for her patient from Valencia.

What the centre writes itself -- the names of services and products, notes -- comes out as written. Consent forms and the WhatsApp reminder can have their text in each language: see Consent forms in other languages and The reminder in other languages.

Recommend Turnera

At the top, next to the language, whoever runs the centre sees the Recommend Turnera button. It opens the referral code of the centre -- always the same, with its name in front, like AURORA-7K2Q -- and the ways to pass it on: copy the code, copy the link to the Turnera page with the code already filled in, or send it by WhatsApp or email.

  • For you: one free month for every resource the recommended centre contracts. If it takes four rooms, four months. They are earned when that centre pays its first fee and come off your next invoices, up to twelve months a year.
  • For the recommended centre: the first month is free and, if they bring data from another program, the migration costs them €150 instead of €300.

At the foot of the dialog you can see how many months the centre has earned this year and whether any recommended centre has not paid its first fee yet. Only those who can change the centre's preferences see the button.

Home

The home screen sums up the day: the appointments still to come, what has been charged, what is still unpaid and the birthdays. Each block is a shortcut — click it and you land on the screen that explains it.

The customers block also brings the last five sign-ups; click anywhere on the row and the record opens. It is for everyday work: someone registered a customer a while ago, their phone number needs adding or they need an appointment, and searching for them by a surname that may have been misspelt is the long way round.

Below are the birthdays of the week, with the day and the age each customer turns. Today's is highlighted, and the row also leads to the record, in case you want to wish them happy birthday or give them a present.

In a centre that manages its staff (the work calendar and attendance control), the home screen adds cards for it:

  • Who is in now: how many of the professionals on shift today are in, and since what time. Below it, who should already have clocked in and has not, with the time their shift began: it is almost always an oversight, and seeing it in time avoids a gap that would have to be corrected later.
  • Holidays and absences: who is away today and who will be during the week, with the reason and the days.
  • Hours this month: the worked hours against the planned ones, the out-of-shift hours of the month and who has the most. Below it, the overtime bank of the year: what is still to be compensated and who is getting close to the legal ceiling of 80 hours (from 80 %), has gone over it or was given a rest too late.

Whoever can look up the clock-ins sees the attendance and hours cards; whoever can see the work calendars sees the holidays and absences card. If nobody has been scheduled yet and nobody has clocked in, they do not appear.

2. The agenda

Menu → Agenda

This is the screen you spend the day on. You can look at it in three ways, and the button at the top left switches between them:

ViewOne column perWhat it is for
Day by member of staff Each member of staff The usual one: who is seeing whom today. It is the one you get when you log in.
Day by room Each room, court or studio When what you are sharing out is the place and not the person.
Week by member of staff Each day of the week For booking someone who asks “what have you got next week?”.

The view you choose is remembered: when you come back tomorrow it will be as you left it.

The line of the hour

A dashed red line crosses the agenda at the current time, with the time written on the left. Everything above it has already happened; the appointment the line runs through is the one that should be under way right now. It moves by itself, with no reloading.

It only shows when the agenda is on today —in the week view, only over today's column— and within the hours the grid paints. If you look at next Monday there is no line, because there is nothing to mark.

Booking, moving and cancelling appointments

  • Booking an appointment: click the free slot where you want it to go. The form opens with the time and the member of staff already filled in; all that is missing is the customer and the service.
  • Opening an appointment: click it.
  • Past days: you can go to any earlier day to look at it, open its appointments, charge them or mark who did not come. What you cannot do is book a new appointment or move one to a day before today: the agenda says so when you click a slot, and the appointment calendar does not let you pick those days. Doing it needs the «Citar en días pasados» (book on past days) permission, which out of the box only the administrator has. Today you can book at any time, also at a time that has already passed: noting down at ten twenty the ten o'clock one is normal at the front desk.
  • Taking it to another day: open it and press Cut. A bar appears above the agenda with the appointment you are moving, and its card stays faded in its place. Go to the day you want, click the free slot and confirm with Paste: the whole appointment moves, with its services, comments and voucher, and if the slot is taken you are told and can pick another. Until you paste it nothing has changed: with Cancel or the Escape key it stays as it was. Once pasted, the agenda offers Notify on WhatsApp to tell the customer the new day.
  • What can be moved: an appointment that changes day goes back to Absent and unconfirmed, because what was known belonged to the previous day. An appointment in progress can be moved to another time or cabin that same day, but not to another day: if it is interrupted, it is closed as appropriate and a new one is booked. An appointment already charged or cancelled does not move: it records what happened that day. An attended or one of someone who did not come can only be moved by whoever has the permission «Mover citas atendidas» (move attended appointments), which out of the box the administrator has: it is meant for one marked by mistake or for the customer who did not come and calls to change it. Without the permission it does not show Cut, and if you try to drag it, the diary says which permission is needed.
  • Moving it: drag it. If you drop it in another column it also changes member of staff or room, depending on the view you are in.
  • Changing how long it lasts: drag the bottom edge.
  • Cancelling it: open it, press Cancel and say who is calling it off: the customer, the centre (the professional cannot make it, the cabin or the court is unavailable, something broke) or an external cause (rain on an outdoor court, a power cut). It is required, because only what the customer calls off counts in their history and in lost appointments. You can also write the reason in your own words, which is kept in the comments of the appointment.
  • An appointment already charged can only be cancelled by whoever has the «Anular citas ya cobradas» (cancel charged appointments) permission, which out of the box only the administrator has. For everyone else, Cancel is greyed out and, when you hover over it, says why. Cancelling it does not give the money back: for that, use the ticket refund. Through the booking portal, the customer cannot cancel an appointment already paid either: they have to call the centre.
  • Removing an appointment made by mistake: within the 15 minutes after creating it, a fourth option appears when cancelling it, Created by mistake. The appointment disappears from the agenda and counts in no report, because it was never a booking. After that time it is no longer offered: it is a cancellation by someone, and you have to say by whom.
  • Taking cancelled appointments off the view: tick Hide cancelled, in the legend under the title. The diary remembers the box, so next time you will find it as you left it.

No need to reload. Whatever is changed at another desk, whatever customers book online and whatever comes in from a connected program shows up on its own in the agenda you have open. A new appointment on the day you are looking at pulses for a moment so you notice it, without moving the screen from where you had it.

What each person can do in the diary depends on their permissions. Whoever cannot book sees the whole diary, but gets no new appointment button, clicking a free slot does nothing and appointments cannot be dragged or stretched: clicking one opens it to read, without a save button. The buttons to close off time, the waiting list and printing only show up for whoever has their permission. Inside an appointment, Cancel, Charge and WhatsApp are always shown, but switched off for whoever cannot use them; hovering over them says why.

The appointment window can be moved aside. Drag it by its title to see the part of the diary it covers while you create or change an appointment. Charge first saves whatever was changed in the appointment —an added service, for instance— and then takes you to the till. And if the customer record is opened from the appointment, its Back goes back to the open appointment, not to the customer list.

The service sets the duration. When you pick the services for the appointment, the duration adjusts itself to what they add up to, rounding up to the slot of that room: two twenty-minute services in a room that runs in fifteens take forty-five, not forty. The moment you touch the duration selector by hand, you are in charge and we stop touching it. On an appointment that already exists nothing is recalculated either: its duration is the one it has.

Quick selection by code. Next to the appointment's services there is the button Quick selection: it opens all the short codes of the price list at a glance —HMBS, AXIL…— so you can click two or three in a row, and on accepting they are added to the appointment. Each code is a single service. The code is set in Catalogue → Services, in the record of each one (Short code: letters and digits, and it cannot be repeated). In the services drop-down it also appears before the name, and you can search by it. Vouchers do not appear in the appointment: a voucher is sold at the till, it is not a treatment given in a room. If the appointment has several services, they are all shown at once in the field, one after another, without having to open the drop-down.

Cancelling cannot be undone. A cancelled appointment stays in view, crossed out — its slot is free again to book over, or it can be taken off the view with Hide cancelled —, but it cannot be recovered: it has to be created again. And marking someone as No show also cancels the appointment, so do not use it for “has not arrived yet”.

If you try to book two appointments at once in the same room or with the same member of staff, the application will not let you and tells you which one it clashes with. It is the only thing it truly blocks: the rest are warnings.

What each card tells you

The background of the card is the category of the service —laser, treatment room, medicine…—, that of its first service when it has several, and below the legend you see the categories of that day with their colour. An appointment without a service is grey. Each category comes with a default colour, which is changed in Catalogue → Services → Agenda colours: choose the shade, see a sample of the card and press Save. A particular service can have its own colour, which wins over its category's: it is chosen in its form, under Colour in the diary, and Use the category's puts it back to the usual one.

The status of the appointment is the bar on the left edge — waiting, in progress, attended, no-show —, with the usual colours, and the legend under the title explains them. With the dark theme the same statuses are painted lighter, and the «no-show» one, which is almost black on a light background, becomes almost white. In front of the name comes the customer number.

An appointment outlined in red has already finished and has not gone through the till. At the end of the day there should be none left: either it is charged or it is marked as a no-show. Cancelled ones are not marked. On top of the colour there are also these signs:

Waiting list

Agenda → Waiting list

Somebody asks for a time that is already taken. Instead of writing it on a piece of paper, it goes on that day's waiting list: who they are, what they wanted done, the stretch that would suit them and whatever is worth knowing when you call. The agenda button shows up front how many are waiting on the day you are looking at.

When a booking is cancelled, the screen says so: “two people are on the waiting list for this day”. Whoever cancels decides who to call — the list goes in order of arrival —, and once they are given a time they are taken off the list.

Turnera neither calls nor writes to anybody. It is on purpose: notifying on its own, the first in line and with a deadline to reply, is a whole feature —with its queue, its retries and its “what if two of them answer”— and deserves to be built with real use in front of it. The usual thing is a list with two names, settled with one phone call.

The phone number only shows on the list for whoever holds the permission for the contact details of the customer, just like on the customer record. And taking somebody off deletes nothing: the row keeps who took them off and when, so tomorrow you can say who was called and who was not.

Closing stretches in the agenda

Agenda → Close a stretch

A bank holiday, a spell of leave, a room being serviced or a broken device. You pick the day, the time band, what it affects — the whole centre, one room or one person — and the reason.

The closed stretch appears hatched on the grid, with the reason written over it. The reason is not compulsory but it is worth putting in: in two weeks' time nobody will remember why that Thursday is closed, and it ends in a phone call.

Closing is not forbidding. On the booking portal the closed slot disappears and the customer does not see it. At the front desk, on the other hand, you get a warning and can carry on: if the room frees up sooner than expected, you know it and the program does not.

Printing the agenda

Agenda → Print

It prints the day as a PDF, one member of staff per sheet, with the time, the customer, their phone number, the room, the service and the notes. First visits are marked on the paper too.

Click the button and you get the whole centre. Open the little arrow and you pick a single person. This is what you print in the morning so that everyone has their day beside the couch and so that, if the power goes, the centre keeps running.

3. Customers

Menu → Customers

The search box at the top searches by first name, surname, phone, tax ID or customer number. For the first name and surnames, the start of each wordis enough: «iba» finds Ibáñez but not Arribas, and «ana gar» finds Ana García. Phone and tax ID are found by any fragment; the customer number must be exact. It is the same search as in the appointment and at the till. The list is sorted by clicking the header of any column (how).

The customer number appears everywhere: in the list, in the record, on the diary card, in the appointment and payment search, in pending payments and in the reports. Customers who came from another platform keep the number they had there, and new ones get the next one when they are registered. And a customer's name in any list is a link to their record, just like on the home screen; in the appointment and at the checkout, the card button next to the customer also takes you there.

A customer record holds their details, their visit history, their vouchers and their notes. The phone number and the email address are links: tapping them opens the dialler or the mail programme, with nothing to copy and paste. It happens throughout the application — staff, suppliers and delegations too — so there is never a number to copy out by hand.

Deactivating a customer

A customer who no longer comes is not deleted: they are deactivated. In their record, Edit and untick Active. They keep everything —appointments, payments, vouchers, history—, but no longer show up in searches: in the customer list, when booking an appointment and at checkout. To see them, tick Include inactive next to the search box; they appear with the tag Inactive, and in their record, Inactive. Reactivating them is ticking again Active.

When the tick is removed, before saving, Turnera warns about what is left pending: the appointments from today on (which stay in the diary: cancel them if the customer is not coming), what they owe and the vouchers with sessions left unused. It warns; it does not stop you.

When the customer asks for their data to be erased

Customer record → Erase their data

This is the right to erasure under the GDPR. It is done by whoever has the permission Erase the data of a customer who asks for it (by default, management). Their name, ID document, phone numbers, email, address, date of birth, notes, alerts, waiting list and portal access are erased, and their appointments from today on are cancelled. The record stays, with the name Datos borrados and its number, because its visits, payments and vouchers refer to it, and they stay in the centre's accounts.

What the law requires to be kept is kept: the invoices, as they are, and the clinical history (at least five years, under Spanish Act 41/2002), blocked: it appears on no screen, but stays identified in case the customer or a court asks for it. Before erasing, the screen says what is erased, what is kept and whether appointments, debts or vouchers are left pending, and asks for the name and surnames to be typed by hand.

It cannot be undone. Only do it when the customer has asked for it, and keep their request.

Going to their next appointment

The record has a upcoming appointments with the ones that customer has ahead, each with its own See in the agendabutton. Clicking it opens the agenda of the right day and the appointment pulses by itself for three seconds, with the screen scrolled to it.

After those three seconds the pulse fades and the agenda is the one you always use. That is on purpose: whoever gets there has come to move the appointment, to charge it or to look at the one next to it, and an agenda you have to «unlock» with a click before you can touch it gets in the way exactly when you were about to work.

Club points

If the centre switches it on in Administration → Preferences, every purchase gives the customer points, and those points can later be used as a payment method. Two figures are set: how many points each euro spent gives and how many points each euro costs when redeeming them. With one and one, a euro is a point.

Neither paying with a voucher nor paying with points gives points, for the obvious reason: with the first the money came in the day the voucher was bought, and with the second no money comes in.

Where the balance comes from

A member's record carries their balance and, underneath, the history of movements: every line says what happened, how many points changed and what they were left with. A purchase, a redemption, a cancelled sale, an adjustment by hand or two records merged.

This is what you show when a customer argues about their balance. The balance used to be a loose number going up and down with no trace; while only the till touched it you could live with that, but once management can give points away, every one of them has to be explainable.

Paying with points

At the point of sale, points are one more way to pay, with the centre's own name behind them — Aurora points —, and they are only offered if the customer is a member and has enough balance for what is being charged. Whoever does not have enough is not shown them, so nobody has to try at the counter to find out.

Adding or taking away points by hand

With Adjust points they are given or taken away, positive or negative, and the reason is compulsory: it is stored as written and shown in the history. It needs the administratorpermission, because giving points away is giving money away and is not a counter task.

The balance cannot go below zero: if you try to take away more than they have, Turnera says so and does nothing.

Alerts

An alert is what to keep in mind when booking or charging that customer: «allergic to nickel, no Indiba». It is written in their record, under Alerts, and from then on it shows up by itself right at the top when you open one of their appointments and when you charge them at the till.

An alert is not a note. A note is a diary: it is read when somebody opens it, which is exactly what does not happen with the phone in your hand giving an appointment. An alert is short, it is live or retired, and it puts itself in front of you without anybody looking for it. That is why they are two different things in two different places.

It shows as a red banner, not as a message you close. That is on purpose: what is dismissed with a click is dismissed without reading it, and a real alert has to stay there while the treatment is being chosen, which is the moment it matters.

It is written and retired by whoever can edit the record; it is also seen by whoever only has the agenda, because otherwise the alert never reaches the person booking and is good for nothing. Withdraw does not delete: the alert stops showing up and who retired it and when stays on record.

No diagnoses here. The whole counter sees it. An alert carries what is needed to give an appointment or to charge; the clinical part goes to the history, which has its own permission and leaves a trace of who opens it.

Duplicate records

It happens in every centre: the same person added twice, with the surname misspelt one of the times. Turnera lets you merge the two records, and in doing so everything from the other one is carried over to the good one: appointments, visits, vouchers and clinical record. Nothing is lost.

A merge cannot be undone. Before confirming it, check on screen that the record that stays is the right one. To confirm you have to type by hand the name of the record that is going to be deleted; pasting it does not work, so nobody confirms without having read it.

4. Charging

The point of sale

Menu → Point of sale

You choose the customer and the professional, and from there you add services and products to the ticket. The payment method comes from the list of those the centre has enabled.

The discount can be set in two ways. With %, as a percentage: the most used ones (5, 10, 15 and 20 %) are one tap away, and any other can be typed. With Final price you say how much you want to charge —“make it 50”— and Turnera works out the discount and spreads it across the lines the customer pays; underneath it shows how much is taken off and what percentage that is, and the total appears with the previous price struck through. It also offers, one tap away, the round prices just below (with €57.30, 55 and 50). The final price only lowers: if you type one higher than the total, it warns you and does not let you charge.

The short way is from the agenda. Open the appointment and click Charge: the point of sale opens with the customer, the member of staff and that appointment's services already filled in. All you have to say is how they pay. And once charged, the appointment is marked as paid — that is the euro icon on the grid.

If the appointment changes with the ticket half done —you go back to the agenda and add a service that was done in the end—, on returning to the POS a notice shows what the appointment has now and two buttons: Update the ticket adds what is new (and removes what was removed from the appointment), and Keep it as it is changes nothing. It is not updated on its own because what was removed or added at the counter may be on purpose.

And shorter still: the top of the POS. As soon as a professional marks an appointment as Attended, the customer appears at the top, under To charge, before they reach the counter. Below are the ones still In progress, who come next. Tap the card and the sale loads just as it does from the agenda. The list refreshes itself every few seconds, only shows today's appointments, and each customer drops off it as soon as they are charged or the visit is left as a debt.

  • Take payment now ticked: you choose the payment method and the money goes into today's till.
  • Take payment now unticked: the visit is left as a debt and shows up in Unpaid visits. As nothing is paid today, the payment method, the split and the amount handed over disappear, and the button changes to Leave as debt. The payment method is chosen on the day it is paid.
  • Tendered: type in what you are handed and the screen works out the change.

A product is sold even if the stock says there are none left: if it is on the counter, it is there. The unit leaves the stock —which stays at zero, never negative— and the outgoing movement is recorded; the stock count is what later sets the right figure. When the sale is refunded or cancelled, the unit comes back.

The Quote button prints as a PDF what is on the ticket without charging anything, to hand to someone who is still thinking about it.

Look things up without losing the ticket. The icon i of each service or product in the search, and the name of each ticket line, open its record in a panel on the right: price, VAT, duration, code, units left in this centre… It closes and the ticket stays the same. And if you need to leave the checkout —to the customer record, to the agenda—, the half-finished ticket stays saved on that workstation: when you come back it appears as it was, with the notice The ticket has been recovered and the button Discard it to start a clean one. It belongs to each user and each centre, it is deleted when the sale is charged and it expires after twelve hours.

Packages

Menu → Catalogue → Packages

A package is a closed price for several things together: “aloe vera moisturiser + leg waxing, 50 €”. It can carry services, products or both, and each line with as many units as it needs.

The price of the package is optional. Left blank, the package is worth whatever its parts add up to, and goes up by itself the day a service goes up. Filled in, the package is an offer: the difference is split across its lines in proportion to what each one costs.

Why it is split and not left on one line. The package goes onto the ticket as the lines it carries inside, not as a single one. That way each one takes its own VAT to the accounting draft, and a voucher can pay for the waxing while the cream is charged. If the discount stayed whole on one line, the VAT and the till would come out wrong.

With the example above: a 24.20 € cream and a 30.00 € waxing, package at 50 €. The cream ends up at 22.32 € and the waxing at 27.68 €, which add up to the 50 that are charged. And if a voucher covers the waxing, what is not paid is those 27.68 €, not the 30 of the tariff.

To sell one, the point of sale has a third tab, Packages, next to services and products. It is added like everything else and appears on the ticket under its name; the voucher box behaves as it does on a single service, in case the customer would rather pay for it and keep the session.

A package is not deleted: it is taken off. It stops being offered at the point of sale, and the tickets that already carry it stay as they are.

A service or a product is not deleted either: it is deactivated, and whatever was sold with it stays as it was. But it cannot be retired while something still uses it: a package on sale that includes it, a voucher on sale of its same group (the service is where it is spent) or, for a service, appointments from today on. Turnera says which ones, and it has to be removed from them (or those appointments changed or cancelled) first.

Payment methods with a fixed discount

Some payment methods come with the discount built in. Gift is a hundred per cent: choose it and the total goes to zero by itself. There is nothing to remember, and you cannot charge by mistake for something you are giving away.

The payment methods the POS offers are cash, card, Bizum, bank transfer, financing, gift and gift card, and, when relevant, club points and the customer's credit balance. These last two only appear if there is a customer with a balance.

Bizum is recorded by hand: the customer sends it from their phone and, when it is seen arriving, it is charged with that payment method. It earns club points like a card and appears on its own line of the cash closing, which is checked against the bank statement.

Coupons

Menu → Catalogue → Coupons

A coupon is a code that takes money off the ticket: SUMMER25, WELCOME. It is handed out on a flyer or online, the customer says it at the counter and it is typed in when charging. It can take off euros — 10 € off — or a percentage — 15% of the whole ticket —, and it takes whatever limits are needed: from when it is valid, until when, how many times in total, and whether the same person may use it more than once.

When charging, the code goes in the Coupon box of the ticket, above the discount. It does not matter how it is typed: it is always stored in capitals. If the coupon does not work, the screen says why, because the cases are not the same: it does not exist, it has not started yet, its date has passed, every use is gone, or that customer already used it.

The discount is shared between the lines, it does not stay on one. Just like with packages: a 10 € coupon on a 42.50 € ticket leaves the 30 € service at 22.94 and the 12.50 product at 9.56. That way each line takes its own VAT to the accounting draft and the day's till adds up.

A 10 € coupon on a 6 € ticket takes off 6: the centre does not give the difference back. And on the listing, each coupon says how many times it has been used and how much money has been given away with it, which is the answer to “what did that campaign cost us”.

A coupon is not deleted: it is taken off. It stops working at the counter and what was already taken off with it stays where it was. Seeing the coupons and creating them are two different permissions: reception sees them, management invents them.

If a charge does not go through, the notice says why and what to do. For example, that the customer has no voucher sessions left for the whole ticket and the rest has to be charged another way, or that the business line for sales has not been chosen in Administration → Preferences → The centre. If it is a server failure, the notice carries a reference of eight letters and digits: with it, support finds exactly what happened.

Paying with a voucher

If the customer holds a voucher with sessions left, the till applies it by itself. Every service on the ticket that belongs to that voucher's group comes out at 0,00 € with the mark Voucher: covers 1 of 1, and the rest of the ticket (services from other groups, products) is paid today with whichever payment method you choose. The totals show the line Covered by vouchers with what the customer has saved.

The payment method is already set to Voucher. If the voucher pays for everything, there is nothing else to choose: it is charged and the session is deducted. If something is left to pay, below appears The rest is paid with to choose how. If the customer prefers to pay and keep the session, choose another payment method instead of Voucher: the whole ticket goes back to its price and the voucher is not touched.

To keep the session of a single service and use it on the others, untick that line's box: it goes back to its price and the voucher is not touched. It is useful for the customer who wants to keep the sessions they have left, or who is paying for someone else today.

When there are several services from the same group and the voucher does not stretch to all of them, it covers the most expensive ones first. And if the visit is left as a debt, the sessions are not spent until it is paid: the voucher stays whole on the customer's record in the meantime.

On the receipt every line paid with a voucher comes out as (voucher session), and the day's cash only counts the money that has really come in.

Paying by two methods at once

The bill is a hundred euros, the customer has fifty in cash and wants to put the rest on a card. Tick “Split the payment across several payment methods” and two lines appear: on each one you pick the method and how much goes on it. You can add more.

Below you can see the sum of the split next to the total. Until it balances to the cent, shown in red, the charge button does not light up. This is deliberate: a split that does not add up is a discrepancy in the till that turns up weeks later and that by then nobody remembers.

The ticket comes out with the breakdown: how much went on each one. And the end-of-day till splits that payment between the two methods, it does not put it all into one.

Unpaid visits

Menu → Unpaid visits

Here are the visits that were seen without being paid for. Click one and the payment dialog opens: you pick the payment method, adjust the discount if need be and settle it. Afterwards you can print the ticket.

When it is settled, Turnera checks again whether the customer has a voucher, because they may have bought one after leaving it owed. If a voucher covers any line, the payment method shows Voucher: those lines go to zero, labelled Voucher, and the session is deducted on settling. If anything else is left, you choose how the rest is paid. If the voucher pays for everything, there is nothing to charge and no ticket to print: only the session is used up. And if the customer prefers to pay and keep the session, choose any other payment method and the voucher is left untouched.

Refunding a ticket

Customer record → History → Refund

The customer comes back with the cream unopened, or a service was charged that in the end was not done. In the history of their record, every paid ticket has the button Refund. The ticket opens with its lines and you tick the ones being refunded: there is no need to refund all of it. Whatever was already refunded shows as Refunded and cannot be ticked again.

Then you choose how it is refunded:

  • As money, the way it came in: what was paid in cash leaves the till and what was paid by card goes back to the card.
  • As store credit: the money stays in the house and the customer spends it another day. The next time they are charged, Store credit shows up as one more payment method.

If the ticket was paid split between two payment methods, the refund goes back split the same way, in the same proportion: from a ticket paid almost entirely by card, almost everything goes back to the card and only what came in as cash leaves the till.

What was paid with points always goes back as points, and what was paid with store credit goes back to the credit: refunding them does not turn them into money. You can write the reason, and it is worth doing.

Refunding undoes everything the sale moved. The product goes back to stock (unless it comes back opened or broken: when the line is ticked, the box Back to stockappears, and if it is unticked the money is refunded but the product is not restocked), the session a voucher paid goes back to that voucher, and the customer loses the points the purchase gave them (if they already spent them, they lose whatever they have left, never going negative). If what is refunded is a voucher that was sold, the voucher disappears from their record, but only if it is untouched: with one that has sessions used it is not known which part to refund, and the application does not allow it.

The original ticket is not touched. The refund is a new ticket, with the lines in negative, marked in the history as Refund of the ticket it undoes; in the original, the refunded lines carry the mark refunded, and once none are left to refund it no longer offers the button. That way the day's takings match the cash: what left today is taken off today, not on the day of the sale. And a coupon used in the sale is not recovered.

The ticket of a refund prints like any other, from the history, and says at the top Refund of ticket and the number of the one it undoes. A product that did not go back to stock has it noted on its line.

If the customer has store credit, their record warns about it at the top, and the till says how much is left when choosing the payment method. If the credit does not cover the whole bill, split the charge: one part with the credit and the rest with another payment method. Refunding is a separate permission, Refunding a ticket, which out of the box only the management role has.

Gift cards

Menu → Gift cards

Someone pays today and another person spends it another day. It is sold with Sell a gift card: who pays for it, who sells it, the amount, the payment method and, if wanted, who it is for, their email, a dedication and until when it is valid (without a date, it does not expire). Once charged, a code like RG-7K2M-Q9TXcomes out, which is printed on a card to give away or emailed with the card as a PDF.

The money comes in the day it is sold, not the day it is spent. The sale is a ticket charged like any other and counts in that day's takings. Spending it brings nothing in: in the cash closing it shows up separately, as Gift card, just like a voucher. That is why a gift card cannot be paid with points, store credit or another card.

To spend it, choose Gift card as the payment method at the till and type the code; what is left is shown below. It can be spent in several visits until it runs out, and if it does not cover the whole bill the charge is split: one part with the card and the rest another way. A debt can also be settled with it.

At the top of the list is what is still to be spent: what is left on all the cards that are still valid. It is money the centre has already charged and owes in services and products, and it is what has to be kept in mind when closing the year. Each card says whether it is valid, spent, expired or refunded.

If something paid with a gift card is refunded, the money goes back to the card. And the sale of a card can be refunded from the buyer's history while it is untouched: it then stops being valid. Seeing the cards and selling them are two permissions; out of the box reception and management have them.

Invoices and VeriFactu

Administration → Invoicing and VeriFactu

Turnera can invoice every sale the way VeriFactu requires. It comes switched off: until it is turned on, charging works as always and the ticket carries no invoice number. It is turned on by whoever holds the permission Invoicing and VeriFactu, which by default only the administrator has, and first the tax ID, company name and registered address (street, postcode and town) of the centre are needed in Preferences: the invoice has to say who issues it and where.

With invoicing on, every paid sale comes with its simplified invoice, which is the usual ticket with a number, issue date, the VAT breakdown and, at the top left, the QR code with “QR tributario:” above it. Its lines and total are those of the invoice; what the ticket carries and is not invoiced (a session paid with a voucher, the sale of a gift card) appears separately, under Not included in this invoice, saying why. A corrective invoice says which invoice it corrects and the reason. Once the centre sends its invoices to the AEAT, “VERI*FACTU” appears below the QR and “Factura verificable en la sede electrónica de la AEAT” next to it; while it does not send them, they do not, because it would not be true. Without invoicing switched on there is no invoice and no QR: the usual ticket is printed. The invoice goes with the sale, not with the payment: what is left owing is invoiced right then all the same, and collecting it is the centre's business. If the amount changes when the debt is settled —a discount of the payment method, or a voucher that takes over a session—, the difference goes into a corrective invoice. A refund produces a corrective invoice, in negative, stating which invoice it corrects. Sessions paid with a voucher are not invoiced (the voucher was invoiced when sold, with its VAT), nor is the sale of a gift card, which is money on account: what is bought with it is invoiced. What is paid with club points counts as a discount: the invoice is for what is paid with money.

In production, an issued invoice is never deleted or changed. If something is wrong, it is refunded and its corrective invoice is issued. And the VAT of every service and product must be decided in the catalogue: without it, the sale cannot be charged and Turnera says what is missing.

Each delegation numbers in three series: simplified, full and corrective. The number is the prefix, the year and a counter that starts again every year, such as S1-2027/000123. The centre chooses the prefix on the same screen; while the series has no invoices it can be changed, and after that changing it opens a new series.

If the customer asks for a full invoice with their tax ID, it is done from their history, with the Full invoice button of the ticket: enter their tax ID, name and address, and an invoice is issued that replaces the ticket. It needs the permission Hacer factura completa (issue a full invoice), which out of the box front desk and management have.

Every invoice is in Reports → Invoice book (permission Libro de facturas (invoice book)), with how each one stands at the Spanish tax agency. From there the book is downloaded in Excel with the daily summary entry, which is what the accountant asks for every quarter.

Test mode and moving to production

Administration → Invoicing and VeriFactu → Invoicing mode

Invoicing has two modes, and every centre starts in test mode. It is for trying it out calmly before invoicing for real: charging, refunding, issuing a full invoice, looking at the book and downloading the Excel, without fear of breaking anything.

  • In test mode, every invoice carries at the top, in red, FACTURA DE PRUEBA · SIN VALIDEZ FISCAL (test invoice, not valid for tax purposes). It is not valid for the tax agency or the customer.
  • They are numbered like real ones, but that numbering is provisional.
  • In the invoice book they carry the label Test, and the Excel says so in the name of its sheets.
  • While the centre is in test mode there is a notice at the top of the screen for whoever handles invoicing.
  • If sending is on, records go to the AEAT test environment, never to the real one.

When the centre is going to invoice for real, with the catalogue VAT reviewed with its accountant, press Move to production…. The screen explains what will happen and asks you to tick that you understand and to type PRODUCCIÓN. On confirming:

  1. Every test invoice is deleted for good, with its records. Sales and payments are not touched.
  2. Every series of every delegation starts again: the next invoice is number 1.
  3. From then on every invoice is real and cannot be deleted or changed, only corrected.

Moving to production cannot be undone. There is no way back to test mode: the screen shows since when the centre has been in production and who took the step. It needs its own permission, Pasar la facturación a producción (move invoicing to production), which out of the box only the administrator has.

Sending to the AEAT

Administration → Invoicing and VeriFactu → Sending to the AEAT

Send means that Turnera sends the Spanish tax agency, on its own and as soon as each invoice is issued, a copy of its data: what the rules call an «invoicing record». Nobody has to do anything invoice by invoice.

  • What is sent: the number, the date, the kind of invoice, the description of what was sold, the VAT breakdown and the total; for a full invoice, also the customer's name and tax ID.
  • What is not sent: the invoice PDF, or anything from the customer's file or clinical record.
  • That way the AEAT keeps its own copy, and whoever holds the invoice can check it by scanning its QR.
  • While sending is off, records are kept in Turnera in order and, when it is turned on, they are all sent, oldest first: none is lost.

Sending is a second switch, also off. Turning it on needs two things, and no certificate has to be uploaded:

  1. Authorise DEVEL before the AEAT, done by the centre once: with the centre's certificate, on the tax agency's website, an authorisation (apoderamiento) in favour of DEVEL is registered to send invoicing records. It is only good for that, and the centre can withdraw it whenever it wants. Once done, tell support through Report an incident and we mark it on that screen.
  2. Turnera's connection with the AEAT, prepared by DEVEL: it is our certificate, with which Turnera identifies itself when sending on the centre's behalf. It is installed once on the server.

The screen shows how each of the two stands, and the switch cannot be turned on until both are ready. If the AEAT rejects a record or they get stuck, a warning appears at the top of the screen for whoever handles invoicing, and it reaches our support. If the AEAT says DEVEL is no longer authorised, the authorisation goes back to Pending and sending stops until it is fixed, without losing anything.

5. Vouchers

A voucher is sessions paid for in advance from a group of services. They are sold in two ways:

  • On their own: in the customer record, in the vouchers section, you create one by hand giving the group, the sessions and when it expires.
  • When selling a service marked as a voucher: the voucher is created by itself when you charge.

How they are used up

A session is deducted when paying at the till, for every service on the ticket that belongs to the voucher's group and is left ticked as covered (see Paying with a voucher). Services from other groups and products on the same ticket are paid with the normal payment method. It can also be deducted by hand from the customer's record, for when someone was seen without going through the till.

Marking an appointment as seen does not use up the voucher. The voucher is deducted when you charge, not when you see the customer. If in your centre people are seen on a voucher without going through the point of sale, someone has to deduct the session by hand from the record, or the balance will stop adding up.

On the agenda, the voucher icon on each appointment says how many sessions that customer has left. Vouchers that are used up or expired are not counted.

Withdrawing a voucher

When it is withdrawn from the customer record, the voucher can no longer be used and disappears from the record and the POS, but it is not deleted: the tickets that charged it and the transfers still say which voucher they were for. If the money also has to be given back, it is done through the refund of the ticket that sold it.

Transferring a voucher to another customer

Customer record → Vouchers → Transfer

Someone buys a voucher of ten sessions, uses three and moves away. The voucher can be passed to another customer of the centre: press Transfer on the voucher's line, look up whoever receives it and that is it. The reason is optional.

A transfer cannot be undone. If you picked the wrong customer, you have to transfer it back the other way, and then both transfers will show. That is deliberate: the day someone asks why their voucher has fewer sessions than they bought, that trail is all there is to answer them with.

Every transfer records from whom to whom, how many sessions were left, who did it and the date and the time. It shows on both records, in the block Transferred vouchers: on the one who gave it and on the one who received it.

The sessions that were left are stored exactly as they stood that day, because the voucher keeps being used afterwards and otherwise, a year from now, nobody would know how much whoever received it used up.

What cannot be done

  • An expired voucher cannot be transferred. What is no longer good for whoever bought it is no good for whoever receives it either, and transferring it would be a quiet way of bringing it back to life. If you want to give it more time, correct the expiry date first.
  • Nor one with no sessions left. There is nothing to pass on.
  • Only between customers of the same centre.

A transfer does not move the sale. Whoever paid for the voucher paid for it: nothing is invoiced again and nothing is credited. It is an assignment between customers, not a till operation, and it does not show up in the cash close.

It needs the TRASPASAR BONOSpermission, which is not the same as the one for attendance used to sell and use up vouchers: changing the owner of a voucher already in use is not the same as charging for it, and not every receptionist has to be able to do it. It is granted in Administration → Users.

6. Equipment

Administration → Devices

A device is not a room. The ultrasound scanner is carried from one cubicle to another and the laser goes wherever it is needed, but there is only one and only one person can use it at a time. That is what this screen sorts out.

Adding a device

For each device you state:

  • The name and how many units there are. With two, two appointments at once are fine and three are not.
  • Which services need it. They are moved from the Available list to the Use it list, with a search box in both. Vouchers do not appear: they are sold, not given as treatments. Until one is chosen, the equipment gets in nobody's way.
  • What has to be recorded each time it is used: the joules on the laser, the power on the sunbed. You add a field with “Add a field” and remove it with the bin on its row.

Each field that is recorded is one of three types:

TypeWhat it asks forWhen to use it

A device that a service uses is not deleted: Turnera says which services use it and it has to be removed from them first, so that no service is left asking for a device that is no longer there. Switching it off is allowed. A device that has already been used is not deleted: it is switched off. When you hit delete, if it has sessions recorded, Turnera deactivates it instead of deleting it, stops checking it when booking and keeps the settings each customer was treated with. For the same reason, a field with recorded sessions cannot be removed from the list: on saving a warning appears and the device stays as it was. Switching the device off is also the way to carry on booking while it is away being repaired.

What changes on the agenda

If two appointments at the same time need the same piece of equipment and there are not units for both, Turnera warns before saving the second one, even if they are in different rooms and with different professionals. The warning says which piece of equipment it is and which other appointment has it taken: the time, the customer and the professional. With that in front of you, you decide: change the time, or save it anyway.

Equipment warns; the room and the professional still block. The difference is real: two appointments do not fit in the same room and one person is not in two places at once, but the laser is carried from one room to another and whoever is treating knows whether there is time. Turnera cannot know that, so it says so and lets you decide.

Recording the session

Agenda → (an appointment) → Equipment for the session

Appointments with a service that uses a device show, below the appointment details, a section with the fields to fill in, grouped by service and device: each group says which service it is for («Armpit laser · Diode laser»). If the appointment has two services with the same device, their fields appear twice, once per service, so it is clear how many joules went into each area. Next to each field, how that customer was treated the previous time in that same service. It is saved with its own button, «Save what was noted», without having to save the whole appointment; a field left blank means «not noted» and is not stored.

If the appointment has no service with a device, the section does not appear.

It is also asked for on the way to charging. When you press Charge in the appointment, the till shows the same session data above the ticket. If a required one is missing, it does not let you charge until it is written down and saved (whoever lacks permission to write it down is warned, but can charge).

Where you see it afterwards. In the customer's record, the Sessions with equipment lists each session, newest first, with the day and time, the service, the device, the parameters with their unit and who did it. It is where a laser treatment is followed session by session. If nothing was ever noted for the customer, the card does not appear.

That is the figure you really look at before starting: in laser hair removal the settings go up session by session, and going too far burns. It is looked up by customer, not by appointment, so it does not matter if last time was in another room, with another person and three weeks ago.

7. The booking portal

This is the screen where the customer books by themselves, at /reservas on the centre's address. It is switched off until the centre switches it on, and it is switched on by delegation.

How the customer logs in

There is no password. The customer types in their email address and receives a six-digit code that is good for fifteen minutes; the same message carries a link that fills it in by itself, so there is nothing to copy. That gets them in.

They are never told whether that address is or is not on anyone's record: the answer is the same in both cases. If it is not, the message simply does not arrive. That way nobody can work out who is a customer of the centre by trying addresses.

With no email gateway set up, the portal does not work. The code cannot leave the server and the customer is left waiting. It is the first thing to get set up before switching the portal on for anyone.

What they see and what they do not

  • Only the services and the rooms marked as bookable online.
  • A service that is not given in a room is not offered to them there. At the front desk that is a warning; on the portal it is a wall, because the customer knows less than the program and a booking made wrongly is a phone call to undo it.
  • Closed stretches do not appear.

How far ahead bookings go, and with which rules

Administration → Preferences → Agenda and bookings

It is set per delegation, the one of the session, and applies to the portal and to integrations that book, such as a campaign platform:

  • Days ahead. How far ahead the agenda is open: with 60, today you can book up to 60 days from now. The window moves forward on its own every day. If you leave it as it is, the agenda opens 30 days ahead.
  • Minimum notice. With 2 hours, at ten the first slot offered is at twelve.
  • Cancellation window, open bookings per client, what happens without a voucher and when the portal closes to clients who owe. These four apply only to the portal.

The portal checkbox only opens or closes it to clients. Integrations that book keep working with the days ahead and the notice. None of these rules apply at the front desk.

Balancing and closing the day's cash

At the end of each day, what is in the till and what has come in through the card terminal is balanced against what Turnera says has been charged. The cancellations report

It counts against the appointments in the range, not the ones cancelled in the range. The difference matters: a March appointment cancelled in April is a March appointment that fell through.

At the top, how many were cancelled and who called them off: the customer, the centre or an external cause. With the Cause filter, the report leaves the ranking and lists one by one the appointments cancelled for that cause: the day, the customer, the professional, when it was created, when it was cancelled and the comments. It is how you review what was marked as a mistake.

Below, which customers cancel the most. Only what the customer calls off counts here: if a class is called off because of rain, it does not push them up the list. It is sorted by number of cancellations and, when tied, by proportion: someone who cancelled three out of three is more of a problem than someone who cancelled three out of forty.

A split payment in the end-of-day till

If a visit was charged with two payment methods, the end-of-day till splits the amount between the two. The fifty in cash goes to cash and the fifty on card to card: the till matches the cash counted.

A refund in the cash closing

A refund counts on the day it was made, in negative and in its payment method: if 12.50 € were refunded in cash today, today's cash is 12.50 less. Those refunded as store credit show up in the payment method Store credit, in negative; whatever is later charged with that credit shows up there in positive. The same happens with gift cards: their sale goes to the payment method it was paid with, and what is spent with them shows up in the row Gift card, which is not money in the till. And in the best-selling services, one that was sold and refunded does not count as sold.

Roles and permissions

Administration → Roles

A role is a job: reception, professional, management. Each role carries inside it the permissions that the job needs, and a person is given roles, not loose permissions. That way, the day somebody new starts, nobody has to remember twenty tick boxes: you give them their role and that is that.

The centre comes with four roles already made, which can be changed and deleted like any other:

  • Reception: gives out times, minds the counter and charges.
  • Professional: their agenda, their customers and the clinical record.
  • Management: runs the centre — catalogue, staff, reports and settings — and creates users.
  • System administrator: everything, delicate permissions included.

To make one to measure, press New role, give it a name that does not exist yet and share out the permissions with the two lists: on the left the available ones, on the right the ones of the role. They move from one side to the other with the arrows or a double click, and above each list there is a search box, so you do not have to run through all seventy. Hovering over a permission explains what it unlocks, which is not always a whole screen: sometimes it is just a button, or whether that button can be pressed. Seeing the services and changing their prices, for example, are two different permissions.

Delicate permissions are marked. They are the ones that take out the data of many people at once — exporting the customer list, the time records of the whole staff — or the ones that cannot be undone, such as wiping the centre. None of the factory roles carries them: give them only to whoever runs the centre. Downloading the history of one customer is not one of these: that is printed in the consulting room and handed over.

Roles are given to a person on their record, in Administration → Users → (a user), with the same two lists. A user may carry several: whoever works the counter and also looks after the stock does not need a role invented for them alone.

On screen, a permission you do not have shows in two ways: either the option does not appear in the menu, or the button is there but will not be pressed. In the catalogue, for example, whoever can see the services but not change them does not see the button to create one, and the edit and retire buttons come out greyed.

The change is noticed on logging back in. What each person may do travels inside their session, so if you change somebody's role while they are working, they will not see it until they leave and come back in.

A role that somebody carries will not let itself be deleted, so that nobody is left without permissions without noticing: first take it off those people, then delete the role.

Which IP addresses each user can sign in from

Administration → Users → (a user)

When you edit a user there is a field Allowed IP addresses: you type one, press Enter, and you can add several. With the word any the user signs in from anywhere, which is what every account starts with; leaving it empty puts any.

It does what it looks like it does: it keeps the front desk account working inside the centre and not from home. Whoever tries from somewhere else sees the same “could not sign in” as with a wrong password, and the attempt is recorded.

This needs the centre to have a fixed IP. With an address that changes, the day the provider changes it that user is locked out. Always leave one administration account on any, or there will be nowhere to fix it from.

How the application looks

Administration → Preferences → Appearance

There are five themes and one is picked for the whole centre. Four are colour ones —Turnera green, Night blue, Plum and Terracotta— and they only change the left-hand bar, the tone of the background and the lines of the agenda; they are there so the application does not clash with the identity of the centre. The fifth is Dark, and that one does turn the whole screen around.

The dark one is for the front desks and rooms that work in low light on purpose, where a white screen dazzles. In it the agenda status colours are lightened so they still read, so the legend changes tones but not meanings: what was blue is still blue, and what was almost black becomes almost white.

The green of the buttons does not change with the theme: it is the colour of an action, and it is in all five.

You see the theme as soon as you pick it in the dropdown, before saving, so you can compare them. It is not kept until you press Save: if you leave the screen without saving, the previous one comes back.

The logo of the centre stays on a white background in every theme, the dark one included. That is on purpose: a mark meant for white paper would disappear on the bar.

The VAT of each thing that is sold

Administration → Catalogue → Services / Products

On the form of each service and each product, under the price, there is a VAT dropdown with five options: 21 %, 10 %, 4 %, Exempt and Undecided.

It is decided here, when the item is created. That is on purpose: whoever is taking the money with the customer in front of them has no reason to know whether a session is exempt, and asking at that moment is the quickest way to end up with books that do not add up. The catalogue carries it and the ticket inherits it.

Exempt is not «at 0 %». A session with a therapeutic purpose given by a healthcare professional is exempt under article 20 of the VAT Act, and that is a different fact from a rate of zero per cent: the invoice has to say why. That is why there is a separate Exempt option and not a zero in the list of rates.

Undecided is not a fault, it is what the first day looks like. Leave it there for whatever you are not sure about and go through it with your accountant; the draft accounting summary will tell you exactly what is missing.

The draft accounting summary

Reports → Draft accounting summary

A draft to review with your accountant. It does not replace your books. No invoice is issued or numbered: this screen only reads the tickets that already exist and adds them up. Each ticket line keeps the VAT the catalogue had on the day of the sale, so changing a rate does not change what has already been sold. Whatever was sold while the service or product was undecided takes the rate you give it later.

It is the summary entry your accountant asks for: one entry per day and rate, with the base, the tax and the range of tickets of each, instead of four hundred loose lines. It downloads to Excel.

At the top it says how much money there is with no VAT set. Those lines add no base and no tax on purpose: making them up would be the very opposite of what this screen is for. They are the list of what to go through in the catalogue.

The screen comes switched off. It is switched on under Administration → Preferences → Payments and loyalty, and it is worth doing once the VAT of the catalogue has been gone through: before that almost everything comes out «not set».

WhatsApp reminders

Appointment record → WhatsApp · Administration → Preferences → Messages to customers

Turnera does not send anything on its own. When you open an appointment there is a WhatsApp button that opens the conversation with the customer, in the WhatsApp of whoever is attending, with the notice already written. You read it, change it if need be and send it. It is what the centre already did — pick up the phone, open WhatsApp and type — without having to copy the number or key in the sentence.

The button carries the customer's phone number inside, so it only works for whoever has the permission to see phone numbers and emails. Everyone else sees it switched off.

There is no automatic reminder. Nobody sends a message without having read it first, and in exchange there is no sending N hours ahead, no log of what was sent and no delivery confirmation. If your centre needs that, talk to us.

The country code

The text of the reminder

Below you will see how the customer will read it. A gap that does not exist is left written as it is so you notice it when reviewing, instead of vanishing and leaving an odd message.

The gaps can be written with their name in that language -- {Vorname} and {Datum} in German, {nome} and {data} in Portuguese -- and the date is written the way it is read there: in German, with dots (22.09.2026). The preview for each language appears once saved.

Payment links with Stripe

Administration → Preferences → Payments and loyalty

To send a customer a link they can pay from their phone, the centre connects its own Stripe account. The money goes straight from your customer to your account: Turnera takes no fee, never stands in between and does not appear on the receipt. Whatever Stripe charges per payment is between you and Stripe.

Connecting it, step by step

  1. Sign in to dashboard.stripe.com with the centre's account. If you do not have one yet, create it there: Stripe will ask for the business details and the bank account where you want to receive the money.
  2. Go to Developers → API keys and copy the secret key, the one starting with sk_live_. The publishable one will not do (pk_).
  3. In Turnera, open Preferences → Payments and loyalty, paste the key into the card Payment links with Stripe and press Connect.

That is all. Turnera checks with Stripe that the key is valid and registers in your account the payment notice: it is what tells Turnera that a customer has paid. You will see it in Stripe, under Developers → Webhooks, named Turnera: pagos por enlace; do not delete it, or payments will stop being marked automatically.

The key is stored encrypted and never shown again: the card only shows which account it is connected to, the last four characters of the key and since when. If you want to try it before charging for real, connect the test key (sk_test_, in Stripe's test mode): the card will remind you that those payments are not real until you replace it with the live one.

Charging with the link

With Stripe connected, the point of sale offers the payment method Payment link (without Stripe it does not appear anywhere). You choose it like any other and the button changes to Close and send link. The sale is closed as a debt, because the customer has not paid yet, and the window that follows brings the link:

  • if the customer has an email address on their record, it has already been sent, and the window says where to;
  • Copy puts it on the clipboard, to paste it wherever you like;
  • Send by WhatsApp opens the WhatsApp of whoever is at the desk with the message already written, in the customer's conversation if they have a phone number.

You can also send it for a debt that already existed: in Unpaid visits, the button Send link on its row. Sending it again sends the same link, not a new one.

The customer opens the link, sees the centre's name and the amount, presses Pay and pays by card on the Stripe page. When done, they come back to a page of the centre confirming the payment. At that moment the debt settles itself, with the payment method Payment link, which is how it later appears in the reports. What marks the debt as paid is the notice Stripe sends, not the customer coming back to the page: nobody can mark it as paid by typing an address by hand.

The link does not expire and always charges whatever is still pending. If in the meantime the bill is paid at the counter, the link says it is already paid and does not let anyone pay again.

If they still pay twice. It can happen if someone charges it at the counter just while the customer is paying in Stripe. Turnera does not touch the till: the bill stays as it was charged at the counter, and the centre's contact email receives a notice with the bill, the amount and the Stripe payment reference. Refund it from your Stripe dashboard, under Payments.

Changing it or disconnecting

Change the key connects the new one and removes the payment notice of the old one. Disconnect forgets the key and deletes the notice from your Stripe account; links you had already sent will no longer be marked as paid. Visible only to people with the permission Connect external programs.

Would you rather not give Turnera the full secret key? Create a restricted key (rk_live_) in Stripe with write permission only on Checkout Sessions and Webhook Endpoints, and paste that one. It works the same.

Informed consent

Customer record → Signed paperwork · Administration → Consents

The customer signs on the tablet on the counter. Nothing is sent to them by e-mail or by WhatsApp, and they do not sign on their own phone: small screens, different browsers and older customers.

How it gets signed

  1. The tablet is always open on Signatures, with its own reception session. It asks by itself every few seconds whether there is anything to sign.
  2. On the customer record, reception presses Ask for signature and chooses the document.
  3. The document appears on the tablet by itself, with the name and the tax number already filled in. The customer reads it and signs with a finger.
  4. The PDF, the stroke, the time, the version of the text and a fingerprint of the document are stored.

Consent forms in other languages

Whoever signs has to understand what they sign. When creating or changing a template, below the main text there is Text in other languages: one optional section per language, with its name and its text. The gaps are written the same way in all of them, with their Spanish names: {{cliente}}, {{nif}}, {{centro}}, {{fecha}} and {{tratamiento}}.

  • If the customer's record has a language the template is written in, the iPad shows that text, and the buttons and the notice below are in their language too. The signed PDF comes out the same.
  • If not, they sign the main text, in the centre's language.
  • Each signature keeps the language it was signed in and the exact text that was read.

The request expires after fifteen minutes and works once only. That is deliberate: a request left lying on the counter is a signature waiting for the wrong person. If it expires, ask for it again from the record.

The templates

They are written in Administration → Consents. In the text you can leave placeholders that fill themselves in: cliente, nif, centro, fecha and tratamiento, written between double braces.

A template is never modified. Saving changes creates the next version and retires the previous one. What has already been signed has to keep existing exactly as it was read: it is half of what defends this signature the day someone disputes what they were told.

If you mark a template as required, the record of every customer who has not signed it says so. Without that warning this would be a folder of papers: the point is finding out before doing the treatment, not afterwards.

Bringing in those already signed on another platform

Consents signed before using Turnera, in another program or on scanned paper, are uploaded as PDF to the customer record with Import signed. You can choose up to 5 at once, and for each one set the name, the document date and the template it matches. If the template is given, it counts as signed and the missing-consent warning stops appearing. In the list they are marked as Imported, with the file and the day they were uploaded; they are downloaded and withdrawn like those signed here. Each PDF can be up to 20 MB. Importing is the permission «Import signed consents», which Reception and Management have.

Who can see what has been signed

Reception can ask for the signature and accompany it, but downloading a signed consent needs the clinical history permission and is written down in the access trail, just like opening a case. A signed consent is health data. That way the tablet on the counter can stay open all day with its session without that session opening anybody's documents.

Withdrawing a signature

It happens: the wrong document gets signed, or another customer's, or the patient withdraws their consent, which is a right of theirs and nobody's mistake. On the line of the document there is a Withdraw button that asks for it with a compulsory reason.

Withdrawing does not delete. The document stays where it was, with its date and its fingerprint, and beside it goes who withdrew it, when and why. A signed consent is the proof of a conversation that took place, and deleting it leaves the centre without it the day somebody argues about what they were told. What does change is that it stops counting: if the template was compulsory, the record warns again that it is missing.

It needs the WITHDRAW CONSENTS, which is its own and not the attentions one: whoever asks for a signature at the counter does not have to be able to undo it. It is granted in Administration → Users, normally only to management.

It is a simple electronic signature. What holds it up is not the stroke: it is the versioning of the text, the time and the fingerprint of the document, which is what lets you prove that the PDF being shown is the same one that was signed.

Working time (clocking in)

Menu → Clock in · Menu → Staff → Working time

The law requires this. Article 34.9 of the Spanish Workers' Statute has required since 2019 that a daily working time register is kept, held for four years and made available to the labour inspectorate. Not keeping it is a fine.

In Clock, in the menu, there are all the professionals of the centre, each with their button Clock. The list only shows names: who is in and since when is part of the working time record, and anyone walking past reception sees the screen. Each person sees that after typing their PIN, together with what they have to do: In, or Out and Break if they are already in. There is no choosing between in and out: the application knows from that person's last clocking.

Anyone can open the screen, and on purpose. A Turnera user is not a person: at the front desk the whole shift usually shares one, and the working-time record belongs to each practitioner. That is why here you press the name instead of looking at who signed in to the application, and why it is the only place where people clock in: always as an employee and with their PIN, never as a user.

The PIN

Every professional needs a four-digit PIN, set in Professionals → Clocking PIN. Tapping their button brings up a keypad and they type it: the fourth digit opens their own screen, without pressing anything else. That is what makes pressing someone else's button not clocking for someone else, and without it the list of names would prove little to an inspection.

The Generate suggests a random one, and it is a better idea than thinking one up: it leaves out the ones that are guessed at a glance —four identical digits, ladders like 1234— which are exactly the ones that come out when you choose by hand. The PIN is visible while you type it, so you can read it out to whoever will use it; write it down before saving, because afterwards it cannot be read again.

Without a PIN there is no clocking in. Whoever does not have one sees their button disabled, with a notice that administration sets it on their record. That way the register always says who clocked in and not only from which counter, which is what the law expects of a working time register. When you start using the clock in a centre, hand out the PINs first.

The PIN is stored encrypted and cannot be read again, not even from administration: if somebody forgets it, they are given a new one. It is cleared with Clear the PIN, and that person cannot clock in until they have another one.

Everyone changes their own from the clocking screen itself: when the keypad comes up, at the bottom there is Change my PIN, which asks for the one in force and the new one twice. You have to know the one that is set, and whoever knows it could already clock in for that person, so it opens no door; what it saves is having the change depend on administration being around that morning.

The record keeps what it always kept and one thing more: the working day belongs to the professional who typed their PIN, and next to it stands which user it was recorded from. They are two different facts on purpose, and together they are what you show when somebody asks.

What each person sees after their PIN

The right PIN opens a window with that person's own information, which goes back to the list by itself after a minute if nothing is touched, and as soon as they clock:

  • Whether they are in or out, and since what time, or whether they are on a break.
  • In, or Out and Break if they are in. A break is time that is not working time (lunch, for instance): it is clocked with Break when leaving and with Back from the break when coming back. It adds no hours and shows in the record between the two stretches of the day.
  • The hours HR has recorded for them for a forgotten clocking and that they have not said anything about yet. With I agree or I do not agree, explaining why in this case, their answer is recorded next to those hours, and it cannot be changed afterwards (see Incidents, further down).
  • My records: their month, day by day, with the breaks, the hours and the total, and the previous months. They can email it to themselves as a PDF or download it.

In Staff → Working time, in the menu, is everything else: choose a range and, if you like, a person. At the top come the totals of each person and month: days recorded, planned hours, worked, ordinary and overtime according to their work calendar, the overtime paid or given back as rest that month, and how many hours declared by HR are awaiting their agreement or have their disagreement. Below, each day with its stretches, its breaks and its hours, and at the bottom the clockings one by one.

What is shown to the labour inspectorate is downloaded from here:

  • Monthly summary (PDF): one sheet per person and month with each day, the total, their working time arrangement and room for the signature of the company and of the person. It is the summary handed over with the payslip.
  • Everything (Excel): a workbook with the company, its tax ID and the centre, the record day by day, the month totals and the raw clockings.
  • Days (CSV), Totals (CSV) and Clock-ins (CSV): the same in files that open with a double click in Excel. The clockings one brings each one as it is stored, with who recorded it and which corrections it has, which is what proves the record has not been rewritten.

The PDF can be downloaded by whoever sees the time and attendance; the rest, by whoever has the permission to export the working time record. They can always be downloaded: the law requires keeping them available to the labour inspectorate.

Access for the labour inspectorate and the worker representatives

At the end of Time and attendance, whoever has the permission “Give access to the working time record” creates read-only links to give to the labour inspectorate or to the worker representatives, without creating a user for them. You choose who it is for, write exactly who (for instance, the inspector's name and the number of the request) and how many days it is valid, up to 90.

Whoever opens it sees the record of any period of the last four years, a year at most each time, and can download the same files. The representatives see it without the tax ID, for data protection. The link is only shown when it is created: copy it then and send it. It can be revoked before it expires, and every visit is logged with when, from where and what was looked at.

This is what the draft Royal Decree on working time records being prepared by the Spanish Ministry of Labour asks for: immediate and remote access for the labour inspectorate and access for the worker representatives. Until it is approved, it is enough to be able to show the record when asked; the link makes that easier.

The monthly summary by email

In each professional's record, whoever has the permission “Monthly working time summary by email” (by default, HR and Management) ticks Email them the summary of their working time every month. From then on, at the start of each month they get the PDF of the previous month. Their record needs an email. It is the copy the law requires handing over with the payslip to part-time workers, and that the draft Royal Decree extends to everyone.

A clocking is not corrected

An entry is not edited, not deleted and not corrected: it stays as it was. That is exactly what makes this record worth something, because one that could be rewritten would prove nothing. When someone forgets to clock, HR opens an incident in their work calendar that explains what happened and declares from what time to what time they actually worked (see Incidents, below). On this screen those hours appear on their day marked as Declared by HR, and in the spreadsheet with “(declarado por RRHH)”.

The days that come out marked

If somebody clocks in and leaves without clocking out, that day comes out as an open shift and with no hours. It is not given a made-up closing time on purpose: a record with hours nobody clocked is worse than an incomplete one, because the incomplete one shows and the made-up one does not. The entry stays open, and after 16 hours it is taken as forgotten: that person's next clocking is a clock-in again, not the clock-out of the day before. For that day to count, HR declares the hours in an incident.

A shift that crosses midnight is counted whole on the day it started, which is how payroll counts it, and what is worked after coming back from a break still belongs to that day. A break with no return within 16 hours is left without an end: none is made up.

The centre's clock

A clocking is not a time somebody writes, like an appointment's: it is the exact moment the button is pressed. To know which day and which stretch it belongs to, the application needs to know which time zone the centre lives in, and it comes set to Europe/Madrid. A centre outside mainland Spain changes it with the TURNERA_TZ variable when installing; otherwise the stretches would come out shifted even if the summed hours were right.

Work calendar

Menu → Staff → Work calendar

The work calendar spreads over the year the hours set by the agreement of each professional: which days they work and on which shift, their holidays, their absences and the public holidays. It is kept by whoever has the role HR (or the permissions «Ver los calendarios laborales» and «Planificar los calendarios laborales»). It is a plan: what was really worked is still what was clocked, and the screen compares one with the other.

Above the calendar you switch between Planning (what has been planned) and Actual (what has been clocked). They are two different things: someone working on a holiday is not planned, it is clocked, and those hours are not regular but overtime.

What is set up once

  • Agreement: set by the centre, it is one a year for the whole staff and all its sites: the name, the yearly working hours full time and the holiday days, calendar days (the Statute's 30) or working days (not counting weekends or public holidays). Each calendar takes the one of its year; it is not chosen person by person.
  • Shifts: «Morning 9-14», «Afternoon 15-21»… One that ends before it starts is a night shift and finishes the next day. A day takes a single shift: for someone who works morning and afternoon, create a split shift, with Add stretch (split shift) or with Start as a split shift, which sets it to 09:00-14:00 and 16:00-20:00 for you to adjust. The break is the gap between one stretch and the next, and does not count as worked.
  • Absences: sick leave, paid leave, unpaid leave… Each one says whether that day counts as worked for the agreement hours. For a sick leave only that it is a sick leave is written down, never the diagnosis.
  • Public holidays: in Settings the region of the centre is chosen, and in Public holidays the button brings the national ones and those of that region for the year.

Local public holidays are not imported. Nobody publishes the two of each town reliably, so they have to be added by hand in Public holidays → Add local holiday. The screen shows how many local ones are set: if it says zero, they are missing.

The calendar of each professional

In Calendars lists every professional of the centre with how theirs stands in the chosen year. Create takes the agreement of the year and asks for the workday (100 % full time, 50 % half time) and, if they fall within the year, the start and leaving dates: with them hours and holidays are prorated automatically, which is what a final settlement needs. Part-timers get the same holidays and half the hours.

To spread the year, choose at the top what to put —a shift, holidays, an absence or Free to remove— and you click a day, or drag from one day to another to mark all those in between. Each day has one thing only: if it has a shift it cannot also be vacation. Nothing is planned before the start date or after the end date: those days do not count towards the hours, so the screen skips them and says how many. Fill by weekdays puts it at once on, for example, Monday to Friday from January to June, skipping public holidays and leaving alone what is already set. On the right, the figures are redone with each change: agreement hours, planned hours, those still to be distributed (or those in excess), vacation days set against those owed and how many are left, and, up to yesterday, what was planned against what was clocked.

Below are the warnings of the Spanish Workers' Statute: fewer than 12 hours of rest between two working days, more than 9 hours in a day, more than 6 hours in a row without a 15-minute break, fewer than 3 days off in 14, a shift on a public holiday, more vacation than is owed, or days marked outside the start and end dates. If someone works at night (from 22:00 to 06:00) it also warns that the Statute treats it as night work, with a maximum average of 8 hours over 15 days and no more than two weeks in a row on night shift, unless voluntary. Each warning carries the link Read the article, which opens the text of the Statute so you can check it. Saving an agreement with fewer than 30 calendar days of vacation (22 working days) also shows the warning. These are warnings and do not prevent saving: some agreements allow otherwise, and what was signed is for HR to know.

Duplicate to another professional copies the calendar as it is to others in the centre (if they already had one that year, it is replaced, and the screen warns first). Download PDF puts the year on one sheet, to pin on the notice board or give to the accountant.

Delete this calendar removes it with all its days, including past ones, which changes the hours worked and the out-of-shift hours of that year. That is why it asks you to type the professional's name by hand.

Closing the year

When a year has ended and has been reviewed, in Calendars, with that year selected, Close leaves it as it is, for the labour inspection: from then on nothing of that year changes in the centre — not the calendars or their days, the incidents, the compensations, the holidays or that year's agreement. It can be viewed and printed. If a shift that year used is edited later, the closed year keeps the hours it had (Turnera keeps a retired copy of the shift), and the out-of-shift hours margin that applies is the one in force when it was closed. You confirm by typing the year and cannot be reopened. A year cannot be closed before 1 May of the following year: until 30 April rest days can still be given for its out-of-shift hours (see below).

What was done: normal hours and out-of-shift hours

In Actual each clocked day is coloured: green if it was all regular hours and orange if there was out-of-shift hours. Hovering over it shows the hours clocked. The rules:

  • Time clocked within the shift planned is regular time.
  • Time clocked outside the shift counts as out-of-shift hours only if it goes beyond the margin of the centre (15 minutes by default, changed in Settings): coming in five minutes early is not overtime. If it goes past the margin, all of it counts: whoever stays forty minutes has done forty.
  • Everything clocked on a holiday, on vacation, in an absence or on a day without a shift is out-of-shift hours.
  • A clock-in without a clock-out adds nothing until HR declares the hours in an incident.
  • A night shift, which crosses midnight, is counted whole on the day it began. On the night the clocks change it counts the hours that really went by: nine in October and seven in March for a shift from 22:00 to 06:00.

Out-of-shift hours go to the professional's overtime bank on the right. Whoever has the permission «Compensate out-of-shift hours» (by default HR and Management) deducts them with Compensate hours, as paid in payroll or as compensatory rest. The pending balance is what is left to compensate. A rest day compensates the oldest hours first.

Turnera does not decide whether some out-of-shift hours are extraordinary: that is for HR to decide according to the contract and the agreement. What it does watch is the cap the Workers' Statute sets on extraordinary hours, 80 hours a year (art. 35.2; for a part-time contract, proportional to its working hours). If the year's out-of-shift hours, not counting those returned as rest within the deadline, exceed that cap, a warning appears. The deadline is four months from when the hours were worked (art. 35.1): the rest for September's hours can be given in January. That is why the date of a compensation can fall as late as 30 April of the following year. If a rest day arrives after the deadline, it is saved anyway, marked out of time , and those hours still count towards the cap. It is a warning: Turnera does not prevent recording anything.

Incidents

With the permission «Record incidents» (by default, HR and Management) Incident appears next to the shifts. With it selected, clicking a day opens its incidents: you write what happened and can upload up to 3 photos. The incident sits on top of whatever the day has —a shift, vacation— without changing it, and the day is marked with a red «!» red. They can be corrected and deleted (except those that declare hours, below), and they show who wrote them down and when. They belong to HR only: the professional does not see them in My calendar.

A forgotten clocking is fixed with an incident. When writing it, tick Declare the actual working hours (forgotten clocking) and enter from what time to what time that person worked (if the end time is earlier than the start, it is a shift that ends the next day). The clocking is not touched: those hours are added to what was clocked that day, the day no longer shows as an open working day and normal and overtime hours are counted with that. It cannot overlap what is already clocked that day, because that already counts.

An incident that declares hours is part of the time record, so it cannot be changed or deleted, nor can photos be removed from it (they can be added). If it was wrong, open another one for the same day: the new one is the one that counts and the previous one stays visible, marked as Replaced. When it is saved, the professional receives an email with the day, the declared hours and the explanation, so they know what is on their record.

Also, the next time they type their PIN at the time clock, they are asked whether they agree with those hours. Until they answer, the incident shows as Awaiting agreement; if they say no, it shows as Disagreed with their reason, here, in the time and attendance and in everything downloaded. The hours count the same in both cases: if there is no agreement, the record reflects what the company declared and the person's disagreement, which is what the draft Royal Decree on working time records asks for.

The roster

Work calendar → Roster shows the week of the whole staff: who works each day and on which shift, who is on vacation, absent or off, and how many work each day. Today is highlighted and marks who is in right now, according to what was clocked. Whoever has no work calendar is not shown in the roster.

The hours summary

Work calendar → Hours gives, for each professional and each month between two dates, the planned, the clocked hours and, of these, the regular and the overtime, with a total per person when there are several months. Download CSV exports it for the accountant. A month without a work calendar says so, and there everything clocked shows as regular.

The year-end projection

Below each professional's calendar, a chart accumulates what is planned month by month, what has been clocked up to today and, at their current pace, how much it would add up to at year end, against the hours of the agreement. If at that pace they would reach the hours of the agreement before the year ends, it says on which day: the moment to give them vacation or to stop counting on that person for more hours. Only whoever plans sees it, not the professional.

Forgotten clock-in warnings

If, according to their calendar, someone should have come in and after the margin for out-of-shift hours they have not clocked in, or should have left and after the margin are still in, an email goes out. With the factory margin that is 15 minutes; a notice never goes out before 15, even if the margin is smaller. That way, whoever stays a few extra minutes within the margin, which is still a regular working day, does not get a false notice. In the Settings of the work calendar you decide whether to notify, and whom: the professional (at the email in their record), HR or both. That is also where you enter the HR person's email; if left empty, HR notices go to the centre's management email (Preferences). A shift gives at most one clock-in and one clock-out notice. No notices on public holidays. When the mobile app exists, the notice will reach it too.

In the diary

In the views by professional, the diary shades in grey what is outside their shift and the days of holidays, absence or public holiday. At first it only marks them and still lets you book. If in Work calendar → Settings you switch on The work calendar blocks the diary, it no longer lets you book or move an appointment outside the shift. Those without a calendar that year are not affected.

The calendar of each professional

Every professional has the right to know their schedule and holidays without needing any permission, and to see what they have clocked, their normal and overtime hours and their hours bank, but not the incidents or the projection. This is meant for the professional's app; on the web, which the office uses, there is no «My calendar» entry in the menu, because the user you sign in with does not belong to any professional.

Connecting an outside program (ERP)

Administration → Integrations

If your accountant or your ERP wants to read the centre's data directly —the catalogue, the customers, the sales— there is no need to give it a Turnera user. You register an integration, which is a credential meant for a program and not for a person.

When creating it you decide:

  • Name. How you will recognise it in the list. For instance, "the accountant's ERP".
  • Contact email. The person who maintains the integration. Used to warn them about maintenance, version changes or API incidents.
  • Delegation. The only one that program will see. It is fixed here because a program cannot choose one on a screen, the way a person does when logging in.
  • Scopes. What it can read: the catalogue, customers, sales or the free slots of the diary, whether it can book appointments and whether it can check if an email or a phone number belongs to a customer (it only answers yes or no). Give only what it needs.
  • Allowed addresses. Which IP it can connect from, one per line. It accepts a range written in CIDR, for example 203.0.113.0/24.

The secret is shown once only. On saving, the screen shows a client_id and a client_secret. Copy them and pass them to whoever programs the ERP over a secure channel: they are not stored in the clear and they cannot be looked up again. If they are lost, they are rotated.

You find out about every new integration. When an integration is saved, the centre owner receives an email with its name, contact, permissions and addresses (never the secret). If you get one you did not expect, switch that integration off and change the administration password.

Booking from outside. A campaign platform that brings you clients can book their appointments directly, with the permissions free slots and book appointments. It only sees and books the same as the portal: the services and places that can be booked online, with their notice and the days ahead. If the customer already has a record with that email, the appointment goes to their record; if not, a new one is opened. Every appointment that comes in this way carries in its comments which platform it comes from and with what reference. If the service needs a piece of equipment and it is busy at that time, the platform receives its own message, different from «slot taken», so it can offer another time.

Notifications to your system (webhooks). Instead of asking every few minutes whether there are new appointments, the integration can receive a notification at an address of its own when an appointment is created, changed or cancelled. In the list of integrations, the Webhooks button lets you add the address (always https:// and public), choose which events it wants and test that it answers. By default it only reports appointments created by that same integration; if you untick that box, it also reports those made at reception and in the portal, and with the customers permission it adds their contact details. Every notification is signed with a secret that is shown only once, like the integration's. When a webhook is added, the centre owner receives an email with the address and the events, and the address cannot be changed afterwards: to point somewhere else, create another webhook. The Latest deliveries button shows what was sent and what the other side answered.

Monitoring. Turnera counts what each integration does without ever storing what is asked. The check of whether an email or a phone number belongs to a customer allows 20 queries per minute and 2,000 per day, and if the use looks like someone going through a list of people or guessing passwords, the centre owner and the integration's contact receive an email.

What to give whoever does the programming. The API guide, with examples and the OpenAPI file, is at devel.es/turnera/integradores. With it and the credentials they have everything they need.

There is no "from anywhere". An ERP runs on a server and that server has a fixed IP. If they tell you it does not, that is a conversation worth having before handing over your customers' data.

Clinical records cannot be granted. It is not a permission that comes switched off: it does not exist. The customers scope returns the administrative record —name, contact, address— and nothing of history, cases or progress notes.

Almost everything is read-only. The only thing an external program can do is book a new appointment in a free slot. Nothing the ERP does changes a payment, a record or an existing appointment.

Changing the secret without cutting the service

The Rotate secret issues a new one and keeps the previous one alive. That way the ERP is reconfigured without a rush; once it answers with the new one, press Retire the previous one and the old one stops working. While the rotation lasts, the list marks it.

Data protection. By authorising an integration, that program's provider starts processing your customers' data on the centre's behalf. Note it in your register of processing activities and check that you have the right processor agreement with them.

Refunds reach the ERP as negative sales, each one saying which sale it undoes, so what it takes to the accounts matches the centre's takings.

Whoever programs the other side will find everything they need —how to authenticate, what is available and how to paginate— in the technical guide docs/api-integracion.md.

Disabling a centre

Administration → Delegations → Edit

When a centre closes, or stops working with Turnera for a while, there is no need to delete it: it is disabled by unticking Delegation active. Its customers, appointments and payments stay where they were, but nobody can get into it, and whoever was in it is signed out straight away.

When signing in to Turnera, if only one centre is left enabled you go straight into it; if several are, you keep choosing. Whoever only worked in the disabled centre can no longer sign in, and the sign-in screen tells them so.

The disabled centre stays in the list of delegations, marked as inactive, and is enabled again from there. Two cannot be disabled: the one you are working in —switch to another one first— and the last one left enabled.

Bringing in data from another platform

Administration → Import from another program

For a centre coming from another platform. It brings in, in one go, the customers (with their notes), the price list of services, packages and products, the packages customers have half used, gift vouchers with a balance, the diary —what lies ahead and the last year—, the stock of each product, each customer's treatment history and what they left owing. It needs the permission «Import from another program», which is sensitive and only the administrator has.

  1. In the source platform, export each screen to CSV: customers, service history, price list, diary, packages, deferred payments, gift vouchers and stock. Where each one is, the import screen itself tells you, under How to get the sheets out of the source platform. Extra sheets —daily cash, invoices, advance payments— do no harm: the report says they are not imported.
  2. Press «Choose files» and choose them all at once. Their names do not matter: each sheet is recognised by its columns.
  3. Press «Try it». Turnera runs the whole import and throws it away at the end: the report says how much of each thing would be created, how much was already there and what is left out, with grouped warnings and examples to look them up in the source. Nothing is saved.
  4. If the report looks right, «Import». The files are not stored anywhere.

The code of each service in the source arrives as its short code. There, one code can be used for several services, and in Turnera it cannot: those get what sets their name apart added —IDEE becomes IDEE20, IDEE30 and IDEE50 for the 20, 30 and 50-minute Indiba— and the trial report says how each one ends up. They can be changed later in the service record.

The vouchers that have already expired with unused sessions arrive disabled: they are kept on the customer's record, but cannot be used. The report counts them separately.

With the stocks, each product arrives with the units the source had in the centre; negatives arrive as zero. If a product is in the stock but no longer in the price list, it is created anyway, with 21 % VAT, so its units are not lost. Without that sheet, products arrive with zero stock and are set with a stocktake from Stock. If someone has already done the stocktake in Turnera, a new import does not overwrite it.

Past appointments arrive attended or cancelled, as they were in the source, and the diary does not ring them in red: they were charged there. The service history —years of sessions— arrives as the customer's visits: those on the same day are one visit, with one line per service, and they show in the history of their record. The device, its settings, the session and the comments go on each line. The sheet does not say how much was charged, so they arrive at zero and paid: they do not move the till or show up as debt. Postponed ones and no-shows are not brought in, because they did not happen. Pending payments arrive in Unpaid visits; those already paid do not, because they are in the source platform's till.

Importing twice duplicates nothing: what was already brought is recognised and counted as «already there». That makes it possible to import a newer export on the day of the switch and bring only what is missing.

It can be undone from «Imports done», and it takes away everything that import brought without touching what was already there. But if someone has already worked with what was imported —an appointment, a charge, a note— Turnera does not undo it and says what prevents it: undoing it would take that work with it.

Packages are brought in with what their name says, which in the source lists the services separated by «+»: «PAQUETE 7 LPG + PRESOTERAPIA» is seven sessions of each, and «TRIO DE ZONAS (AX+ING+BP)» one of each area. The import report shows which service it understood in each part, so it can be checked. If a part is not recognised («RFN400» is a device, not a service), that package is not brought in until it is stated in a sheet Contenido de paquetes with the columns ID Paquete, ID Servicio and Cantidad (the IDs are those of the source price list), which is uploaded with the others and overrides the name.

What is not brought: the source platform's portal passwords, vouchers with no balance, used-up packages, the old slot of moved appointments, daily tills and invoices, which are closed accounts and are looked up in the source platform. Signed consents are not in any sheet: they are brought in as PDFs from each customer's record (see how).

After importing: professionals arrive with the code they had in the source and need a name, and customers with a repeated ID are all brought in and reviewed with the record merge (a minor with their mother's ID is not the same person).

Taking the centre's data

Administration → Take your data

The data belongs to the centre, not to Turnera. From here the admin account downloads everything the centre has stored, in a single zip file and whenever it wants: to move to another program, to keep its own copy or to hand it to whoever looks after its IT.

Inside there are three things and a LEEME.txt that explains them:

  • turnera.sql, the whole database. Any IT person loads it into MySQL with one command, and with it you can come back to Turnera or move the data to another program.
  • One spreadsheet per table —customers, appointments, payments, vouchers, clinical records…—, which opens in Excel with a double click.
  • The documents: the signed consents as PDF and the logo, each in its own file.

Passwords, clock-in PINs and integration secrets are left out: outside Turnera they are useless, and in the wrong hands someone could try to crack them.

Why two keys are needed. It is the most sensitive download in the application, because it takes the data of every customer at once, clinical records included. Only the admin account can ask for it —no other, even with every permission— and on top of that it needs a link that goes to the email of the centre's owner, the one given when signing up, which cannot be changed from the application. Whoever steals the password does not have the email, and whoever reads the email does not have the password.

  1. Click Send me the link. The screen tells you which email it goes to, with part of the address hidden.
  2. Open the email in the same browser, signed in as admin, and press Download. The button shows how far it has got.
  3. Keep the zip somewhere safe: it holds the data of all your customers.

The link works only once and expires in 30 minutes; if the download is interrupted, ask for another, up to three an hour. When it finishes another email arrives saying it has been downloaded, with the time and the address it was done from: if it was not you, change the admin password and write to us. The screen also keeps the list of exports requested and downloaded.

10. The clinical record

In the centres that use it, each customer has a history and cases, and each case its progress notes. It prints to PDF.

Every time someone opens a clinical record it is logged: who, when and what they looked at. It is not surveillance, it is what the law requires for health data, and it is what you have to be able to show if anyone asks.

Seeing the clinical record is a separate permission from seeing the customer record. Whoever is at the front desk has no need for it.

Deleting or annulling an evolution

An evolution is only deleted on the same day and by whoever wrote it: it is for removing one noted on the wrong record. After that day, or if someone else wrote it, it is annulled, giving the reason. An annulled evolution does not disappear: it stays in the case, struck through, with who annulled it, when and why. It can no longer be changed nor its photos touched, and it is left out when the case is printed. That way the clinical history always says who noted what and when, which is what the law requires.

Marking where it hurts: the anatomical map

Customer record → Clinical history → New case (or Edit case)

Below the case form is the anatomical map: the whole body, front and back, in four layers -- Muscle, Bone, Skin (male) and Skin (female).

  • Moving the mouse over the body highlights what is underneath and tells you its name and side: «Descending part of trapezius muscle · right side», «Lumbar region · left side». On the skin you get the regions of the body; on the muscle layer, each muscle and the bones that show; on the bone layer, each bone.
  • A click marks it. The zone is added to the list on the right with its number, and there you note whether it hurts and how much, from 0 to 10, with the same green-to-red bar as the progress notes. On the map, each marked zone is tinted with the colour of its pain.
  • Several zones can be marked, on different layers and views. Clicking one in the list takes the map to the layer and view where it was marked.
  • The mouse wheel or the magnifier buttons zoom in up to six times; with the body enlarged, drag to move it. That way the small muscles of the hand, the foot or the face are easy to mark.
  • The side is always the patient's: in the front view, their right side is on the left of the screen.

The zones are saved with the case. When it is opened later, the map shows them already tinted with the list beside it, and the PDF report lists them with their name, side and pain, in the patient's language.

Older cases that had a point on the picture of their line of activity keep it as it was, with their usual picture. The anatomical map is for new cases and for those that had no point.

The images are generated from Z-Anatomy (CC BY-SA 4.0), BodyParts3D from the Database Center for Life Science (CC BY 4.0) and the Human Reference Atlas by HuBMAP (CC BY 4.0), and are published under the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence.

Printing a case

On the case page, PDF report produces the whole case to give to the patient or send to another specialist: the medical background, the case details and each progress note in its own block, from oldest to newest, with the date, who saw the patient, symptoms, examination, diagnosis, treatment, pain, notes and its photos. Every page carries a footer saying it is a confidential document with health data, and its number.

Printing is a separate permission, Print the clinical record, which the Professional role and the administrator have out of the box; reception does not. Whoever does not have it does not see the button. Every print is recorded in the access log.

The pain of each session

When a case is opened and in each progress note you can tick There is pain. Ticking it shows a bar from 0 to 10, from green to red: 0 is no pain and 10 the worst imaginable, the scale doctors and physiotherapists use. If it is not ticked, no pain is noted, which is not the same as a zero: a zero says it was asked and it did not hurt. On the case page each progress note carries its pain in a label of the same colour, so you can see at a glance whether it is easing, and it also appears in the PDF.

Photos in the progress notes

Each progress note takes up to three photos, to follow by eye how an injury, a scar or a posture changes. They are added with Add photo in the progress note itself; from a tablet or a phone the photo can be taken there and then. They are uploaded when you press Save, and removed with the cross on each one.

  • Turnera reduces each photo before uploading it, and strips what the camera writes inside, such as where it was taken.
  • They are health data: only those who can see the clinical record see them, and they travel with the backup and with the export of the centre's data.
  • On the case page they appear small under each progress note; click one to see it full size.

Medicines in the treatment

Above the treatment, in the case and in each progress note, there is Add a medicine to the treatment. With three letters (ibupro, enantyum) it suggests the medicines marketed in Spain that start that way, with their active ingredient, the laboratory and whether they need a prescription. Picking one adds it on a line of the treatment, ready to write the dosage after it.

It is a writing aid, not a prescription: the treatment is still free text. The list comes from CIMA, the medicines information centre of the AEMPS (the Spanish medicines agency), checked on the spot, so it is always up to date. Only what is typed of the medicine's name reaches the AEMPS, nothing about the patient. If one day it does not answer, Turnera says so and the treatment is written by hand as always.

In a training centre: following the student

A club, an academy or a gym does not keep clinical records, so Turnera does not show them. In each student's record there is their follow-up: the programmes they have open, each with its goal, the starting point, the physical assessment and the training plan, and the coach in charge.

Inside each programme you write the session notes: what was worked on and how it went, with up to three photos or screenshots if they help (a posture, a technical gesture). When the goal is met, the programme is marked as finished. There is no medical background, no body map, no pain scale and no medicines: that belongs to clinics. The permission is called See the follow-up and the programme prints to PDF the same way.

11. When something goes wrong

“It will not let me book the appointment”

If it says the slot is taken, there is another appointment in that room or with that member of staff at that time; the warning says which. If it says the device is in use, there is another appointment at that time that needs the same one, even if it is in another room.

“Which version are you on?”

At the bottom of the left-hand menu, under the active delegation. It is the first thing to look at before writing in, because a fault already fixed in a later version is solved by updating.

“A menu option is missing”

It is a permission your user account does not have. Whoever administers the centre can give it to you.

“The customer is not receiving the portal code”

Check that the address on their record is the one you are typing, that the email gateway is set up, and that it has not landed in their junk folder. The code expires after fifteen minutes: once that time is up you have to ask for another.

“The end-of-day till does not balance”

Look first at the payments split across several payment methods and the visits that were left owing. An unpaid visit is not in the till: it is in Unpaid visits.

“I cancelled an appointment by mistake”

It cannot be recovered: you have to create it again with the same details.
