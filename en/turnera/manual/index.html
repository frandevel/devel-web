The Turnera manual

How Turnera is used day to day in a centre: booking, charging, and knowing what happened. It is written for the person at the front desk, not for the person who writes the software.

1. Logging in and finding your way around

Turnera opens in the browser, with the centre's address and the username and password you were given. There is nothing to install and it works the same on the front desk computer, on a tablet or on a phone.

Each person has their own user account. This is not red tape: the application records who did each thing, and with a shared account that information is worth nothing at all. What is more, each user sees only their own part — the receptionist has no need to open the clinical record, and the physiotherapist has no need to see the till.

The menu on the left

At the top, with no heading, is what you use every day: Home, Agenda, Point of sale, Unpaid visits and Customers. Below, grouped together, is what you set up now and then (catalogue, stock, staff) and what you only look at (reports and settings).

If one of those entries is missing for you it is not a fault: your user account does not have that permission. Whoever administers the centre can give it to you.

At the top of the menu is the centre's logo, if one has been uploaded under Administration → Centre details; if not, Turnera's.

Sorting any list

Every list —customers, suppliers, services, products, stock, outstanding payments, reports…— is sorted by clicking the column header. Once, from lowest to highest or from A to Z; again, the other way round; and a third time goes back to the usual order. The arrow next to the title tells you which column the list is sorted by and in which direction.

The whole list is sorted, not just the page on screen: sort the customers by registration date and go to page two, and it carries on where the first one ended. Anything empty —a customer with no email, a product with no brand— always goes last, in both directions, which is where you look for it.

Which version am I on

Right at the bottom of the left-hand menu, under the active delegation, is the Turnera version this centre is running. It is the first thing you are asked for when something does not work, so it is worth knowing where it is before you need it. Hovering over it also says when it was built.

Home

The home screen sums up the day: the appointments still to come, what has been charged, what is still unpaid and the birthdays. Each block is a shortcut — click it and you land on the screen that explains it.

The customers block also brings the last five sign-ups, each one linking to its record. It is for the everyday case: somebody registered a customer a while ago, you need to add their phone number or give them an appointment, and looking them up by a surname that may have been typed wrong is the long way round.

2. The agenda

Menu → Agenda

This is the screen you spend the day on. You can look at it in three ways, and the button at the top left switches between them:

ViewOne column perWhat it is for
Day by member of staff Each member of staff The usual one: who is seeing whom today. It is the one you get when you log in.
Day by room Each room, court or studio When what you are sharing out is the place and not the person.
Week by member of staff Each day of the week For booking someone who asks “what have you got next week?”.

The view you choose is remembered: when you come back tomorrow it will be as you left it.

The line of the hour

A dashed red line crosses the agenda at the current time, with the time written on the left. Everything above it has already happened; the appointment the line runs through is the one that should be under way right now. It moves by itself, with no reloading.

It only shows when the agenda is on today —in the week view, only over today's column— and within the hours the grid paints. If you look at next Monday there is no line, because there is nothing to mark.

Booking, moving and cancelling appointments

  • Booking an appointment: click the free slot where you want it to go. The form opens with the time and the member of staff already filled in; all that is missing is the customer and the service.
  • Opening an appointment: click it.
  • Moving it: drag it. If you drop it in another column it also changes member of staff or room, depending on the view you are in.
  • Changing how long it lasts: drag the bottom edge.
  • Cancelling it: open it and click Cancel. You can write down the reason, and it is worth doing: a month later, in the cancellations report, that reason is the only thing that explains what happened.
  • Taking cancelled appointments off the view: tick Hide cancelled, in the legend under the title. The diary remembers the box, so next time you will find it as you left it.

What each person can do in the diary depends on their permissions. Whoever cannot book sees the whole diary, but gets no new appointment button, clicking a free slot does nothing and appointments cannot be dragged or stretched: clicking one opens it to read, without a save button. The buttons to close off time, the waiting list and printing only show up for whoever has their permission. Inside an appointment, Cancel, Charge and WhatsApp are always shown, but switched off for whoever cannot use them; hovering over them says why.

The service sets the duration. When you pick the services for the appointment, the duration adjusts itself to what they add up to, rounding up to the slot of that room: two twenty-minute services in a room that runs in fifteens take forty-five, not forty. The moment you touch the duration selector by hand, you are in charge and we stop touching it. On an appointment that already exists nothing is recalculated either: its duration is the one it has.

Cancelling cannot be undone. A cancelled appointment stays in view, crossed out — its slot is free again to book over, or it can be taken off the view with Hide cancelled —, but it cannot be recovered: it has to be created again. And marking someone as No show also cancels the appointment, so do not use it for “has not arrived yet”.

If you try to book two appointments at once in the same room or with the same member of staff, the application will not let you and tells you which one it clashes with. It is the only thing it truly blocks: the rest are warnings.

What each card tells you

The colour of the card is the status of the appointment — waiting, in progress, seen, did not come — and the legend under the title explains them. With the dark theme the same statuses are painted lighter, and «did not come», which on a light background is almost black, becomes almost white: it is still the one that falls outside the scale. On top of the colour there are four signs:

SignalWhat it means
Orange bar on the left edge, with a star next to the name That person's first visit to the centre. It is not a mark anyone has to set: it works itself out by looking at whether the person has any earlier appointment.
Voucher icon with a number, at the bottom right The customer has a voucher, and that number is the sessions they have left. Hover over it and it opens out to show which voucher each one is from and when it expires.
Green tickThe appointment is confirmed.
Euro iconThat appointment has already been through the till.

Waiting list

Agenda → Waiting list

Somebody asks for a time that is already taken. Instead of writing it on a piece of paper, it goes on that day's waiting list: who they are, what they wanted done, the stretch that would suit them and whatever is worth knowing when you call. The agenda button shows up front how many are waiting on the day you are looking at.

When a booking is cancelled, the screen says so: “two people are on the waiting list for this day”. Whoever cancels decides who to call — the list goes in order of arrival —, and once they are given a time they are taken off the list.

Turnera neither calls nor writes to anybody. It is on purpose: notifying on its own, the first in line and with a deadline to reply, is a whole feature —with its queue, its retries and its “what if two of them answer”— and deserves to be built with real use in front of it. The usual thing is a list with two names, settled with one phone call.

The phone number only shows on the list for whoever holds the permission for the contact details of the customer, just like on the customer record. And taking somebody off deletes nothing: the row keeps who took them off and when, so tomorrow you can say who was called and who was not.

Closing stretches in the agenda

Agenda → Close a stretch

A bank holiday, a spell of leave, a room being serviced or a broken device. You pick the day, the time band, what it affects — the whole centre, one room or one person — and the reason.

The closed stretch appears hatched on the grid, with the reason written over it. The reason is not compulsory but it is worth putting in: in two weeks' time nobody will remember why that Thursday is closed, and it ends in a phone call.

Closing is not forbidding. On the booking portal the closed slot disappears and the customer does not see it. At the front desk, on the other hand, you get a warning and can carry on: if the room frees up sooner than expected, you know it and the program does not.

Printing the agenda

Agenda → Print

It prints the day as a PDF, one member of staff per sheet, with the time, the customer, their phone number, the room, the service and the notes. First visits are marked on the paper too.

Click the button and you get the whole centre. Open the little arrow and you pick a single person. This is what you print in the morning so that everyone has their day beside the couch and so that, if the power goes, the centre keeps running.

3. Customers

Menu → Customers

The search box at the top searches by first name, surname, phone number or tax ID. There is no need to type it in full. The list is sorted by clicking the header of any column (how).

A customer record holds their details, their visit history, their vouchers and their notes. The phone number and the email address are links: tapping them opens the dialler or the mail programme, with nothing to copy and paste. It happens throughout the application — staff, suppliers and delegations too — so there is never a number to copy out by hand.

Going to their next appointment

The record has a upcoming appointments with the ones that customer has ahead, each with its own See in the agendabutton. Clicking it opens the agenda of the right day and the appointment pulses by itself for three seconds, with the screen scrolled to it.

After those three seconds the pulse fades and the agenda is the one you always use. That is on purpose: whoever gets there has come to move the appointment, to charge it or to look at the one next to it, and an agenda you have to «unlock» with a click before you can touch it gets in the way exactly when you were about to work.

Club points

If the centre switches it on in Administration → Preferences, every purchase gives the customer points, and those points can later be used as a payment method. Two figures are set: how many points each euro spent gives and how many points each euro costs when redeeming them. With one and one, a euro is a point.

Neither paying with a voucher nor paying with points gives points, for the obvious reason: with the first the money came in the day the voucher was bought, and with the second no money comes in.

Where the balance comes from

A member's record carries their balance and, underneath, the history of movements: every line says what happened, how many points changed and what they were left with. A purchase, a redemption, a cancelled sale, an adjustment by hand or two records merged.

This is what you show when a customer argues about their balance. The balance used to be a loose number going up and down with no trace; while only the till touched it you could live with that, but once management can give points away, every one of them has to be explainable.

Paying with points

At the point of sale, points are one more way to pay, with the centre's own name behind them — Aurora points —, and they are only offered if the customer is a member and has enough balance for what is being charged. Whoever does not have enough is not shown them, so nobody has to try at the counter to find out.

Adding or taking away points by hand

With Adjust points they are given or taken away, positive or negative, and the reason is compulsory: it is stored as written and shown in the history. It needs the administratorpermission, because giving points away is giving money away and is not a counter task.

The balance cannot go below zero: if you try to take away more than they have, Turnera says so and does nothing.

Alerts

An alert is what to keep in mind when booking or charging that customer: «allergic to nickel, no Indiba». It is written in their record, under Alerts, and from then on it shows up by itself right at the top when you open one of their appointments and when you charge them at the till.

An alert is not a note. A note is a diary: it is read when somebody opens it, which is exactly what does not happen with the phone in your hand giving an appointment. An alert is short, it is live or retired, and it puts itself in front of you without anybody looking for it. That is why they are two different things in two different places.

It shows as a red banner, not as a message you close. That is on purpose: what is dismissed with a click is dismissed without reading it, and a real alert has to stay there while the treatment is being chosen, which is the moment it matters.

It is written and retired by whoever can edit the record; it is also seen by whoever only has the agenda, because otherwise the alert never reaches the person booking and is good for nothing. Withdraw does not delete: the alert stops showing up and who retired it and when stays on record.

No diagnoses here. The whole counter sees it. An alert carries what is needed to give an appointment or to charge; the clinical part goes to the history, which has its own permission and leaves a trace of who opens it.

Duplicate records

It happens in every centre: the same person added twice, with the surname misspelt one of the times. Turnera lets you merge the two records, and in doing so everything from the other one is carried over to the good one: appointments, visits, vouchers and clinical record. Nothing is lost.

A merge cannot be undone. Before confirming it, check on the screen that the record being kept is the right one.

4. Charging

The point of sale

Menu → Point of sale

You pick the customer and the member of staff, and from there you add services and products to the ticket. The discount is a percentage of the total, and the payment method comes from the list of the ones the centre has active.

The short way is from the agenda. Open the appointment and click Charge: the point of sale opens with the customer, the member of staff and that appointment's services already filled in. All you have to say is how they pay. And once charged, the appointment is marked as paid — that is the euro icon on the grid.

  • Paid now ticked: the money goes into today's till.
  • Paid now unticked: the visit is left as a debt and shows up in Unpaid visits.
  • Tendered: type in what you are handed and the screen works out the change.

The Quote button prints as a PDF what is on the ticket without charging anything, to hand to someone who is still thinking about it.

Packages

Menu → Catalogue → Packages

A package is a closed price for several things together: “aloe vera moisturiser + leg waxing, 50 €”. It can carry services, products or both, and each line with as many units as it needs.

The price of the package is optional. Left blank, the package is worth whatever its parts add up to, and goes up by itself the day a service goes up. Filled in, the package is an offer: the difference is split across its lines in proportion to what each one costs.

Why it is split and not left on one line. The package goes onto the ticket as the lines it carries inside, not as a single one. That way each one takes its own VAT to the accounting draft, and a voucher can pay for the waxing while the cream is charged. If the discount stayed whole on one line, the VAT and the till would come out wrong.

With the example above: a 24.20 € cream and a 30.00 € waxing, package at 50 €. The cream ends up at 22.32 € and the waxing at 27.68 €, which add up to the 50 that are charged. And if a voucher covers the waxing, what is not paid is those 27.68 €, not the 30 of the tariff.

To sell one, the point of sale has a third tab, Packages, next to services and products. It is added like everything else and appears on the ticket under its name; the voucher box behaves as it does on a single service, in case the customer would rather pay for it and keep the session.

A package is not deleted: it is taken off. It stops being offered at the point of sale, and the tickets that already carry it stay as they are.

Payment methods with a fixed discount

Some payment methods come with the discount built in. Gift is a hundred per cent: choose it and the total goes to zero by itself. There is nothing to remember, and you cannot charge by mistake for something you are giving away.

The payment methods the till offers are cash, card, bank transfer, financing, gift and gift card and, when it applies, the club points and the customer's store credit. These last two only appear when there is a customer with a balance.

Coupons

Menu → Catalogue → Coupons

A coupon is a code that takes money off the ticket: SUMMER25, WELCOME. It is handed out on a flyer or online, the customer says it at the counter and it is typed in when charging. It can take off euros — 10 € off — or a percentage — 15% of the whole ticket —, and it takes whatever limits are needed: from when it is valid, until when, how many times in total, and whether the same person may use it more than once.

When charging, the code goes in the Coupon box of the ticket, above the discount. It does not matter how it is typed: it is always stored in capitals. If the coupon does not work, the screen says why, because the cases are not the same: it does not exist, it has not started yet, its date has passed, every use is gone, or that customer already used it.

The discount is shared between the lines, it does not stay on one. Just like with packages: a 10 € coupon on a 42.50 € ticket leaves the 30 € service at 22.94 and the 12.50 product at 9.56. That way each line takes its own VAT to the accounting draft and the day's till adds up.

A 10 € coupon on a 6 € ticket takes off 6: the centre does not give the difference back. And on the listing, each coupon says how many times it has been used and how much money has been given away with it, which is the answer to “what did that campaign cost us”.

A coupon is not deleted: it is taken off. It stops working at the counter and what was already taken off with it stays where it was. Seeing the coupons and creating them are two different permissions: reception sees them, management invents them.

Paying with a voucher

If the customer holds a voucher with sessions left, the till applies it by itself. Every service on the ticket that belongs to that voucher's group comes out at 0,00 € with the mark Voucher: covers 1 of 1, and the rest of the ticket (services from other groups, products) is paid today with whichever payment method you choose. The totals show the line Covered by vouchers with what the customer has saved.

If you would rather charge for a service and keep the session, untick the box on that line: it goes back to its price and the voucher is left alone. Useful for the customer who wants to keep the sessions they have left, or the one who is paying for somebody else today.

When there are several services from the same group and the voucher does not stretch to all of them, it covers the most expensive ones first. And if the visit is left as a debt, the sessions are not spent until it is paid: the voucher stays whole on the customer's record in the meantime.

On the receipt every line paid with a voucher comes out as (voucher session), and the day's takings only count the money that actually came in. “Voucher” is no longer a payment method you pick from the list.

Paying by two methods at once

The bill is a hundred euros, the customer has fifty in cash and wants to put the rest on a card. Tick “Split the payment across several payment methods” and two lines appear: on each one you pick the method and how much goes on it. You can add more.

Below you can see the sum of the split next to the total. Until it balances to the cent, shown in red, the charge button does not light up. This is deliberate: a split that does not add up is a discrepancy in the till that turns up weeks later and that by then nobody remembers.

The ticket comes out with the breakdown: how much went on each one. And the end-of-day till splits that payment between the two methods, it does not put it all into one.

Unpaid visits

Menu → Unpaid visits

Here are the visits that were seen without being paid for. Click one and the payment dialog opens: you pick the payment method, adjust the discount if need be and settle it. Afterwards you can print the ticket.

Refunding a ticket

Customer record → History → Refund

The customer comes back with the cream unopened, or a service was charged that in the end was not done. In the history of their record, every paid ticket has the button Refund. The ticket opens with its lines and you tick the ones being refunded: there is no need to refund all of it. Whatever was already refunded shows as Refunded and cannot be ticked again.

Then you choose how it is refunded:

  • As money, the way it came in: what was paid in cash leaves the drawer and what was paid by card goes back to the card.
  • As store credit: the money stays in the house and the customer spends it another day. The next time they are charged, Store credit shows up as one more payment method.

If the ticket was paid split between two payment methods, the refund goes back split the same way, in the same proportion: from a ticket paid almost entirely by card, almost everything goes back to the card and only what came in as cash leaves the drawer.

What was paid with points always goes back as points, and what was paid with store credit goes back to the credit: refunding them does not turn them into money. You can write the reason, and it is worth doing.

Refunding undoes everything the sale moved. The product goes back to stock (unless it comes back opened or broken: when the line is ticked, the box Back to stockappears, and if it is unticked the money is refunded but the product is not restocked), the session a voucher paid goes back to that voucher, and the customer loses the points the purchase gave them (if they already spent them, they lose whatever they have left, never going negative). If what is refunded is a voucher that was sold, the voucher disappears from their record, but only if it is untouched: with one that has sessions used it is not known which part to refund, and the application does not allow it.

The original ticket is not touched. The refund is a new ticket, with the lines in negative, marked in the history as Refund of the ticket it undoes; in the original, the refunded lines carry the mark refunded, and once none are left to refund it no longer offers the button. That way the day's takings match the drawer: what left today is taken off today, not on the day of the sale. And a coupon used in the sale is not recovered.

The ticket of a refund prints like any other, from the history, and says at the top Refund of ticket and the number of the one it undoes. A product that did not go back to stock has it noted on its line.

If the customer has store credit, their record warns about it at the top, and the till says how much is left when choosing the payment method. If the credit does not cover the whole bill, split the charge: one part with the credit and the rest with another payment method. Refunding is a separate permission, Refunding a ticket, which out of the box only the management role has.

Gift cards

Menu → Gift cards

Someone pays today and another person spends it another day. It is sold with Sell a gift card: who pays for it, who sells it, the amount, the payment method and, if wanted, who it is for, their email, a dedication and until when it is valid (without a date, it does not expire). Once charged, a code like RG-7K2M-Q9TXcomes out, which is printed on a card to give away or emailed with the card as a PDF.

The money comes in the day it is sold, not the day it is spent. The sale is a ticket charged like any other and counts in that day's takings. Spending it brings nothing in: in the cash closing it shows up separately, as Gift card, just like a voucher. That is why a gift card cannot be paid with points, store credit or another card.

To spend it, choose Gift card as the payment method at the till and type the code; what is left is shown below. It can be spent in several visits until it runs out, and if it does not cover the whole bill the charge is split: one part with the card and the rest another way. A debt can also be settled with it.

At the top of the list is what is still to be spent: what is left on all the cards that are still valid. It is money the centre has already charged and owes in services and products, and it is what has to be kept in mind when closing the year. Each card says whether it is valid, spent, expired or refunded.

If something paid with a gift card is refunded, the money goes back to the card. And the sale of a card can be refunded from the buyer's history while it is untouched: it then stops being valid. Seeing the cards and selling them are two permissions; out of the box reception and management have them.

5. Vouchers

A voucher is sessions paid for in advance from a group of services. They are sold in two ways:

  • On their own: in the customer record, in the vouchers section, you create one by hand giving the group, the sessions and when it expires.
  • When selling a service marked as a voucher: the voucher is created by itself when you charge.

How they are used up

A session is deducted when paying at the till, for every service on the ticket that belongs to the voucher's group and is left ticked as covered (see Paying with a voucher). Services from other groups and products on the same ticket are paid with the normal payment method. It can also be deducted by hand from the customer's record, for when someone was seen without going through the till.

Marking an appointment as seen does not use up the voucher. The voucher is deducted when you charge, not when you see the customer. If in your centre people are seen on a voucher without going through the point of sale, someone has to deduct the session by hand from the record, or the balance will stop adding up.

On the agenda, the voucher icon on each appointment says how many sessions that customer has left. Vouchers that are used up or expired are not counted.

Transferring a voucher to another customer

Customer record → Vouchers → Transfer

Someone buys a voucher of ten sessions, uses three and moves away. The voucher can be passed to another customer of the centre: press Transfer on the voucher's line, look up whoever receives it and that is it. The reason is optional.

A transfer cannot be undone. If you picked the wrong customer, you have to transfer it back the other way, and then both transfers will show. That is deliberate: the day someone asks why their voucher has fewer sessions than they bought, that trail is all there is to answer them with.

Every transfer records from whom to whom, how many sessions were left, who did it and the date and the time. It shows on both records, in the block Transferred vouchers: on the one who gave it and on the one who received it.

The sessions that were left are stored exactly as they stood that day, because the voucher keeps being used afterwards and otherwise, a year from now, nobody would know how much whoever received it used up.

What cannot be done

  • An expired voucher cannot be transferred. What is no longer good for whoever bought it is no good for whoever receives it either, and transferring it would be a quiet way of bringing it back to life. If you want to give it more time, correct the expiry date first.
  • Nor one with no sessions left. There is nothing to pass on.
  • Only between customers of the same centre.

A transfer does not move the sale. Whoever paid for the voucher paid for it: nothing is invoiced again and nothing is credited. It is an assignment between customers, not a till operation, and it does not show up in the cash close.

It needs the TRASPASAR BONOSpermission, which is not the same as the one for attendance used to sell and use up vouchers: changing the owner of a voucher already in use is not the same as charging for it, and not every receptionist has to be able to do it. It is granted in Administration → Users.

6. Equipment

Administration → Devices

A device is not a room. The ultrasound scanner is carried from one cubicle to another and the laser goes wherever it is needed, but there is only one and only one person can use it at a time. That is what this screen sorts out.

Adding a device

For each device you state:

  • The name and how many units there are. With two, two appointments at once are fine and three are not.
  • Which services need it. As long as none is ticked, the device gets in nobody's way.
  • What has to be recorded each time it is used: the joules on the laser, the power on the sunbed. You add a field with “Add a field” and remove it with the bin on its row.

Each field that is recorded is one of three types:

TypeWhat it asks forWhen to use it
NumberA figure, with its unit and, if you like, a minimum and a maximum Joules, watts, degrees. This is the case for almost every device
TextWhatever you typeA handpiece, a short note
List of optionsOne of the options you type in, separated by commas Areas, programmes, named intensities

Marking a field as compulsory means the appointment will not let you save the session without it. The limits on the number are checked as you record too: if the laser runs from 1 to 40 joules, a 60 will not go in.

A device that has already been used is not deleted: it is switched off. When you hit delete, if it has sessions recorded, Turnera deactivates it instead of deleting it, stops checking it when booking and keeps the settings each customer was treated with. For the same reason, a field with recorded sessions cannot be removed from the list: on saving a warning appears and the device stays as it was. Switching the device off is also the way to carry on booking while it is away being repaired.

What changes on the agenda

If two appointments at the same time need the same piece of equipment and there are not units for both, Turnera warns before saving the second one, even if they are in different rooms and with different professionals. The warning says which piece of equipment it is and which other appointment has it taken: the time, the customer and the professional. With that in front of you, you decide: change the time, or save it anyway.

Equipment warns; the room and the professional still block. The difference is real: two appointments do not fit in the same room and one person is not in two places at once, but the laser is carried from one room to another and whoever is treating knows whether there is time. Turnera cannot know that, so it says so and lets you decide.

Recording the session

Agenda → (an appointment) → Equipment for the session

On appointments that include a service with a device, below the appointment details there is a section with the fields to fill in, grouped by device. Next to each one, the settings that customer was treated with last time. It is saved with its own button, “Save what is recorded”, without having to save the whole appointment; a field left blank is taken to mean “not recorded” and nothing is written.

If the appointment has no service with a device, the section does not appear.

That is the figure you really look at before starting: in laser hair removal the settings go up session by session, and going too far burns. It is looked up by customer, not by appointment, so it does not matter if last time was in another room, with another person and three weeks ago.

7. The booking portal

This is the screen where the customer books by themselves, at /reservas on the centre's address. It is switched off until the centre switches it on, and it is switched on by delegation.

How the customer logs in

There is no password. The customer types in their email address and receives a six-digit code that is good for fifteen minutes; the same message carries a link that fills it in by itself, so there is nothing to copy. That gets them in.

They are never told whether that address is or is not on anyone's record: the answer is the same in both cases. If it is not, the message simply does not arrive. That way nobody can work out who is a customer of the centre by trying addresses.

With no email gateway set up, the portal does not work. The code cannot leave the server and the customer is left waiting. It is the first thing to get set up before switching the portal on for anyone.

What they see and what they do not

  • Only the services and the rooms marked as bookable online.
  • A service that is not given in a room is not offered to them there. At the front desk that is a warning; on the portal it is a wall, because the customer knows less than the program and a booking made wrongly is a phone call to undo it.
  • Closed stretches do not appear.

How far ahead bookings go, and with which rules

Administration → Preferences → Bookings from outside

It is set per delegation, the one of the session, and applies to the portal and to integrations that book, such as a campaign platform:

  • Days ahead. How far ahead the agenda is open: with 60, today you can book up to 60 days from now. The window moves forward on its own every day. If you leave it as it is, the agenda opens 30 days ahead.
  • Minimum notice. With 2 hours, at ten the first slot offered is at twelve.
  • Cancellation window, open bookings per client, what happens without a voucher and when the portal closes to clients who owe. These four apply only to the portal.

The portal checkbox only opens or closes it to clients. Integrations that book keep working with the days ahead and the notice. None of these rules apply at the front desk.

The scorecard

Menu → Scorecard

Six figures that tell you at a glance whether the business is doing well and where it is heading. Each one carries a traffic light —On track, Watch, Act—, the change against the same period a year ago and a line with its last twelve months. Below, revenue month by month with last year's alongside and, for the current month, the closing forecast dotted. You can look at the whole company or at one delegation.

The cancellations report

It counts against the appointments in the range, not the ones cancelled in the range. The difference matters: a March appointment cancelled in April is a March appointment that fell through. It comes out sorted by number of cancellations and, where equal, by proportion: three out of three is worse than three out of forty.

A split payment in the end-of-day till

If a visit was charged with two payment methods, the end-of-day till splits the amount between the two. The fifty in cash goes to cash and the fifty on card to card: the till matches what is in the drawer.

A refund in the cash closing

A refund counts on the day it was made, in negative and in its payment method: if 12.50 € were refunded in cash today, today's cash is 12.50 less. Those refunded as store credit show up in the payment method Store credit, in negative; whatever is later charged with that credit shows up there in positive. The same happens with gift cards: their sale goes to the payment method it was paid with, and what is spent with them shows up in the row Gift card, which is not money in the drawer. And in the best-selling services, one that was sold and refunded does not count as sold.

9. Centre settings

Menu → Administration

What you set once and hardly ever look at again. The ones most looked at:

The agenda

  • Opening and closing time: how far the grid runs.
  • Minimum length of an appointment: how many minutes apart an appointment can start. It is the centre's step.
  • Days to show: how many the week view draws.

Each room's step

Administration → Rooms → (one) → Agenda step

Each place can have its own step. The laser room goes in fifteens and the indiba equipment goes in fives. Leave it empty and the centre's step is followed, which is how they all start out.

The grid is drawn with the finest step there is, because the columns share the axis and cannot be ruled two ways at once. What does belong to each place is where an appointment lands when you drop it and how long it can last.

Rooms, courts and studios

For each one you state how many people fit (a room 1, a padel court 4, a spinning studio 20), whether it is booked online, and which services are given there. With none ticked, everything is given there.

Others

  • Devices: the machines that cannot be shared and what is recorded for each session. It is explained under Equipment.
  • Catalogue: services, products, voucher groups and providers. Each service and each product also carries its VAT, which is decided here and not at the counter.
  • Staff: who sees customers, their colour on the agenda and their column.
  • Roles and users: who gets in, what they may do and from which IP addresses.
  • Centre details: name, tax ID, address and logo. The logo heads the PDFs —tickets, quotes, agenda— and replaces Turnera's at the top of the menu, on a white background so that it reads whatever colour it is. Until one is uploaded, Turnera's appears there.
  • Email: the gateway the notifications and the portal codes go out through.

Roles and permissions

Administration → Roles

A role is a job: reception, professional, management. Each role carries inside it the permissions that the job needs, and a person is given roles, not loose permissions. That way, the day somebody new starts, nobody has to remember twenty tick boxes: you give them their role and that is that.

The centre comes with four roles already made, which can be changed and deleted like any other:

  • Reception: gives out times, minds the counter and charges.
  • Professional: their agenda, their customers and the clinical record.
  • Management: runs the centre — catalogue, staff, reports and settings — and creates users.
  • System administrator: everything, delicate permissions included.

To make one to measure, press New role, give it a name that does not exist yet and share out the permissions with the two lists: on the left the available ones, on the right the ones of the role. They move from one side to the other with the arrows or a double click, and above each list there is a search box, so you do not have to run through all seventy. Hovering over a permission explains what it unlocks, which is not always a whole screen: sometimes it is just a button, or whether that button can be pressed. Seeing the services and changing their prices, for example, are two different permissions.

Delicate permissions are marked. They are the ones that take out the data of many people at once — exporting the customer list, the time records of the whole staff — or the ones that cannot be undone, such as wiping the centre. None of the factory roles carries them: give them only to whoever runs the centre. Downloading the history of one customer is not one of these: that is printed in the consulting room and handed over.

Roles are given to a person on their record, in Administration → Users → (a user), with the same two lists. A user may carry several: whoever works the counter and also looks after the stock does not need a role invented for them alone.

On screen, a permission you do not have shows in two ways: either the option does not appear in the menu, or the button is there but will not be pressed. In the catalogue, for example, whoever can see the services but not change them does not see the button to create one, and the edit and retire buttons come out greyed.

The change is noticed on logging back in. What each person may do travels inside their session, so if you change somebody's role while they are working, they will not see it until they leave and come back in.

A role that somebody carries will not let itself be deleted, so that nobody is left without permissions without noticing: first take it off those people, then delete the role.

Which IP addresses each user can sign in from

Administration → Users → (a user)

When you edit a user there is a field Allowed IP addresses: you type one, press Enter, and you can add several. With the word any the user signs in from anywhere, which is what every account starts with; leaving it empty puts any.

It does what it looks like it does: it keeps the front desk account working inside the centre and not from home. Whoever tries from somewhere else sees the same “could not sign in” as with a wrong password, and the attempt is recorded.

This needs the centre to have a fixed IP. With an address that changes, the day the provider changes it that user is locked out. Always leave one administration account on any, or there will be nowhere to fix it from.

How the application looks

Administration → Preferences → How the application looks

There are five themes and one is picked for the whole centre. Four are colour ones —Turnera green, Night blue, Plum and Terracotta— and they only change the left-hand bar, the tone of the background and the lines of the agenda; they are there so the application does not clash with the identity of the centre. The fifth is Dark, and that one does turn the whole screen around.

The dark one is for the front desks and rooms that work in low light on purpose, where a white screen dazzles. In it the agenda status colours are lightened so they still read, so the legend changes tones but not meanings: what was blue is still blue, and what was almost black becomes almost white.

The green of the buttons does not change with the theme: it is the colour of an action, and it is in all five.

You see the theme as soon as you pick it in the dropdown, before saving, so you can compare them. It is not kept until you press Save: if you leave the screen without saving, the previous one comes back.

The logo of the centre stays on a white background in every theme, the dark one included. That is on purpose: a mark meant for white paper would disappear on the bar.

The VAT of each thing that is sold

Administration → Catalogue → Services / Products

On the form of each service and each product, under the price, there is a VAT dropdown with five options: 21 %, 10 %, 4 %, Exempt and Undecided.

It is decided here, when the item is created. That is on purpose: whoever is taking the money with the customer in front of them has no reason to know whether a session is exempt, and asking at that moment is the quickest way to end up with books that do not add up. The catalogue carries it and the ticket inherits it.

Exempt is not «at 0 %». A session with a therapeutic purpose given by a healthcare professional is exempt under article 20 of the VAT Act, and that is a different fact from a rate of zero per cent: the invoice has to say why. That is why there is a separate Exempt option and not a zero in the list of rates.

Undecided is not a fault, it is what the first day looks like. Leave it there for whatever you are not sure about and go through it with your accountant; the draft accounting summary will tell you exactly what is missing.

The draft accounting summary

Reports → Draft accounting summary

A draft to review with your accountant. It does not replace your books. No invoice is issued or numbered: this screen only reads the tickets that already exist and adds them up. The rates it uses are the ones the catalogue has today , so if you change one, the draft of past months changes with it.

It is the summary entry your accountant asks for: one entry per day and rate, with the base, the tax and the range of tickets of each, instead of four hundred loose lines. It downloads to Excel.

At the top it says how much money there is with no VAT set. Those lines add no base and no tax on purpose: making them up would be the very opposite of what this screen is for. They are the list of what to go through in the catalogue.

The screen comes switched off. It is switched on under Administration → Preferences → Draft accounting summary, and it is worth doing once the VAT of the catalogue has been gone through: before that almost everything comes out «not set».

WhatsApp reminders

Appointment card → WhatsApp · Administration → Preferences → WhatsApp notices

Turnera does not send anything on its own. When you open an appointment there is a WhatsApp button that opens the conversation with the customer, in the WhatsApp of whoever is attending, with the notice already written. You read it, change it if need be and send it. It is what the centre already did — pick up the phone, open WhatsApp and type — without having to copy the number or key in the sentence.

The button carries the customer's phone number inside, so it only works for whoever has the permission to see phone numbers and emails. Everyone else sees it switched off.

There is no automatic reminder. Nobody sends a message without having read it first, and in exchange there is no sending N hours ahead, no log of what was sent and no delivery confirmation. If your centre needs that, talk to us.

The country code

WhatsApp needs the phone number with its international code. The one you put here is prepended to customers' numbers that do not carry it; a customer whose number already starts with + is left as it is. Without a code the button cannot open the conversation.

The text of the reminder

You write it, with gaps in braces that are filled in by themselves with the appointment's details: {nombre}, {apellidos}, {servicio}, {profesional}, {fecha}, {hora}, {centro}, {telefono} and {enlace}. Below you will see how the customer will read it. A gap that does not exist is left written as it is so you notice it when reviewing, instead of vanishing and leaving an odd message.

Informed consent

Customer record → Signed paperwork · Administration → Consents

The customer signs on the tablet on the counter. Nothing is sent to them by e-mail or by WhatsApp, and they do not sign on their own phone: small screens, different browsers and older customers.

How it gets signed

  1. The tablet is always open on Signatures, with its own reception session. It asks by itself every few seconds whether there is anything to sign.
  2. On the customer record, reception presses Ask for signature and chooses the document.
  3. The document appears on the tablet by itself, with the name and the tax number already filled in. The customer reads it and signs with a finger.
  4. The PDF, the stroke, the time, the version of the text and a fingerprint of the document are stored.

The request expires after fifteen minutes and works once only. That is deliberate: a request left lying on the counter is a signature waiting for the wrong person. If it expires, ask for it again from the record.

The templates

They are written in Administration → Consents. In the text you can leave placeholders that fill themselves in: cliente, nif, centro, fecha and tratamiento, written between double braces.

A template is never modified. Saving changes creates the next version and retires the previous one. What has already been signed has to keep existing exactly as it was read: it is half of what defends this signature the day someone disputes what they were told.

If you mark a template as required, the record of every customer who has not signed it says so. Without that warning this would be a folder of papers: the point is finding out before doing the treatment, not afterwards.

Who can see what has been signed

Reception can ask for the signature and accompany it, but downloading a signed consent needs the clinical history permission and is written down in the access trail, just like opening a case. A signed consent is health data. That way the tablet on the counter can stay open all day with its session without that session opening anybody's documents.

Withdrawing a signature

It happens: the wrong document gets signed, or another customer's, or the patient withdraws their consent, which is a right of theirs and nobody's mistake. On the line of the document there is a Withdraw button that asks for it with a compulsory reason.

Withdrawing does not delete. The document stays where it was, with its date and its fingerprint, and beside it goes who withdrew it, when and why. A signed consent is the proof of a conversation that took place, and deleting it leaves the centre without it the day somebody argues about what they were told. What does change is that it stops counting: if the template was compulsory, the record warns again that it is missing.

It needs the WITHDRAW CONSENTS, which is its own and not the attentions one: whoever asks for a signature at the counter does not have to be able to undo it. It is granted in Administration → Users, normally only to management.

It is a simple electronic signature. What holds it up is not the stroke: it is the versioning of the text, the time and the fingerprint of the document, which is what lets you prove that the PDF being shown is the same one that was signed.

Working time (clocking in)

Menu → Clock in · Administration → Attendance record

The law requires this. Article 34.9 of the Spanish Workers' Statute has required since 2019 that a daily working time register is kept, held for four years and made available to the labour inspectorate. Not keeping it is a fine.

In Clock, in the menu, holds every professional of the centre, each one with a button that says In or Out depending on what is due. There is nothing to choose: the application knows from that person's last clocking.

Anyone can open the screen, and on purpose. A Turnera user is not a person: at the front desk the whole shift usually shares one, and the working-time record belongs to each practitioner. That is why here you press the name instead of looking at who signed in to the application, and why it is the only place where people clock in: always as an employee and with their PIN, never as a user.

The PIN

Every professional can have a PIN of four to six digits, set in Professionals → Clocking PIN. Pressing their button brings up a keypad to type it on. It is what makes pressing somebody else's button not the same as clocking in for them, and without it a list of names would prove little to an inspector.

The Generate suggests a random one, and it is a better idea than thinking one up: it leaves out the ones that are guessed at a glance —four identical digits, ladders like 1234— which are exactly the ones that come out when you choose by hand. The PIN is visible while you type it, so you can read it out to whoever will use it; write it down before saving, because afterwards it cannot be read again.

The PIN is optional, but do set it. Whoever has none clocks by pressing their name, and nothing else, so a centre starting out with this is not left without a record while the PINs are handed out. Once they are, the record says who clocked and not just from which desk.

The PIN is stored encrypted and cannot be read again, not even from administration: if somebody forgets it, they are given a new one. It is cleared with Clear the PIN, and that person clocks by pressing their name again.

Everyone changes their own from the clocking screen itself: when the keypad comes up, at the bottom there is Change my PIN, which asks for the one in force and the new one twice. You have to know the one that is set, and whoever knows it could already clock in for that person, so it opens no door; what it saves is having the change depend on administration being around that morning.

The record keeps what it always kept and one thing more: the working day belongs to the professional who typed their PIN, and next to it stands which user it was recorded from. They are two different facts on purpose, and together they are what you show when somebody asks.

In Administration → Working time is everything else: choose a range and, if you want, a person. At the top come the days with their shifts and the hours added up; underneath, the entries one by one. It downloads to Excel, and that is what you show the inspectorate.

Correcting an entry

An entry is never edited and never deleted. Correcting it adds another line that replaces it, with the right time, who put it there and the reason, which is required. The wrong entry stays in sight, marked as corrected.

It may look awkward and it is exactly what makes this register worth something: one that could be rewritten would prove nothing. If somebody clocked in by mistake and never came in, tick This entry never happened; the line stays, but it stops counting.

The days that come out marked

If somebody clocks in and leaves without clocking out, that day comes out as an open shift and with no hours. No closing time is invented for it, on purpose: a register with hours nobody clocked is worse than an incomplete one, because the incomplete one shows and the invented one does not. Correct it from the list of entries.

A shift that crosses midnight counts whole on the day it started, which is how whoever does the payroll counts it. And breaks are not clocked: the law does not ask for them and every collective agreement counts them differently, so inventing them here would mean counting wrong in almost all of them.

The centre's clock

A clocking is not a time somebody writes, like an appointment's: it is the exact moment the button is pressed. To know which day and which stretch it belongs to, the application needs to know which time zone the centre lives in, and it comes set to Europe/Madrid. A centre outside mainland Spain changes it with the TURNERA_TZ variable when installing; otherwise the stretches would come out shifted even if the summed hours were right.

Connecting an outside program (ERP)

Administration → Integrations

If your accountant or your ERP wants to read the centre's data directly —the catalogue, the customers, the sales— there is no need to give it a Turnera user. You register an integration, which is a credential meant for a program and not for a person.

When creating it you decide:

  • Name. How you will recognise it in the list. For instance, "the accountant's ERP".
  • Delegation. The only one that program will see. It is fixed here because a program cannot choose one on a screen, the way a person does when logging in.
  • Scopes. What it can read: the catalogue, the clients, the sales or the free slots in the diary, and whether it can book appointments. Give it only what it needs.
  • Allowed addresses. Which IP it can connect from, one per line. It accepts a range written in CIDR, for example 203.0.113.0/24.

The secret is shown once only. On saving, the screen shows a client_id and a client_secret. Copy them and pass them to whoever programs the ERP over a secure channel: they are not stored in the clear and they cannot be looked up again. If they are lost, they are rotated.

Booking from outside. A campaign platform that brings you clients can book their appointments directly, with the permissions free slots and book appointments. It only sees and books what the portal offers: the services and places booked online, with their notice and how many days ahead. If the client already has a record with that email, the appointment goes to that record; if not, a new one is opened. Every appointment that comes in this way says in its comments which platform it comes from and its reference.

What to give whoever does the programming. The API guide, with examples and the OpenAPI file, is at devel.es/turnera/api. With it and the credentials they have everything they need.

There is no "from anywhere". An ERP runs on a server and that server has a fixed IP. If they tell you it does not, that is a conversation worth having before handing over your customers' data.

Clinical records cannot be granted. It is not a permission that comes switched off: it does not exist. The customers scope returns the administrative record —name, contact, address— and nothing of history, cases or progress notes.

Read only. An outside program can read, never change. Nothing the ERP does alters an appointment, a payment or a record.

Changing the secret without cutting the service

The Rotate secret issues a new one and keeps the previous one alive. That way the ERP is reconfigured without a rush; once it answers with the new one, press Retire the previous one and the old one stops working. While the rotation lasts, the list marks it.

Data protection. By authorising an integration, that program's provider starts processing your customers' data on the centre's behalf. Note it in your register of processing activities and check that you have the right processor agreement with them.

Refunds reach the ERP as negative sales, each one saying which sale it undoes, so what it takes to the accounts matches the centre's takings.

Whoever programs the other side will find everything they need —how to authenticate, what is available and how to paginate— in the technical guide docs/api-integracion.md.

Disabling a centre

Administration → Delegations → Edit

When a centre closes, or stops working with Turnera for a while, there is no need to delete it: it is disabled by unticking Delegation active. Its customers, appointments and payments stay where they were, but nobody can get into it, and whoever was in it is signed out straight away.

When signing in to Turnera, if only one centre is left enabled you go straight into it; if several are, you keep choosing. Whoever only worked in the disabled centre can no longer sign in, and the sign-in screen tells them so.

The disabled centre stays in the list of delegations, marked as inactive, and is enabled again from there. Two cannot be disabled: the one you are working in —switch to another one first— and the last one left enabled.

Taking the centre's data

Administration → Take your data

The data belongs to the centre, not to Turnera. From here the admin account downloads everything the centre has stored, in a single zip file and whenever it wants: to move to another program, to keep its own copy or to hand it to whoever looks after its IT.

Inside there are three things and a LEEME.txt that explains them:

  • turnera.sql, the whole database. Any IT person loads it into MySQL with one command, and with it you can come back to Turnera or move the data to another program.
  • One spreadsheet per table —customers, appointments, payments, vouchers, clinical records…—, which opens in Excel with a double click.
  • The documents: the signed consents as PDF and the logo, each in its own file.

Passwords, clock-in PINs and integration secrets are left out: outside Turnera they are useless, and in the wrong hands someone could try to crack them.

Why two keys are needed. It is the most sensitive download in the application, because it takes the data of every customer at once, clinical records included. Only the admin account can ask for it —no other, even with every permission— and on top of that it needs a link that goes to the email of the centre's owner, the one given when signing up, which cannot be changed from the application. Whoever steals the password does not have the email, and whoever reads the email does not have the password.

  1. Press Send me the link. The screen tells you which email it goes to, with part of the address hidden.
  2. Open the email in the same browser, signed in as admin, and press Download. The button shows how far it has got.
  3. Keep the zip somewhere safe: it holds the data of all your customers.

The link works only once and expires in 30 minutes; if the download is interrupted, ask for another, up to three an hour. When it finishes another email arrives saying it has been downloaded, with the time and the address it was done from: if it was not you, change the admin password and write to us. The screen also keeps the list of exports requested and downloaded.

10. The clinical record

In the centres that use it, each customer has a history and cases, and each case its progress notes. It prints to PDF.

Every time someone opens a clinical record it is logged: who, when and what they looked at. It is not surveillance, it is what the law requires for health data, and it is what you have to be able to show if anyone asks.

Seeing the clinical record is a separate permission from seeing the customer record. Whoever is at the front desk has no need for it.

In training centres the application does not talk about a clinical record: the same fields are called starting point, goal, physical assessment and training plan, which is what they are called there.

11. When something goes wrong

“It will not let me book the appointment”

If it says the slot is taken, there is another appointment in that room or with that member of staff at that time; the warning says which. If it says the device is in use, there is another appointment at that time that needs the same one, even if it is in another room.

“Which version are you on?”

At the bottom of the left-hand menu, under the active delegation. It is the first thing to look at before writing in, because a fault already fixed in a later version is solved by updating.

“A menu option is missing”

It is a permission your user account does not have. Whoever administers the centre can give it to you.

“The customer is not receiving the portal code”

Check that the address on their record is the one you are typing, that the email gateway is set up, and that it has not landed in their junk folder. The code expires after fifteen minutes: once that time is up you have to ask for another.

“The end-of-day till does not balance”

Look first at the payments split across several payment methods and the visits that were left owing. An unpaid visit is not in the till: it is in Unpaid visits.

“I cancelled an appointment by mistake”

It cannot be recovered: you have to create it again with the same details.
