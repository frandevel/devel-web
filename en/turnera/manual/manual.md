# The Turnera manual

How Turnera is used day to day in a centre: booking, charging, and knowing what happened. It is written for the person at the front desk, not for the person who writes the software.

## Getting help

If something does not work as it should, **report it from the application itself**: at the bottom right of every screen there is the round **lifebuoy**. It is seen by whoever has the permission **Notify support**. By default only the administrator has it, and it is given to whoever you want in Administration → Roles: whoever runs into the problem is usually the person at the front desk.

1. Click the lifebuoy. *Report an incident*.
2. In *What happened*, describe what you were doing, what you expected to happen and what happened instead. A sentence like «it doesn't work» does not let us reproduce the problem, so the application will ask you for a little more.
3. Choose the **urgency**: *Low* if it can wait, *Normal* if it is annoying but you can work, *High* if something cannot be done and *Blocking* if the centre has stopped.
4. If it helps, **attach a screenshot** or another image of up to 5 MB (up to five; only images are accepted). They stay with the incident and only the support team sees them: make sure no customer data shows in the screenshot.
5. Enter your name and the email address you want us to reply to. The phone number is optional, in case we ever needed to talk to you.
6. Click *Send*. The application gives you the **incident number**: keep it in case you need to refer to it.

**You do not need to look for technical details.** We send, along with the incident, the screen you were on, your user, the Turnera version and the last error you got. If the error message had a **reference** of eight letters and digits, it reaches us too: with it we find exactly what happened.

**Where the incident ends up.** If you can view and open support tickets (section 12 ter), it is opened as a **Support ticket**, the same as *New ticket*: the application gives you its number, like *T-41*, and a button *Open in Support* to follow its status and read the team's replies. The data we send automatically (the screen, the delegation, the last error, the phone number you entered) goes inside the ticket, and the urgencies *High* and *Blocking* mark it as *It stops me working*.

If you do not have those permissions, the incident goes **by email**, as always, and the reply arrives at the email address you entered; it does not appear in Support. The same happens if the ticket service is not available at that moment: the incident goes by email and the application tells you so clearly. If it tells you the incident has been recorded but the notice could not be sent, and it is urgent, write to us at **soporte@devel.es** with the incident number.

**Is it a question about how to do something?** It is probably in this manual. It opens from the application itself with *User manual*, at the bottom of the left-hand menu next to the version, and also from the incident window. It opens in the language you have chosen in Turnera.

## 1. Logging in and finding your way around

*[Captura] The sign-in screen. Everybody signs in with their own user.*

Turnera opens in the browser, with the centre's address and the username and password you were given. There is nothing to install and it works the same on the front desk computer, on a tablet or on a phone.

**Each person has their own user account.** This is not red tape: the application records who did each thing, and with a shared account that information is worth nothing at all. What is more, each user sees only their own part — the receptionist has no need to open the clinical record, and the physiotherapist has no need to see the till.

### Accepting the terms of service

The first time the centre enters Turnera, or when DEVEL prepares a new version of the terms, the application does not open the agenda: it opens the terms screen. At the top is the summary of what has been contracted — the fee the centre starts with, the billing, the set-up — and below, in tabs, each complete document: the service contract with its annexes, the record of processing activities and the set-up checklist. Each one can be read right there and downloaded.

Accepted by **whoever administers the centre** (the user who manages the centre's preferences): they mark each document as read, write their name, ID number and position, declare that they can represent the centre and press **I accept**. Turnera saves who accepted, when, from which address and the exact fingerprint of each text, and sends a copy by email to the centre and to DEVEL. Until then **nobody at the centre can use Turnera**: the other users see a notice that the administrator must accept the terms.

If the centre prefers to sign on paper, DEVEL sends the documents, signs them and returns them; then the screen does not appear. And if the terms or the fee change later, notice is given in advance and the screen comes back only for the new version, with the previously accepted one kept in the history.

### The menu on the left

*[Captura] The menu, and right at the bottom the active delegation and the version the centre is running.*

At the top, with no heading, is what you use every day: **Home**, **Agenda**, **Point of sale**, **Unpaid visits** and **Customers**. Below, grouped, is what you set up now and then (catalogue and stock), the team under **Staff** (professionals, work calendar and working time) and what you only look up (reports and settings).

**Favourites.** Each user can put the screens they use most at the very top. When you open a screen from the menu, next to its title there is a **star**: press it and the screen moves to the **Favourites**, the first in the menu, which stays fixed at the top even when you scroll down the rest of the menu; the star is filled in, and pressing it again removes it. Favourites are **reordered by dragging** one on top of another with the mouse; on a tablet or a phone, with the up and down arrows shown next to each one. They belong to each user and follow them to any computer; the screen also stays in its usual place, and favourites give access to nothing that permissions do not give.

**Menu search.** When the menu has so many screens that they do not all fit at once, a box appears under the logo, **Find a screen…** (with the bar collapsed or with the whole menu in view it is not needed and does not appear). As you type, only the screens whose name contains what you typed remain, regardless of capitals or accents; if what you typed is part of the name of a section, all the screens in that section appear. With the arrow keys **↑** and **↓** you move through the results, **Enter** opens the highlighted one and **Esc** clears the search and brings back the full menu. Only the screens the user already sees in their menu are searched.

On a computer the menu stays fixed on the left even when you scroll down the page, and the button at the bottom collapses it to show only the icons. On a phone and on a tablet held upright it is hidden: it opens with the **☰** button at the top left and closes when you choose an entry, when you tap outside it or with the `Esc`.

If one of those entries is missing for you it is not a fault: your user account does not have that permission. Whoever administers the centre can give it to you.

It may also be that the centre does not have that part of Turnera. Some centres only use Turnera for their staff (work calendar, clocking in and the working-time record), with no agenda or till; they do not see the rest, not even whoever manages the centre. To add a part, write to support.

At the top of the menu is the centre's logo, if one has been uploaded under *Administration → Centre details*; if not, Turnera's.

### Compact layout

In the session menu, top right, you choose between **Normal layout** and **Compact layout**. The compact one shrinks the text, the margins and the height of fields and rows so that on a laptop or a small monitor more fits on each screen without scrolling. The choice is saved in that browser, so each computer can have its own.

The first time someone signs in from a small screen, Turnera offers to switch to the compact one. If they say no, they are not asked again; they can change it whenever they like from the menu.

### Changing the password

In the session menu, at the top right, there is **Change password**: it asks for the current one and the new one. Changing it **closes every other session** of that user —the one on the phone, the one on the computer at home, the one somebody might have open with the old password— and only whoever changed it stays in. A password is changed because you suspect someone else knows it, and that would be pointless if their session stayed open. The same happens when administration resets someone's password, or disables them. In all three cases **the devices registered to sign in with fingerprint are removed too**, and they have to be registered again.

### Signing in with your fingerprint

If the centre has it switched on (Preferences, Signing in to the web group), the session menu has **Sign in with fingerprint**, to register the device and sign in with the fingerprint, face or PIN of the device itself, without typing the password. Registering it asks for **the current password**: that way nobody can leave their own device set up on someone else's account by using a session left open. The password always keeps working.

In Administration → Users → (a user) you can see, at the end of the record, which devices that person signs in with by fingerprint, and you can **remove** any you do not recognise or one from a lost device. Only the person themselves can register a new one, from their own device.

### Shared devices: signing in with the card and PIN

For devices that several people use during the day —a booth tablet, a room computer— Turnera can show on the sign-in screen **one card per professional**: you tap your own, type the PIN and you are in. To sign out, just press the button **Sign out** at the top, and if the device sits **three minutes unused** the session closes by itself and the cards come back. It is the same PIN as the time clock, with the same attempts: five failures in a row lock it for both, and HR is told which device it came from.

It is off by default. You switch it on in Administration → Preferences, group *Access*, with **Shared devices**. While it is off Turnera works as usual; when you switch it on Administration → Devices. Devices that are not registered keep signing in with username and password.

#### Registering a device

1. In Administration → Devices, **Add device**: you give it a name (“Booth 1”) and the mode *Shared device*. A **code** of eight letters and digits comes out, for example `K7M4-9QXD`.
2. On the device itself, on the sign-in screen, press **Register this device** and type the code. You do not need to sign in first: the code already carries the permission.

The person who administers can also do it **even if you are not at the centre**: get the code from home and dictate it by phone to whoever is in front of the device. The code is valid for **only once** and expires after **15 minutes**; if it is not used in time, **New code** gives another. The device stays in the branch you were signed in with when registering it, and shows that branch’s cards.

#### Who appears on the cards

The active professionals of that branch who have an **PIN** and a **Turnera user** associated. The user is associated in Administration → Users → (a user), field **Professional**; a professional can only be associated with one user. On signing in with their PIN, each person works with that user’s permissions.

#### Changing or removing a device

The list shows the status of each device, when it was last used and who signed in. From there you can **rename it**, switch it to mode *Normal* (it stays in the list, but signs in with username and password) or **revoke it**: it is no longer recognised and whoever was signed in is signed out on their next action. This is what to do with a lost tablet. A revoked device comes back with a new code, and so does one whose browser data has been deleted.

On a shared device, registering a fingerprint is not offered: that device belongs to everyone. The fingerprint is for each person’s phone or laptop (see Signing in with your fingerprint).

### Waiting room: a screen that calls whoever can go in

For centres with a lot of foot traffic. It is **off** until it is turned on in *Administration → Preferences → Access*, with **Waiting room**. While it is off, Turnera works as usual and nothing described below appears.

**The screen.** In *Administration → Devices* the TV or tablet in the waiting room is registered with the mode *Waiting-room screen*, just like a shared workstation: a code appears, open `/sala-de-espera` on that device and type in the code. The screen does not sign anyone in and can only read what is being called. It is best to press **Turn sound on** once: browsers do not let sound play until someone touches the screen.

**Calling.** Whoever is attending opens the appointment and presses **Call to room**. Choose the room (the appointment's room comes preselected) and the screen immediately shows the name and the room, with a sound alert. Each room has one call at a time: calling the same room again replaces the previous call. Calls clear themselves after ten minutes, or sooner with **Remove call**.

**Calling the next one.** This is the usual way at reception, and the queue is **per professional**. In the agenda, the megaphone button opens today's waiting list, grouped by professional. You choose the room to call to (a resource or a call room) and each professional has their own button **Call the next one**: it calls the first in their queue, among their appointments today with status *On hold*. Patients of other professionals do not count or change the order. Each time an appointment moves to *On hold*, Turnera stores the arrival time by itself; nothing needs to be noted. **Arriving early gives no priority.** Each patient counts from their *effective time*, which is the later of the appointment time and the time they arrived: whoever arrives before their appointment counts from the appointment time, and whoever arrives late counts from when they arrive. Patients are called by effective time, from earliest to latest; if they coincide, the earlier appointment goes first and then the one created first. If there is no arrival time (because nobody moved it to *On hold* in Turnera), the appointment time counts. The status of the appointment does not change, and nobody who already has a call on screen is called. If that professional has nobody, it shows «Nobody is waiting». If two people at reception press at the same time, each calls a different person. With no professional chosen, nobody is called. The list is in the same order as the calls, so the first of each professional is the one the button will call; a megaphone marks whoever has already been called. In the same list, each patient has a button **Call** to call them by hand, and the appointment's own button **Call to room** is still there for one-off cases.

**Rooms without a professional.** Cash desk, blood draws or radiology have no automatic queue: reception calls the specific patient by hand. How they should work beyond that has not been decided.

**Call rooms.** Cash desk, blood draws or nursing are not agenda resources, because Turnera charges per resource. They are registered separately, in *Preferences → Access*, below the waiting-room switch: they can be renamed, moved up or down, and turned on or off, and the name cannot be repeated. They do not count towards the fee. When calling to a room, active resources come first, followed by active call rooms.

**What it shows.** In Preferences you choose between the name with the first surname shortened (“Ana R.”) or just a code, with no personal data. If the screen loses its connection to the server, it warns in red so nobody trusts what it shows.

### Setting up the waiting-room screen

**What has not been tested yet.** What follows has been checked in a desktop browser. It has not yet been tested on a real television in a waiting room: neither the automatic start of the mini PC, nor kiosk mode, nor sound through the TV speakers. Until then, do a full test before leaving it running (step 5).

**The supported setup:** a mini PC (or a browser stick) connected to the television in the room, with the browser in kiosk mode. Sending the image from another device (“casting”) is not documented.

1. **Registering the device.** In *Administration → Devices* create a workstation with the mode *Waiting-room screen*. A one-time code appears, valid for 15 minutes. On the mini PC open `https://<your centre>/sala-de-espera`, press *Register with a code* and type in the code. The key stays saved in that browser. To stop recognising the screen, revoke it from *Workstations*.
2. **Starting by itself.** On the mini PC, set up a system account that signs in without asking for a password, turn off sleep and the screensaver, and make the browser open full screen with that address at start-up. Example for Chrome: `chrome --kiosk --autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required https://<your centre>/sala-de-espera`. With that parameter there is no need to press *Turn sound on*. If it is opened any other way, press *Turn sound on* once after every start-up: browsers do not allow sound until someone touches the screen.
3. **No Turnera session on that screen.** It is not needed and not advisable: the screen reads with its own key and does not need anyone to be signed in. Do not open Turnera with a user in that browser.
4. **If something fails.** If the screen loses its connection, a red warning appears and it recovers by itself as soon as there is a network. If the device is changed (or the browser data is deleted), a new code is issued in *Workstations* and it is registered again.
5. **Volume and test.** Turn up the volume on the television and on the mini PC's system, and make a test call from an appointment with *Call to room* to hear the alert and see the name. Then remove the call with *Remove call*.

### Notices from Turnera

Sometimes a strip appears at the very top, also on the sign-in screen and in the booking portal, with a notice from us to every centre: that we are going to stop for a few minutes for an update, or that we need to go into maintenance. If it is **red**, it is urgent. It cannot be closed and it disappears on its own when it is no longer needed.

While a new version is being installed, the notice says that for a few minutes the application may be interrupted for a moment and that, when the installation finishes, you will be told in the same place. Then a notice appears with the button *Reload now*: save what you are doing and reload the page to get the new version.

### Search by words in any list

The search boxes of the lists —products, vouchers, services, suppliers, professionals, service groups, maintenance, outstanding payments, users…— all work the same way: each word typed after a space is one more filter. The words are searched **in any order** and capitals and accents do not matter; only items that match every word appear. With “voucher 3 hair”, the 3-session hair-removal vouchers appear, even if the name says “Laser hair removal · voucher of 3”.

### Sorting any list

Every list —customers, suppliers, services, products, stock, outstanding payments, reports…— is sorted by **clicking the column header**. Once, from lowest to highest or from A to Z; again, the other way round; and a third time goes back to the usual order. The arrow next to the title tells you which column the list is sorted by and in which direction.

The whole list is sorted, not just the page on screen: sort the customers by registration date and go to page two, and it carries on where the first one ended. Anything empty —a customer with no email, a product with no brand— always goes last, in both directions, which is where you look for it.

### Which version am I on

Right at the bottom of the left-hand menu, under the active delegation, is the **Turnera version** this centre is running. It is the first thing you are asked for when something does not work, so it is worth knowing where it is before you need it. Hovering over it also says when it was built. It reads like this: `2026.9.262-bdc9dd2` is from 2026, month 9, the 262nd change of that month; what comes after the hyphen identifies the exact change, and it is what you should copy when asking for help.

### Languages

Turnera speaks **Spanish, English, German and Portuguese** (from Portugal). Each person picks their own in the selector at the top and it stays set; anyone who does not pick one sees the centre's, which is changed in *Administration → Preferences*.

Whatever a customer is given or sent does not come out in the language of whoever presses the button, but in the **customer's own**: on their record, *Edit → Language*. That covers tickets, quotes, reports, consent forms, booking-portal emails and payment links. If the record says *The centre's*, it comes out in the centre's language. So a German physiotherapist works in Turnera in German and prints the ticket in Spanish for her patient from Valencia.

What the centre writes itself -- the names of services and products, notes -- comes out as written. Consent forms and the WhatsApp reminder can have their text in each language: see Consent forms in other languages and The reminder in other languages.

### Recommend Turnera

At the top, next to the language, whoever runs the centre sees the **Recommend Turnera** button. It opens the **referral code** of the centre -- always the same, with its name in front, like `AURORA-7K2Q` -- and the ways to pass it on: copy the code, copy the link to the Turnera page with the code already filled in, or send it by WhatsApp or email.

- **For you:** one free month for every resource the recommended centre contracts. If it takes four rooms, four months. They are earned when that centre pays its first fee and come off your next invoices, up to twelve months a year.
- **For the recommended centre:** the first month is free and, if they bring data from another program, the migration costs them €150 instead of €300.

At the foot of the dialog you can see how many months the centre has earned this year and whether any recommended centre has not paid its first fee yet. Only those who can change the centre's preferences see the button.

### Looking up a price without opening the catalogue

To give a price on the spot without opening any record, there is the **price lookup**. It opens with the shortcut `Alt` + `P` (on a Mac, `Option` + `P`) from any screen, or with the button **€** at the top, which is the one to use on a phone or tablet. Press the shortcut again to close it.

You type what you are looking for -- the name of a service, a voucher, a package or a product, in any order and with or without accents -- and the name and price appear, VAT included. Everything active with a price shows up; anything you don't want to offer is disabled or left without a price in its catalogue record. A package without its own price is worth what its services and products add up to. Products also show how many units are left at the centre you are in («5 units in stock», or «Out of stock» in red). Nothing else is shown: no costs, no VAT breakdown and no record.

- **Who sees it:** whoever has the permission *Look up prices*. Reception, Professional, Management and the Catalogue module role have it. A role that already existed does not get it by itself: tick it in *Administration → Roles*. This is what lets a professional give a price without being able to open the tariffs.
- **What is not stored:** lookups leave no trace.

### Home

*[Captura] The home screen sums the day up. Every block leads to the screen that explains it.*

The home screen sums up the day: the appointments still to come, what has been charged, what is still unpaid and the birthdays. Each block is a shortcut — click it and you land on the screen that explains it.

The **customers** and the **birthdays** are two different cards, and each has its own permission, so they can be granted separately. The customers card shows **the five latest registrations**; press anywhere on the row and the record opens. It is for everyday things: someone registered a customer a moment ago, you need to add a phone number or book them in, and searching by a surname that may have been misspelt is the long way round.

The **birthdays** card shows this week's, with the day and the age each customer turns. Today's is highlighted, and the row also leads to the record, in case you want to congratulate them or give them something. By default reception and management see them: “See customers on Home” and “See birthdays on Home”.

*[Captura] Customers and birthdays, each on its own card and with its own permission.*

The **customers with pending vouchers** card tells you how many customers still have voucher sessions to use and shows the ten most urgent: first whoever has a voucher about to expire, with the sessions left and the date. It is the list to call before those sessions are lost, and each row opens the customer's record. It is shown to whoever can see vouchers or customers.

In a centre that manages its **staff** (the work calendar and attendance control), the home screen adds cards for it:

- **Who is in now**: how many of the professionals on shift today are in, and since what time. Below it, **who should already have clocked in and has not**, with the time their shift began: it is almost always an oversight, and seeing it in time avoids a gap that would have to be corrected later.
- **Holidays and absences**: who is away today and who will be during the week, with the reason and the days.
- **Hours this month**: the worked hours against the planned ones, the out-of-shift hours of the month and who has the most. Below it, the **overtime bank of the year**: what is still to be compensated and who is getting close to the legal ceiling of 80 hours (from 80 %), has gone over it or was given a rest too late.

Whoever can look up the clock-ins sees the attendance and hours cards; whoever can see the work calendars sees the holidays and absences card. If nobody has been scheduled yet and nobody has clocked in, they do not appear.

#### Customising the Home screen

Not everyone wants to see the same thing when they log in: at the front desk the till is in the way and there is no room for pending payments. The **Customise**button, at the top right, opens the list of cards that person may see and lets you **switch off the ones you do not want and order them with the arrows**, one row up or down at a time. Nothing has to be dragged. The choice is saved for that user, on any computer they log in from, and *Reset* goes back to the usual Home screen.

*[Captura] Customising Home: switch off what you do not need and order it with the arrows.*

What decides which cards are offered is **permissions**: here you cannot switch on a card the role does not let you see, and if the role later loses a permission, its card disappears by itself. The other way round too: whoever has never customised the Home screen sees, without touching anything, every new card their permissions give them. The till of the day has its own permission, “See the till on the Home screen”, separate from charging at the till, so it can be taken away from a role without stopping them from charging.

## 2. The agenda

Menu → Agenda

This is the screen you spend the day on. You can look at it in three ways, and the button at the top left switches between them:

| View | One column per | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Day by member of staff** | Each member of staff | The usual one: who is seeing whom today. It is the one you get when you log in. |
| **Day by room** | Each room, court or studio | When what you are sharing out is the place and not the person. |
| **Week by member of staff** | Each day of the week | For booking someone who asks “what have you got next week?”. |

*[Captura] A member of staff’s week. The striped day is a closed stretch: here, a bank holiday.*

The view you choose is remembered: when you come back tomorrow it will be as you left it.

**The buttons in the header are just an icon**, so the agenda has room: the arrows and the calendar to go from one day to another and back to today, the padlock to *close a stretch*, the printer to *print*, the people for the *waiting list* and the **+** for the *new appointment*. Hover over each one and it says what it does.

### How full each day is

When you open the calendar to pick the date, each day with appointments has a **point**, as on flight booking sites: **green** (hollow) if it is below 50 %, **orange** from 50 % and **red**, slightly bigger, from 75 %. A day with no appointments has no dot. Hovering over it shows how many appointments it has, how much is booked and how much time is left free.

Occupancy is measured in time, not in number of appointments: what is booked against the professionals' shifts according to their work calendar, or the diary's opening hours for those without one. The dots update by themselves when someone books or cancels an appointment. The orange and red thresholds are changed in **Administration → Preferences → Agenda**.

### The line of the hour

A **dashed red line crosses the agenda at the current time**, with the time written on the left. Everything above it has already happened; the appointment the line runs through is the one that should be under way right now. It moves by itself, with no reloading.

It only shows when the agenda is on today —in the week view, only over today's column— and within the hours the grid paints. If you look at next Monday there is no line, because there is nothing to mark.

*[Captura] The day's agenda, one column per member of staff. The orange bar and the star mark a first visit.*

### The start time of a slot

When the agenda’s time steps are shorter than an hour and you hover over a free slot, its start time appears, so you know what time the appointment would fall at if you place it there. Reception sees the same agenda hours as the rest of the team.

### Booking, moving and cancelling appointments

- **Booking an appointment:** click the free slot where you want it to go. The form opens with the time and the member of staff already filled in; all that is missing is the customer and the service.
- **New customer calling on the phone:** while no customer has been chosen, below the search box there is *+ New customer*. It opens a **first visit** which only asks for first name, surname and mobile (the ID number is optional: some people will not give it over the phone), and leaves the customer set on the appointment. The rest of the record (ID, date of birth, email...) is filled in when they arrive. In the meantime the appointment and the record show **First visit** in blue; as soon as the record is opened and the other details are saved, the notice goes away by itself. When the mobile number is typed, and the ID number if given, Turnera checks whether they already belong to another record (it does not matter how they are written: with spaces, with +34, with dots or hyphens...). If they do, it shows who it is, whether the phone, the ID or both match, with the button *Use this record*; the first visit is not registered unless it is ticked *It is another person with the same phone or ID* (a mother and her daughter, or a minor with their mother's ID). When a full record is registered from Customers the same notice appears, although there it does not prevent saving.
- **The room, in the professional views:** in *Day by member of staff* and *Week by member of staff* the column is the person, so the appointment has a drop-down with the room (or the court, or the hall). The professional's usual room comes preselected if it is free at that time and, if not, the first free one; taken rooms are marked «taken» and cannot be chosen. If the proposed room is taken while the appointment is being filled in, it is saved in the first free one and says so. In *Day by resource* the room is the column that was clicked and the drop-down does not appear. The usual room is chosen in the professional's record (*Usual room*, or *None* so that it always takes the first free one).
- **If the appointment doesn't fit, the warning says why:** what it clashes with and what to do. For example, «It doesn't fit: Room 2 already has an appointment from 10:00 to 11:00 with Ana López (Professional 3). Choose another time or another free slot», or that the professional already has an appointment, or is on a visit, at that time.
- **Automatic duration:** in a new appointment the duration is set to *Automatic*: what the chosen services add up to and, while there are none, the room's step. Another one can be chosen by hand.
- **Opening an appointment:** click it.
- **The previous customer, one click away:** clicking the customer box without typing anything shows the last 5 customers you have worked with (in an appointment, at the point of sale or by opening their record). The customer box at the point of sale works the same way.
- **Adding a service to an existing appointment:** the duration grows by the length of the new service (and removing it shortens it), even if it had been adjusted by hand; you can fine-tune it afterwards. If that makes the appointment reach the next one of the same professional or room, it warns you: there would not be time to do everything in that appointment.
- **See what is going to be done without opening it:** rest the mouse on the appointment for a moment. A card appears, like the voucher or “Owes” ones, with the customer and the time at the top, their services one per line and, below, the professional, the status and the appointment's notices. Over the voucher or “Owes” label you get that label's card instead.
- **Move an appointment back to an earlier time:** an appointment that has been moved at some point (to another day, time, practitioner or room) carries a clock next to its time. Clicking it opens its history: where it is now and the times it has been at, each with when and by whom it was moved from there, and the *Move back here* button. Moving back is a move like dragging it, with the same checks: if the slot is taken or the practitioner does not work at that time, it says so. It does not notify the client, because it is done when the client asks for it. The history starts counting with this version of Turnera; appointments moved before have no clock.
- **Past days:** you can go to any earlier day to look at it, open its appointments, charge them or mark who did not come. What you cannot do is **book a new appointment or move one to a day before today**: the agenda says so when you click a slot, and the appointment calendar does not let you pick those days. Doing it needs the **«Citar en días pasados» (book on past days)** permission, which out of the box only the administrator has. **Today you can book at any time**, also at a time that has already passed: noting down at ten twenty the ten o'clock one is normal at the front desk.
- **Taking it to another day:** open it and press *Cut*. A bar appears above the agenda with the appointment you are moving, and its card stays faded in its place. Go to the day you want, click the free slot and confirm with *Paste*: the whole appointment moves, with its services, comments and voucher, and if the slot is taken you are told and can pick another. Until you paste it nothing has changed: with *Cancel* or the **Escape** key it stays as it was. Once pasted, the agenda offers *Notify on WhatsApp* to tell the customer the new day.
- **What can be moved:** an appointment that changes day goes back to *Absent* and unconfirmed, because what was known belonged to the previous day. An appointment *in progress* can be moved to another time or cabin that same day, but not to another day: if it is interrupted, it is closed as appropriate and a new one is booked. An appointment already charged or cancelled does not move: it records what happened that day. An *attended* or one of someone who *did not come* can only be moved by whoever has the permission **«Mover citas atendidas» (move attended appointments)**, which out of the box the administrator has: it is meant for one marked by mistake or for the customer who did not come and calls to change it. Without the permission it does not show *Cut*, and if you try to drag it, the diary says which permission is needed.
- **Moving it:** drag it. If you drop it in another column it also changes professional or room, depending on the view. In the professional views it keeps its room and, if that is taken at the new time, it moves to the first free one.
- **Changing how long it lasts:** drag the bottom edge.
- **The device also counts when you move or lengthen it.** If the appointment needs a device that another appointment has taken at the new time, Turnera warns you just as it does when creating it, naming the appointment that has it: you can change it or save it anyway (see Devices).
- **Cancelling it:** open it, press *Cancel* and say **who is calling it off**: the customer, the centre (the professional cannot make it, the cabin or the court is unavailable, something broke) or an **external cause** (rain on an outdoor court, a power cut). It is required, because only what the customer calls off counts in their history and in lost appointments. You can also write the reason in your own words, which is kept in the comments of the appointment. It also records **who cancelled it and with what role** (for example, *ana (Reception)*), with the day and time: the record of the cancelled appointment says so in one line. If the customer cancels it from the booking portal, it shows *Booking portal*, with no role. Those cancelled before this information existed do not have it, and it is not made up.
- **An appointment already charged** can only be cancelled by whoever has the **«Anular citas ya cobradas» (cancel charged appointments)** permission, which out of the box only the administrator has. For everyone else, *Cancel* is greyed out and, when you hover over it, says why. Cancelling it does not give the money back: for that, use the ticket refund. Through the booking portal, the customer cannot cancel an appointment already paid either: they have to call the centre.
- **Removing an appointment made by mistake:** within the **15 minutes** after creating it, a fourth option appears when cancelling it, *Created by mistake*. The appointment disappears from the agenda and counts in no report, because it was never a booking. After that time it is no longer offered: it is a cancellation by someone, and you have to say by whom. Even though it disappears from the agenda, it also records who removed it and with what role, and whoever has the permission *Appointments report* can see it with the button *Cancellations* in the header of the agenda, which leads to the cancellations report with the appointments created by mistake in the last 30 days.
- **Taking cancelled appointments off the view:** tick *Hide cancelled*, under the header. The agenda remembers the box, so next time you will find it as you left it.
- **Keep the names in view when scrolling:** tick *Keep the column headers in view*, next to it. The row with the professional or the room of each column stays at the top while you scroll down through the hours. Each workstation remembers it on its own: on a large screen you may not need it.

**No need to reload.** Whatever is changed at another desk, whatever customers book online and whatever comes in from a connected program shows up on its own in the agenda you have open. A new appointment on the day you are looking at pulses for a moment so you notice it, without moving the screen from where you had it.

**What each person can do in the diary depends on their permissions.** Whoever cannot book sees the whole diary, but gets no new appointment button, clicking a free slot does nothing and appointments cannot be dragged or stretched: clicking one opens it to read, without a save button. The buttons to close off time, the waiting list and printing only show up for whoever has their permission; the waiting list one, besides, only if the centre has it switched on. Inside an appointment, *Cancel*, *Charge* and *WhatsApp* are always shown, but switched off for whoever cannot use them; hovering over them says why.

**The appointment window can be moved aside.** Drag it by its title to see the part of the agenda it covers while you create or change it. A click outside it does not close it, so nothing you were typing gets lost: it closes with the *X*, with *Close* or with the **Escape**. *Charge* first saves whatever was changed in the appointment —an added service, for instance— and then takes you to the till. And if the customer record is opened from the appointment, its *Back* goes back to the open appointment, not to the customer list.

**The service sets the duration.** A new appointment starts with its length at *0 minutes* and the services added to it build it up. If it is saved with no services and without touching the length, the appointment keeps the minimum step of the centre (the *Minimum length of an appointment*). When you pick the services for the appointment, the duration adjusts itself to what they add up to, rounding up to the slot of that room: two twenty-minute services in a room that runs in fifteens take forty-five, not forty. The moment you touch the duration selector by hand, you are in charge and we stop touching it. On an appointment that already exists nothing is recalculated either: its duration is the one it has.

**Services, in two lists.** On the left are the *Available*, with their short code in front and a search box that finds by name or code; on the right, those the appointment includes, with just the name and minutes, and at the top how many there are and how long they add up to. A click marks a service and the arrows in the middle move it from one list to the other; a double click (or the Enter key) moves it directly. Vouchers do not appear in the appointment: a voucher is sold at the POS, it is not a treatment given in a room.

**Quick selection by code.** The *Quick*button, in the header of the available ones, opens all the **short codes** of the price list at a glance —HMBS, AXIL…— to click two or three in a row, and on accepting they are added to the appointment. Each code is a single service. The code is set in *Catalogue → Services*, in the record of each one (*Short code*: letters and digits, and it cannot be repeated). It no longer appears in the appointment's list: it is for finding the service, not for reading it.

**Cancelling cannot be undone.** A cancelled appointment stays in view, crossed out — its slot is free again to book over, or it can be taken off the view with *Hide cancelled* —, but it cannot be recovered: it has to be created again. And marking someone as *No show* also cancels the appointment, so do not use it for “has not arrived yet”.

If you try to book two appointments at once in the same room or with the same member of staff, the application will not let you and tells you which one it clashes with. It is the only thing it truly blocks: the rest are warnings.

While you are outside the agenda, at the bottom right and just above the help button, there appears **a calendar icon: *Back to the appointment***. Press it and the appointment opens again, on its day, just as you left it. The icon disappears by itself when you reach the agenda; it does not appear in the POS because there is already a button for the same thing there.

### Drop-in visits: another professional comes to see the client

Diary → open the appointment → Visits → Add visit

Sometimes one professional has the appointment and another has to drop in for a moment to see the client: to assess a treatment, to diagnose something. That is a **visit**. It is noted from the appointment itself, in the section *Visits*, with *Add visit*: who drops in, at what time (within the appointment; it starts halfway) and how long it takes (15 minutes to begin with), with a note if needed.

- The visit shows in the column of whoever makes it, in *Day by member of staff* and in their *Week by member of staff*, as an orange strip with the client and the room. The appointment card says who drops in and when. Clicking the strip opens the appointment or removes the visit.
- **It takes the time of whoever visits, not the room.** They cannot be booked at that time, and the warning explains it: «It doesn't fit: Mariluz has a visit from 13:30 to 13:45 with Ana López (Room 2)».
- **It goes with the appointment.** If the appointment moves, the visit moves with it; if it is cancelled, the visit disappears. If whoever visits already has an appointment at the new time, the appointment does not move and the warning says what it clashes with.
- If, when it is noted, whoever visits already has an appointment at that time, it says with whom and from what time to what time, so another time or another person can be chosen.

### Classes: appointments with several places

A tennis class, a pilates class or a pressotherapy group are a single appointment with several customers. For an appointment to take more than one, two things are needed: the place must have room for them (Administration → Rooms → (one) → Capacity) and the service must have places (*Catalogue → Services*, field *Capacity*). The smaller of the two numbers rules: a two-place service in a one-place room is still a one-place appointment.

- **Enrol.** When you open an already saved appointment that takes several people, the list of who is coming appears under the comments. Look up the customer and press *Enrol*, until the places are full. The first one is whoever had the appointment.
- **Who came.** Each person has their own status in their drop-down: *Enrolled*, *Attended*, *No show* or *Withdrew*. That is why these appointments do not show the status buttons of the whole appointment: they would say the same thing twice.
- **Charge.** Each person pays their own with the *Charge* button on their row, which opens the till with them and the services of the class. Once charged, their row says *Billed* and they can no longer be charged again, removed from the list or have their status changed. At the till, at the top, under *To charge*, everyone who came and has not paid yet appears one by one.

If what you want is to see several people one after another —five-minute services, one at 10:00 and another at 10:10— you need none of this: just lower the agenda step of that room (see *Each room's step*, under Administration) and give each one an appointment.

### Repeating appointments

For the client who comes every Tuesday, the class every Monday and Thursday or the check-up on the 1st of each month, there is no need to book the appointments one by one. When booking a new appointment, tick **Repeats** and choose:

- **Every day**, **every week** (and which days: Monday and Thursday, for example) or **every month** (and which days of the month: the 1st and the 15th, for example). Months without that day —a 31st in November— are skipped.
- **Until when:** until a date or a number of times. One year from the first one at most.

They all go at the same time, with the same professional, the same room, the same services and the same comments as the first one. When you press *Review the days* the agenda **shows the list of dates before saving anything**: which ones fit and, for those that do not, why —there is already another appointment at that time, it is a public holiday, the professional does not work that day…—. When you confirm, those that fit are booked and the rest are skipped: nothing is ever booked on top of something else. The skipped ones are listed in the notice, so you can look at them by hand if needed.

In the agenda, the appointments of a series carry a **circular arrows icon** next to the name, and when opened it says so at the top. Each one is a normal appointment: it is confirmed, charged and marked separately.

- **Changing one:** when saving, the agenda asks whether the change is *only for this one* or for *this and following*. The following ones keep their day and take the new time, duration, professional, services and comments. Those already charged are not touched, and if the change does not fit on some day, that one stays as it was: the notice says which days.
- **Cancelling:** when cancelling an appointment of a series there are two buttons, *Cancel only this one* and *This and following*. The previous ones are never touched, and neither are those already charged: those are cancelled one by one, with their permission.

### Waiting list

Agenda → Waiting list

**It comes switched off.** It is rarely used, and a button that almost nobody presses gets in the way on the screen people look at most. Whoever wants it switches it on in *Administration → Preferences → Agenda*, with *Use the waiting list*. Switching it off only hides it: whatever had been noted down is kept in case it is switched on again.

Somebody asks for a time that is already taken. Instead of writing it on a piece of paper, it goes on that day's waiting list: who they are, what they wanted done, the stretch that would suit them and whatever is worth knowing when you call. The agenda button shows up front how many are waiting on the day you are looking at.

**When a booking is cancelled, the screen says so:** “two people are on the waiting list for this day”. Whoever cancels decides who to call — the list goes in order of arrival —, and once they are given a time they are *taken off* the list.

**Turnera neither calls nor writes to anybody.** It is on purpose: notifying on its own, the first in line and with a deadline to reply, is a whole feature —with its queue, its retries and its “what if two of them answer”— and deserves to be built with real use in front of it. The usual thing is a list with two names, settled with one phone call.

The phone number only shows on the list for whoever holds the permission for the *contact details of the customer*, just like on the customer record. And taking somebody off deletes nothing: the row keeps who took them off and when, so tomorrow you can say who was called and who was not.

### Closing stretches in the agenda

Agenda → Close a stretch

A bank holiday, a spell of leave, a room being serviced or a broken device. You pick the day, the time band, what it affects — the whole centre, one room or one person — and the reason.

The closed stretch appears hatched on the grid, with the reason written over it. **The reason is not compulsory but it is worth putting in**: in two weeks' time nobody will remember why that Thursday is closed, and it ends in a phone call.

**If there are already appointments in that stretch, you are warned before closing**: “There are 2 appointments in that stretch”. It is only a warning. Closing does not cancel or move any appointment; talking to those customers is still up to you. Cancelled appointments do not count, and neither does an appointment that ends exactly when the closure starts.

**If you make a mistake, reopen it.** Every stretch closed by hand has an open padlock in the corner: click it, confirm, and the stretch is free again. With the permission *Edit closures* the padlock opens the closure to change the reason and whether it deducts hours without reopening it; it can also be reopened from there. A closure that deducts hours can only be reopened by someone with *Edit closures*. Appointments do not change. Only those who can close stretches see the padlock, and it does not appear on what the work calendar marks (holidays, bank holidays or off-shift), which is changed there. If two stretches start at the same time in the same column, the padlock and handle of the second move to the left so both can be clicked. In a professional's week view their closures and those of the whole centre appear; a room's are seen in the resource view.

**A stretch for one person can also be deducted from their hours.** It is what is used for a meal at an odd time or a personal errand. With the staff module switched on, when closing a stretch for a person there appears *Deduct from hours*, **ticked by default**; when closing a room or the whole centre it does not appear. Each closed stretch of a person shows at a glance, with a clock, whether it deducts from their hours or not. Unticked, the stretch stays as an informative note in their work calendar and deducts nothing. Ticked, whatever the stretch covers of their shift stops counting as planned hours, from the moment it is closed and even if nobody has clocked in yet: the work calendar shows those hours short against the agreement. If the person stays clocked in during the stretch, what overlaps between the clocked time, the stretch and their shift is also deducted from their normal hours; if they clock out, those hours were no longer clocked. Either way worked and planned go down by the same amount. No PIN is needed and there is no minimum, and the hours are not made up automatically. Reopening the stretch also removes the deduction; in a closed year it cannot be done.

**A closure can repeat every day.** Tick *Repeat every day* and choose *Until*: the same stretch is closed every day up to that one, inclusive, for at most one year. If any of those days already has appointments in that stretch, the warning lists them before closing; the appointments are not touched. Each day is a closure of its own: you can move or reopen one without touching the rest. If *Deduct from hours*is ticked, it is deducted on each of the days, and if it cannot be done on any of them (no calendar that year, or the year closed) none is closed.

**A closed stretch moves like an appointment.** Grab it by the handle in its corner, next to the padlock, and drag it to another time; or stretch the tab on its bottom edge to change how long it lasts. It can be taken to another column: a person's to another person, a room's to another room and, in the week view, to another day. A whole-centre one only changes time. If it left a partial absence in the work calendar, the absence goes with it; moving one that deducts hours needs the permission *Plan the work calendars*. The rest of the stretch still ignores clicks: you can book on top of it just as before. Whatever is closed, moved or reopened at one terminal shows up straight away on the others.

In the rooms view, only closures of that room or of the whole centre are striped; a person’s closure does not stripe the rooms, and vice versa. Each closure shows in the view that has its column.

**Closing is not forbidding.** On the booking portal the closed slot disappears and the customer does not see it. At the front desk, on the other hand, you get a warning and can carry on: if the room frees up sooner than expected, you know it and the program does not.

### Printing the agenda

Agenda → Print

Opens the browser's print window with the day being viewed, one professional per sheet. It does not download any file: from there you choose the printer or, if you prefer, “Save as PDF”. Each line carries the client number, name and surnames, the service, the start time, the appointment notes and, if the client has vouchers with sessions, which one and how many sessions are left out of the total it was sold with, for example *4/5*. It carries no phone number, end time or cabin.

Pressing the button prints the whole centre (professionals with no appointments that day do not use a sheet). Opening the little arrow lets you choose a single person, and their sheet is printed even if they have no appointments that day. Cancelled appointments and those of no-shows are not included. This is what is printed in the morning to carry the day around and so that, if the power goes out, the centre can keep working.

## 3. Customers

Menu → Customers

The search box at the top searches by first name, surname, phone, tax ID or **customer number**. For the first name and surnames, **the start of each word** is enough: «iba» finds Ibáñez but not Arribas, and «ana gar» finds Ana García. Phone and tax ID are found by any fragment; the customer number must be exact. If you type **only digits, and fewer than 9 of them**, the search understands it is a customer number (a phone number has 9): if there is a customer with that number, you get **only that customer**, without those that have it inside their phone or tax ID; and if nobody has it, it searches as usual. With 9 digits or more, or with letters, nothing changes. **If what is typed is mostly digits** (three or more, and at least half of the text), it also searches by phone, mobile and tax ID comparing only the digits: «666123456» finds a phone stored as «666 12-34 56», and the +34 or 0034 prefix does not need to be typed. It is the same search as the one in appointments and in charging. The list is sorted by clicking the header of any column (how).

**The customer number appears everywhere**: in the list, on the record, on the agenda card (in short appointments, when you press it), in the appointment and payment search, in pending payments and in the reports. For customers who came from another platform it is the one they had there, and new ones get the next number when they are created. And **a customer's name in any list is a link to their record**, just like on the home screen; in the appointment and at the checkout, the card button next to the customer also takes you there.

A customer record holds their details, visit history, vouchers and notes. The visit history comes **before the signed documents**, because it is what people look at most. **The phone number and the email address are links**: clicking them opens the dialler or the email program, with no copying or pasting. This happens throughout the application —also for professionals, suppliers and branches—, so there is nowhere you need to copy a number by hand. In the history, **each service and each product takes you to its record**: clicking its name opens it in the catalogue, if the person looking has permission to see services or products; otherwise it reads as plain text. In each visit in the history, the comment icon is filled in green if it has any, and a **non-conformity** is marked with a large red exclamation mark: hovering over it shows what was noted, by whom and when.

*[Captura] A customer record. The phone number and the email address are links.*

Whoever can edit records but does not have the «See phones and emails» permission does not see the customer's contact details when editing them —phone, mobile, email and address— nor can change them: a notice appears instead, and saving leaves the contact details as they were.

In the history, sessions settled with a voucher carry a ticket icon: hover over it (or tap it, or use the keyboard) to see which voucher they were from and the sessions used from it, with the date of each. The permission to see vouchers is needed. Old imported sessions with no known voucher only show the icon. The same happens in the customer record’s voucher list: hovering over the ticket icon next to the group, or over the sessions, shows the card of the sessions used from that voucher.

Lines that were not made in Turnera but arrived with the **import from another program**, carry a small mark (a downward arrow) next to the name of the service or product. Hovering over it says so. Those charged here do not carry it.

What was charged on each visit —the amount and the *Ticket* of the history, which starts the printing, is seen by whoever has the permission “See the amounts and tickets in the history”. If it is removed from a role or a person, their history still shows what was done and when, but without amounts or ticket. It is given by default to whoever can already see the records.

### How they heard about us

In the customer record, and when booking a *first visit*, you can choose **How did they hear about us?**: internet or search engines, social media, friends, family, acquaintances, radio, television, press or magazines, street advertising, walking past, referred by another centre or recommended by a professional. If it is none of these, *Other* lets you write it in a few words. It is optional, and the *Customers* report filters by it.

### Comments on a visit and nonconformities

Each visit in the history has an icon on its left to **comment on it**. A comment is *Information* (a note about that visit) or a **Nonconformity**: a complaint or something that went wrong. A visit with comments shows a speech bubble, and one with a nonconformity a **red exclamation mark** so that nobody overlooks it. Hovering over it shows the text, with who wrote it and when; clicking it shows them all and lets you add another. Only whoever has the permission «Delete comments and non-conformities», which the administrator has out of the box, can delete them; everyone else does not see the delete button.

### Visits with several professionals

If several people attended the same visit (two services charged on one ticket, each with its professional), the history names them all in the professional column and, under each service, who did it.

In the history, a service that was settled against a voucher carries a small ticket icon next to its name (hovering says “Paid with voucher”); one without it was charged separately. Whoever cannot see the amounts sees it too, because it is not money, and it lets you count at a glance the sessions that came out of the voucher.

### Under-age customers: the legal guardian

When the date of birth says the customer **is not yet 18**, the record shows the section *Legal guardian details* by itself: relationship, first name, surnames, address, phone, ID document type and number, and email. For an adult, or with no date of birth, the section does not appear. In the minor's record, if there is no guardian yet, it says so. As they are another person's contact details, only those with the “See phones and emails” permission can see and change them. When a customer's data is erased at their request, these are erased too.

### Record notes

Customer record → Notes

*[Captura] Record notes: whoever can write sees the box; whoever can delete sees the bin on each note.*

The record has a notebook for what is worth knowing about that person next time: that they prefer afternoon appointments, that they should be notified by WhatsApp. Each note keeps who wrote it and when.

Writing and deleting are **two different permissions**. With “Write customer notes” you see the box to write in and the button *Add note*; without it, notes can only be read. With “Delete clients’ notes” each note also has a bin, which asks before deleting: **a deleted note cannot be recovered**. Being able to write does not give the right to delete, nor the other way round. By default reception and management write, and management deletes.

### The customer's photo

Customer record → Photo

The record carries a photo of the customer, so they can be recognised at the front desk. Until there is one, a silhouette is shown. Whoever can edit records presses *Photo*, below the details, and chooses:

- **Upload photo**: a JPEG or PNG photo from the computer. Turnera reduces it and removes the data the camera writes into it (where and with what it was taken) before saving it.
- **Take photo with the camera**: with the computer's webcam. The live image opens; if there is more than one camera, it is chosen at the top. *Take photo* freezes it, *Retake* goes back to the live image and *Save* puts it on the record. Only the square you see is kept, so it is best to keep the face in the centre. The option does not appear if the browser cannot use cameras.
- **Remove photo**: it first asks “Remove the photo of…?”, with the customer's name, and only after confirming with *Remove photo* does the record go back to the silhouette and the photo is deleted. *Cancel* leaves it as it was.

The first time, the browser asks whether it may use the camera: you have to accept. If permission was refused, if no camera is connected or if another program is using it (a video call, for example), Turnera says so and lets you try again. The camera switches off as soon as the photo is taken or the window is closed. Everyone who can see the record can see the photo, and it also goes in the export of the centre's data.

### Deactivating a customer

A customer who no longer comes is not deleted: they are **deactivated**. In their record, *Edit* and untick *Active*. They keep everything —appointments, payments, vouchers, history—, but **no longer show up in searches**: in the customer list, when booking an appointment and at checkout. To see them, tick *Include inactive* next to the search box; they appear with the tag *Inactive*, and in their record, *Inactive*. Reactivating them is ticking again *Active*.

When the tick is removed, before saving, Turnera warns about what is left pending: the **appointments from today on** (which stay in the diary: cancel them if the customer is not coming), what they **owe** and the **vouchers with sessions** left unused. It warns; it does not stop you.

### When the customer asks for their data to be erased

Customer record → Erase their data

This is the **right to erasure** under the GDPR. It is done by whoever has the permission *Erase the data of a customer who asks for it* (by default, management). Their name, ID document, phone numbers, email, address, date of birth, photo, notes, alerts, waiting list and portal access are erased, and their appointments from today on are cancelled. The record stays, with the name *Datos borrados* and its number, because its visits, payments and vouchers refer to it, and they stay in the centre's accounts.

What the law requires to be kept **is kept**: the **invoices**, as they are, and the **clinical history** (at least five years, under Spanish Act 41/2002), blocked: it appears on no screen, but stays identified in case the customer or a court asks for it. Before erasing, the screen says what is erased, what is kept and whether appointments, debts or vouchers are left pending, and asks for the name and surnames to be typed by hand.

**It cannot be undone.** Only do it when the customer has asked for it, and keep their request.

### Going to their next appointment

The record has a **upcoming appointments** with the ones that customer has ahead, each with its own *See in the agenda*button. Clicking it opens the agenda of the right day and **the appointment pulses by itself for three seconds**, with the screen scrolled to it.

After those three seconds the pulse fades and the agenda is the one you always use. That is on purpose: whoever gets there has come to move the appointment, to charge it or to look at the one next to it, and an agenda you have to «unlock» with a click before you can touch it gets in the way exactly when you were about to work.

### Club points

If the centre switches it on in *Administration → Preferences*, every purchase gives the customer points, and those points can later be used as a payment method. Two figures are set: **how many points each euro spent gives** and **how many points each euro costs** when redeeming them. With one and one, a euro is a point.

Neither paying with a voucher nor paying with points gives points, for the obvious reason: with the first the money came in the day the voucher was bought, and with the second no money comes in.

#### Where the balance comes from

A member's record carries their balance and, underneath, **the history of movements**: every line says what happened, how many points changed and what they were left with. A purchase, a redemption, a cancelled sale, an adjustment by hand or two records merged.

**This is what you show when a customer argues about their balance.** The balance used to be a loose number going up and down with no trace; while only the till touched it you could live with that, but once management can give points away, every one of them has to be explainable.

#### Paying with points

At the point of sale, points are one more way to pay, with the centre's own name behind them — *Aurora points* —, and **they are only offered if the customer is a member and has enough balance for what is being charged**. Whoever does not have enough is not shown them, so nobody has to try at the counter to find out.

#### Adding or taking away points by hand

With *Adjust points* they are given or taken away, positive or negative, and **the reason is compulsory**: it is stored as written and shown in the history. It needs the **administrator**permission, because giving points away is giving money away and is not a counter task.

The balance cannot go below zero: if you try to take away more than they have, Turnera says so and does nothing.

### Alerts

An alert is **what to keep in mind when booking or charging** that customer: «allergic to nickel, no Indiba». It is written in their record, under *Alerts*, and from then on it **shows up by itself** right at the top when you open one of their appointments and when you charge them at the till.

**An alert is not a note.** A note is a diary: it is read when somebody opens it, which is exactly what does not happen with the phone in your hand giving an appointment. An alert is short, it is live or retired, and it puts itself in front of you without anybody looking for it. That is why they are two different things in two different places.

It shows as a red banner, not as a message you close. That is on purpose: what is dismissed with a click is dismissed without reading it, and a real alert has to stay there while the treatment is being chosen, which is the moment it matters.

It is written and retired by whoever can edit the record; **it is also seen by whoever only has the agenda**, because otherwise the alert never reaches the person booking and is good for nothing. *Withdraw* does not delete: the alert stops showing up and who retired it and when stays on record.

**No diagnoses here.** The whole counter sees it. An alert carries what is needed to give an appointment or to charge; the clinical part goes to the history, which has its own permission and leaves a trace of who opens it.

### Duplicate records

It happens in every centre: the same person added twice, with the surname misspelt one of the times. Turnera lets you **merge** the two records, and in doing so everything from the other one is carried over to the good one: appointments, visits, vouchers and clinical record. Nothing is lost. The duplicate's photo passes to the good one if it had none; if both had one, the good one's is kept.

**A merge cannot be undone.** It is done from the record you want to keep: press **Merge** and search for the duplicate, which is the one that gets deleted. Before confirming, the window shows in green which one stays and in red which one is removed. To confirm you have to **type in by hand the code** of the record that will be deleted (or its name, if it has no code); pasting does not work, so that nobody confirms without having read it.

## 4. Charging

### The point of sale

Menu → Point of sale

You choose the client and the professional, and from there products, vouchers and packages are added to the ticket, in the three tabs at the top: *Products*, *Vouchers* and *Packages*. Services are not searched for in the POS: they come with the appointment, when you press *Charge* in the agenda. In *Products* only those that are sold appear; what the centre uses up in sessions (consumables) does not appear. The payment method comes from the list of those the centre has active.

**Back to the appointment.** If the ticket comes from an appointment, the ticket card has at the top the button *Back to the appointment*, which goes to the agenda and opens that same appointment, in case something needs changing before charging.

**The discount can be set in two ways.** With *%*, as a percentage: the most used ones (5, 10, 15 and 20 %) are one tap away, and any other can be typed. With *Final price* you say how much you want to charge —“make it 50”— and Turnera works out the discount and spreads it across the lines the customer pays; underneath it shows how much is taken off and what percentage that is, and the total appears with the previous price struck through. It also offers, one tap away, the round prices just below (with €57.30, 55 and 50). The final price only lowers: if you type one higher than the total, it warns you and does not let you charge.

**On the ticket each line shows its own price.** A discount or a reduction on the whole bill goes on a separate line, *Discount*, and below it the total charged; the same when collecting a debt. Internally the reduction is in fact spread across the lines, in proportion to their price: that way each practitioner's production, the refund of a single line and the invoice match what went into the till.

**A promotion on one service goes on its line.** Each line of the receipt (service, product, voucher or package) has its own *Disc.* box: 20% off the groin only discounts the groin, and the underarms stay at full price. The receipt discount, if any, is applied afterwards to what is left. On the receipt and the invoice the line shows its total discount: 20% from the promotion and 10% from the receipt show as 28%.

**The short way is from the agenda.** Open the appointment and click *Charge*: the point of sale opens with the customer, the member of staff and that appointment's services already filled in. All you have to say is how they pay. And once charged, the appointment is marked as paid — that is the euro icon on the grid.

**By default, only what was done is charged.** Until the appointment is saved as *Seen*, the button *Charge* button on the appointment cannot be pressed and the appointment does not show in *To charge* at the point of sale: that way the till and the agenda count the same and nothing is charged that was not done. If your centre charges before the service, turn it off in *Administration → Preferences → Payments and loyalty → Charge only what was done*. In classes each attendee counts: they are charged once it is recorded that they came.

**An appointment is not marked as attended before its start time.** Until then, the button *Seen* cannot be pressed (and in a class, neither can marking that someone came). This way an appointment on another day is not charged by mistake as attended. Once the start time has passed, it can, even if the appointment has not finished yet. It goes with the previous setting: if *Charge only what was done* is switched off, an appointment can be marked as attended at any time.

**If the appointment changes with the ticket half done** —you go back to the agenda and add a service that was done in the end—, on returning to the POS a notice shows what the appointment has now and two buttons: *Update the ticket* adds what is new (and removes what was removed from the appointment), and *Keep it as it is* changes nothing. It is not updated on its own because what was removed or added at the counter may be on purpose.

**And shorter still: the top of the POS.** As soon as a professional marks an appointment as *Attended*, the customer appears at the top, under *To charge*, before they reach the counter. Below are the ones still *In progress*, who are next (with *Charge only what was done* switched on, these are shown but not charged until they have been seen). Click the card and the sale loads just as from the agenda. The list refreshes itself every few seconds, only shows today's appointments and each customer disappears from it as soon as they pay or the amount is left owing.

**Several appointments on the same day, a single ticket.** If the customer has come for more than one treatment today —with the same practitioner or different ones— there is no need to charge them one by one. At the top, in *To charge*, a single card appears with “2 appointments”, “3 appointments”…; when you press it the ticket is built with all of them, in one sale and with one total. Above the lines the list *Today's appointments*: only those already **marked as attended** are added to the ticket, and they come in on their own. The rest — still in the room, not started, or past but nobody marked them as attended — are shown unticked and cannot be added: to charge them here, go back to the appointment and set it as attended. Untick the one you do not want to charge now — it stays waiting above for when the client comes out — or mark another as attended to add it.

*[Captura] The customer's appointments for today: the ticked ones go on the ticket, each service with the practitioner who did it.*

Each service is recorded **in the name of whoever did it** (the line says *Done by: …*), so each practitioner's production comes out right even when everything is charged together. The practitioner chosen at the top is the one who sells anything else that is added, such as a product. The discount on each line, the ticket discount and the split of the payment work just as with a single appointment. If any of the appointments is missing a required equipment reading, the charge waits: record it, or untick that appointment. Only appointments from **today** that have no class place and have not yet been charged are joined together; those from earlier days are charged from *Unpaid visits*.

- **Take payment now** ticked: you choose the payment method and the money goes into today's till.
- **Take payment now** unticked: the visit is left as a debt and shows up in *Unpaid visits*. As nothing is paid today, the payment method, the split and the amount handed over disappear, and the button changes to *Leave as debt*. The payment method is chosen on the day it is paid.
- **Tendered**: type in what you are handed and the screen works out the change.

**Vouchers and payment method.** If the customer has vouchers that pay for part of the ticket, the payment method shows as *Voucher*. Choosing *Card* or *Cash* does not remove the vouchers: the vouchers keep paying for their sessions and that payment method is for the rest, as the amount shown below says. To avoid using a voucher for a session, untick it on its line.

**Gift.** When you choose the payment method *Gift* (or any other with its own discount), the ticket discount is set to 100 % by itself and the total is zero, which is what gets recorded. If another payment method is chosen afterwards, the discount goes back to zero.

**Unsigned consents.** If the customer still has a consent to sign, the till warns about it when charging and marks it in «To charge». It does not stop the charge. The notice is switched off and on in *Administration → Preferences → Payments and loyalty → Consents at the till*; it comes switched on.

A product **with no units cannot be sold**: the till does not let you add it to the ticket, nor more units than are left in the centre, and says how many there are. To sell it, first record a stock entry. If the centre sells what it has on the counter even if Turnera has not recorded it, switch on *Administration → Preferences → Payments and loyalty → Sell without units*; then the unit comes out of stock —which stays at zero, never negative—, the output is recorded and the stocktake later sets the right figure. When the sale is refunded or cancelled, the unit comes back.

The *Print quote* directly opens the print dialog with what is on the ticket, without charging anything, to give to someone who is thinking it over; if you prefer to save it, choose *Save as PDF* in that same dialog. *Quote by WhatsApp* opens WhatsApp with a short message (just the centre's name, no amounts) and, if the browser allows it, attaches the PDF; if not, you have to attach it by hand after saving it from the print dialog.

**Look things up without losing the ticket.** The icon *i* of each product, voucher or package in the search box, and the name of each ticket line, open its sheet in a panel on the right: price, VAT, duration, code, units left at this centre… Close it and the ticket stays as it was. And if you need to leave the POS —to the client’s record, to the agenda—, **the half-finished ticket stays saved on that workstation**: when you come back it appears as it was, with the notice *The ticket has been recovered* and the button *Discard it* to start a clean one. It belongs to each user and each centre, it is deleted when the sale is charged and it expires after twelve hours.

### Products and services

Menu → Catalogue → Products / Services

When creating or correcting a product or a service, the **category** and the **brand** are chosen from a list that filters as you type, so you use the one that already exists. If it is not there, type it and it is created («New: will be added…»). If you type one that already exists with different capitals or accents, the existing one is kept, so there are never two almost identical ones.

In a product's record, whoever manages the store or sees the stock movements report also has its **ins and outs** in this branch, latest first: date and time, whether it comes in or goes out, how many units, and whether it was a sale or a manual movement.

When recording a movement by hand (in, out or adjustment) you can write the **reason**, which is optional: a product that arrived broken, a retail one moved to the treatment room. In the product's ins and outs and in the movements report, lines with a reason carry a comment icon on the left, like the notes in the customer history; tapping it opens a dialog with the text and who wrote it. An adjustment that does not change the units leaves no movement, so it does not keep the reason either.

In the list in **Stock**, products with any movement that has a reason carry the same icon next to their name: pressing it shows their latest movements with their reason. And the stock-in, stock-out or adjustment dialog shows the product's last five movements underneath, so you can see what was done before and why. The reason cannot be changed or deleted: it is the trail of the movement.

### Restocking

Menu → Catalogue → Restocking

Lists, by supplier, the products that are **below their minimum** in this branch (the minimum is the one on the product record). Only the suppliers ticked in **Restocking settings**are shown, which is also where you set the **address for draft orders**. For each product you say how many units to order; if you do not, the ones missing up to the minimum. **Prepare the draft** sends the supplier's order, with its email address, to that address so you can forward it; nothing reaches the supplier directly. Afterwards the draft is marked as **Sent to the supplier** or you **Discard** it (for example, because that material is provided by the public authority and is not bought). While a product is in an open draft it is not proposed again. It is seen by whoever sees the stock; preparing drafts and changing the settings is for whoever moves stock.

### Packages

Menu → Catalogue → Packages

A package is a closed price for several things together: “aloe vera moisturiser + leg waxing, 50 €”. It can carry services, products or both, and each line with as many units as it needs.

**The price of the package is optional.** Left blank, the package is worth whatever its parts add up to, and goes up by itself the day a service goes up. Filled in, the package is an offer: the difference is split across its lines in proportion to what each one costs.

**Why it is split and not left on one line.** The package goes onto the ticket as the lines it carries inside, not as a single one. That way each one takes its own VAT to the accounting draft, and a voucher can pay for the waxing while the cream is charged. If the discount stayed whole on one line, the VAT and the till would come out wrong.

With the example above: a 24.20 € cream and a 30.00 € waxing, package at 50 €. The cream ends up at 22.32 € and the waxing at 27.68 €, which add up to the 50 that are charged. And if a voucher covers the waxing, what is not paid is those 27.68 €, not the 30 of the tariff.

To sell one, the point of sale has a third tab, *Packages*, next to *Products* and *Vouchers*. It is added like everything else and appears on the ticket with its name; the voucher checkbox behaves as on a single service, in case the client prefers to pay for it and keep the session.

**Sale dates.** A package can be an offer with dates: *On sale from* and *On sale until (expiry)*, both optional. Outside them it does not appear in the point of sale and cannot be charged; on the last day it can still be sold, and what has already been sold does not change. In the package list, the status says *Expired* or *Not on sale yet*, with its dates underneath.

A package is not deleted: it is *taken off*. It stops being offered at the point of sale, and the tickets that already carry it stay as they are.

A service or a product is not deleted either: it is **deactivated**, and whatever was sold with it stays as it was. But it cannot be retired while something still uses it: a **package** on sale that includes it, a **voucher** on sale of its same group (the service is where it is spent) or, for a service, **appointments from today on**. Turnera says which ones, and it has to be removed from them (or those appointments changed or cancelled) first.

### Payment methods with a fixed discount

Some payment methods come with the discount built in. **Gift** is a hundred per cent: choose it and the total goes to zero by itself. There is nothing to remember, and you cannot charge by mistake for something you are giving away.

The payment methods the POS offers are cash, card, **Bizum**, bank transfer, financing, gift and gift card, and, when relevant, club points and the customer's credit balance. These last two only appear if there is a customer with a balance.

**Bizum** is recorded by hand: the customer sends it from their phone and, when it is seen arriving, it is charged with that payment method. It earns club points like a card and appears on its own line of the cash closing, which is checked against the bank statement.

### Coupons

Menu → Catalogue → Coupons

A coupon is a code that takes money off the ticket: **SUMMER25**, **WELCOME**. It is handed out on a flyer or online, the customer says it at the counter and it is typed in when charging. It can take off **euros** — 10 € off — or a **percentage** — 15% of the whole ticket —, and it takes whatever limits are needed: from when it is valid, until when, how many times in total, and whether the same person may use it more than once.

When charging, the code goes in the **Coupon** box of the ticket, above the discount. It does not matter how it is typed: it is always stored in capitals. If the coupon does not work, the screen says why, because the cases are not the same: it does not exist, it has not started yet, its date has passed, every use is gone, or that customer already used it.

**The discount is shared between the lines, it does not stay on one.** Just like with packages: a 10 € coupon on a 42.50 € ticket leaves the 30 € service at 22.94 and the 12.50 product at 9.56. That way each line takes its own VAT to the accounting draft and the day's till adds up.

A 10 € coupon on a 6 € ticket takes off 6: the centre does not give the difference back. And on the listing, each coupon says how many times it has been used and **how much money has been given away with it**, which is the answer to “what did that campaign cost us”.

A coupon is not deleted: it is **taken off**. It stops working at the counter and what was already taken off with it stays where it was. Seeing the coupons and creating them are two different permissions: reception sees them, management invents them.

**If a charge does not go through, the notice says why and what to do.** For example, that the customer has no voucher sessions left for the whole ticket and the rest has to be charged another way, or that the business line for sales has not been chosen in *Administration → Preferences → The centre*. If it is a server failure, the notice carries a **reference** of eight letters and digits: with it, support finds exactly what happened.

### Promotions

Menu → Catalogue → Promotions

A promotion is a **phrase that the point of sale reminds the cashier of** while it lasts: “20% off groin and underarms until the end of the month”, “second pressotherapy session at half price”. It is created with the phrase and two dates, **From** and **To**, both included. While it is running, it appears at the top of the point of sale in a blue box, so that nobody forgets to apply it.

The promotion **does not discount anything by itself**: it is a reminder. The discount is set on the line of the service, product or voucher with the line percentage, or on the whole receipt, as always. The list marks each promotion as *Running*, *Upcoming* or *Ended*; ended ones stay as history, and can be edited or deleted if they are no longer wanted.

Managing them requires the permission “Manage promotions”, which by default Management has (and the administrator, who has everything). Seeing the reminder at the point of sale needs nothing more than being able to charge. It is a module of its own, **Promotions**, switched on by default in new centres; it needs Till.

### Paying with a voucher

If the customer holds a voucher with sessions left, the till applies it by itself. Every service on the ticket that belongs to that voucher's group comes out at **0,00 €** with the mark *Voucher: covers 1 of 1*, and the rest of the ticket (services from other groups, products) is paid today with whichever payment method you choose. The totals show the line *Covered by vouchers* with what the customer has saved.

The payment method is already set to **Voucher**. If the voucher pays for everything, there is nothing else to choose: it is charged and the session is deducted. If something is left to pay, below appears *The rest is paid with* to choose how. **If the customer prefers to pay and keep the session**, choose another payment method instead of Voucher: the whole ticket goes back to its price and the voucher is not touched.

**To keep the session of a single service** and use it on the others, untick that line's box: it goes back to its price and the voucher is not touched. It is useful for the customer who wants to keep the sessions they have left, or who is paying for someone else today.

When there are several services from the same group and the voucher does not stretch to all of them, it covers the most expensive ones first. And if the visit is left as a debt, the sessions are not spent until it is paid: the voucher stays whole on the customer's record in the meantime.

**The session and the voucher on the same ticket.** The customer has a laser session and on the way out buys a laser voucher: add both to the ticket. The session comes out of the voucher being bought, at €0.00, and only the voucher is charged, which starts with that session already used. If she already had another voucher of the same group with sessions left, the session comes out of that one, because **the oldest voucher is always used first**; the new one stays whole. It also works when left as a debt: the session comes out of the voucher just the same and what is owed is the voucher. Whatever was already charged on another ticket does not change when the voucher is bought later.

To refund that voucher you also have to refund the session that came out of it, in the same refund: the session goes back to the voucher and the voucher, whole again, is withdrawn. A voucher with used sessions is not refunded on its own.

On the receipt every line paid with a voucher comes out as *(voucher session)*, and the day's cash only counts the money that has really come in.

### Paying by two methods at once

The bill is a hundred euros, the customer has fifty in cash and wants to put the rest on a card. Tick **“Split the payment across several payment methods”** and two lines appear: on each one you pick the method and how much goes on it. You can add more.

*[Captura] The split keeps the count: what is collected, what has been entered and what is left, and the gift card balance.*

While splitting, **the POS does the sums for whoever is charging**. Below the lines you see three figures — what there is *To collect*, what *Entered* so far and what *Left to enter*— and they change with every amount you type, so there is no need to subtract in your head. What is left shows in orange while something is missing; when the split adds up exactly, it turns to *Adds up*, in green; and if it goes over, it says *Over by* and by how much, in red. **As long as it does not add up to the cent, the charge button does not light up.** This is deliberate: a split that does not add up is a cash discrepancy that shows up weeks later and that nobody remembers.

**One of the methods can be a gift card.** When you choose *Gift card* on one of the lines, the POS shows the customer's cards at a touch (or you type the code) and says how much is left on it: *“Right now the gift card has €100.00 left”*, and below, what will be left after this payment. The card pays only the part it has been given; if its balance falls short, it says so and asks you to lower that part and pay the rest another way.

The ticket comes out with the breakdown: how much went on each one. And the end-of-day till splits that payment between the two methods, it does not put it all into one.

### Charge only part now

The customer pays for something big and leaves a small service because they do not have enough cash or their card limit does not allow it. Instead of leaving everything owing, tick **“Charge only part now”**, next to *Take payment now*, and type the amount to be charged. Below you will see the summary: *so many euros are charged now and so many are left owing*. The amount has to be greater than zero and less than the total; until it is, the charge button stays off.

That part can be split between several payment methods, as in the previous section, but **the split has to add up to the amount charged now, not the total**. The cash handed over and the change are calculated on the part paid in cash. A part payment only accepts payment methods that bring in money: it cannot be combined with leaving the sale owing, with payment by link, with the gift card, with credit balance or with payment methods that carry their own discount, and it does not spend vouchers either. Those options switch themselves off, with a note explaining why. When charging, the confirmation says how much is left owing, and the sale becomes a debt of *Unpaid visits* for that amount.

### Unpaid visits

*[Captura] The visits that were seen without paying. Tapping one opens the payment dialog.*

Menu → Unpaid visits

Here are the visits that were seen without being paid for. Click one and the payment dialog opens: you pick the payment method, adjust the discount if need be and settle it. Afterwards you can print the ticket.

**In the menu, next to *Unpaid visits*, you can see how many of today's are still unpaid** in the active delegation: a payment left half done that is worth picking up before closing the day. With none, no number shows. It updates itself when a payment is taken or left as a debt at any terminal.

**When it is settled, Turnera checks again whether the customer has a voucher**, because they may have bought one after leaving it owed. If a voucher covers any line, the payment method shows *Voucher*: those lines go to zero, labelled *Voucher*, and the session is deducted on settling. If anything else is left, you choose how the rest is paid. If the voucher pays for everything, there is nothing to charge and no ticket to print: only the session is used up. And if the customer prefers to pay and keep the session, choose any other payment method and the voucher is left untouched.

**A debt can be settled in one or several payments.** In the payment dialog, the field *Amount to charge* appears with everything that is pending; if you type less, what is recorded is a **part payment** and the button now says *Charge* followed by the amount. The debt stays in the list with what is left to pay and, below, what is *paid on account*; with the button *View part payments* you can see the date, payment method and amount of each one. The customer record and the home panel always count what is left to collect, with part payments already deducted.

**The discount and vouchers do not apply to a part payment: the discount is applied when paying the rest**, on what is left to collect. An account that already has part payments does not accept a voucher either, and what is left is paid in money. The invoice is still a single one, for the sale total: part payments do not generate new invoices, and the invoice and points follow their usual course when the whole account is settled.

**Splitting the payment works the same as in the POS.** When settling a whole debt you can tick *Split the payment between several methods*: the same lines, the same three figures that update by themselves, the same *Adds up*, and one of the methods can be a gift card with its balance in view. It is offered when the debt is settled in one go: with payments already made, or if one of the parts is the payment link, credit balance, club points, a voucher or a method with a fixed discount, it is not allowed and those options appear switched off with the reason.

*[Captura] Collecting a split debt: the same split and the same figures as in the POS.*

### Pay in instalments

Menu → Payment plans

When a customer cannot pay all at once, the debt can be deferred in monthly instalments, **with no interest**, and followed on one screen to see whether each instalment is paid on time. It is a centre option and **comes switched off**: if your centre does not offer instalments, leave it that way and you will see none of this. It is switched on in Administration → Preferences, group *Invoicing*, with the checkbox **“Allow payments in instalments”** (you must be able to change the centre's preferences).

**Creating the plan.** There are two places. At the point of sale, when a sale is left owing, it offers *Pay the rest in instalments*; in *Unpaid visits*, each debt has the button *Pay in instalments*. You enter the number of instalments (up to 60) and the date of the first, and Turnera divides them equally, with the last one absorbing any leftover cents. The dates can be changed one by one.

*[Captura] When a sale is left owing at the point of sale, the rest can be paid in instalments.*

*[Captura] The number of instalments and the date of the first; the instalments come out calculated and their dates can be changed.*

**Part now, the rest in instalments.** If the customer leaves a down payment, it is charged first as a partial charge or a part payment, and the plan covers only what is left. An account that already has part payments also accepts a plan: only the rest is deferred.

**Collecting the instalments.** They are collected from *Unpaid visits*, like any part payment. The debt shows its status (*Up to date*, *Due soon* or *Overdue*) and the link *View plan*. What is collected goes to the **oldest instalment still due**. If the customer pays more than that instalment asks for, the surplus **pays off the last ones early**: the plan is shortened from the end and the following instalments do not change in amount. If everything outstanding is collected at once, the plan is completed.

*[Captura] A debt with a plan shows its status and the link to see the instalments.*

**The Payment plans screen** gathers all the plans. It can be filtered by status (in progress, overdue, due soon, up to date, completed or cancelled) and by customer. Opening a plan shows its instalments: how much of each has been collected, whether it is *paid*, *paid early* (brought forward with a larger payment), *next*, *overdue* or *pending*, and how many days late the payment was.

*[Captura] All the plans, showing who is up to date and who has fallen behind.*

*[Captura] A plan's detail: each instalment with what has been collected, its status and the delay.*

From the detail you can **change the date of an instalment** (it must stay between the previous one and the next) and **cancel the plan**: the debt stays pending with what has already been collected and can be deferred again. The amount of the instalments cannot be edited once the plan is created. Turnera does not notify the customer or block anything when an instalment falls due: it only shows it, so that the centre decides what to do.

**On Home** there is a card, *Overdue instalments*, with the instalments whose date has passed without being collected. It is added from *Customise*, like the others.

*[Captura] The Overdue instalments card, to see at a glance who has fallen behind.*

**Who can.** There are two permissions, both by default only for *Management* and *Till*: **See the payment plans** (the screen, the follow-up and the Home card) and **Manage the payment plans** (create plans, change dates and cancel). If you switch the option off while plans are in progress, those plans are kept and can still be seen until they finish; you just can no longer create new ones.

### Refunding a ticket

Customer record → History → Refund

The customer comes back with the cream unopened, or a service was charged that in the end was not done. In the history of their record, every paid ticket has the button **Refund**. The ticket opens with its lines and you tick the ones being refunded: **there is no need to refund all of it**. Whatever was already refunded shows as *Refunded* and cannot be ticked again.

Then you choose how it is refunded:

- **As money**, the way it came in: what was paid in cash leaves the till and what was paid by card goes back to the card.
- **As store credit**: the money stays in the house and the customer spends it another day. The next time they are charged, *Store credit* shows up as one more payment method.

If the ticket was paid split between two payment methods, the refund goes back split the same way, in the same proportion: from a ticket paid almost entirely by card, almost everything goes back to the card and only what came in as cash leaves the till.

What was paid with points always goes back as points, and what was paid with store credit goes back to the credit: refunding them does not turn them into money. You can write the reason, and it is worth doing.

**Refunding undoes everything the sale moved.** The product goes back to stock (unless it comes back opened or broken: when the line is ticked, the box *Back to stock*appears, and if it is unticked the money is refunded but the product is not restocked), the session a voucher paid goes back to that voucher, and the customer loses the points the purchase gave them (if they already spent them, they lose whatever they have left, never going negative). If what is refunded is a voucher that was sold, the voucher disappears from their record, but only if it is untouched: with one that has sessions used it is not known which part to refund, and the application does not allow it.

**The original ticket is not touched.** The refund is a new ticket, with the lines in negative, marked in the history as *Refund of the* ticket it undoes; in the original, the refunded lines carry the mark *refunded*, and once none are left to refund it no longer offers the button. That way the day's takings match the cash: what left today is taken off today, not on the day of the sale. And a coupon used in the sale is not recovered.

The ticket of a refund prints like any other, from the history, and says at the top *Refund of ticket* and the number of the one it undoes. A product that did not go back to stock has it noted on its line.

If the customer has store credit, their record warns about it at the top, and the till says how much is left when choosing the payment method. If the credit does not cover the whole bill, split the charge: one part with the credit and the rest with another payment method. Refunding is a separate permission, *Refunding a ticket*, which out of the box only the management role has.

### Gift cards

Menu → Gift cards

Someone pays today and another person spends it another day. It is sold with **Sell a gift card**: who pays for it, who sells it, the amount, the payment method and, if you like, who it is for, a dedication and how long it is valid (with no date, it does not expire). When it is charged you get a code like `RG-7K2M-Q9TX`, which is printed on a card to give as a gift (*Print* directly opens the print dialog) or sent by email with the card as a PDF. *Send by WhatsApp* opens WhatsApp with the centre, the code and the amount (unless the card was sold with the amount hidden) and attaches the PDF if the browser allows it.

To find a customer's cards, choose the customer above the list as in the other screens, by name, code or phone, and only the cards they bought are shown. Next to it, a text box searches by card code or by who the card is for: the person who receives it has no linked file, so they are found by name.

Whoever has the permission **Correct and withdraw gift cards** (out of the box, Management) sees two more buttons on each card. *Correct* fixes what was entered wrongly when it was sold —almost always the expiry date—, the recipient, the email, the message or whether the amount is shown; the amount and the balance are not touched. *Withdraw* leaves it with no value; it does not give the money back: for that there is *Refund* on the card's sale. Both actions are recorded with who and when.

*[Captura] Selling a gift card. The box at the bottom hides the amount in the email and on the printed card.*

If the gift is a surprise and you do not want what it cost to be known, tick **“Do not show the amount in the email or on the printed card”**only the dedication and the code will appear, without saying how much it is worth. The balance remains the same for the centre, and when it is spent the POS shows it as usual.

The **email** arrives laid out like a gift: with the centre's logo, the dedication in its own box in a letter-style typeface, the code clearly visible on a coupon and, below, how to use it and until when it is valid. If no dedication was written, the box does not appear.

After selling it you see the code with a large button, **Copy code**: press it and the code is placed on the clipboard, ready to paste into a message or an email. The button turns green with a tick and says *Code copied*, so you can see it worked.

*[Captura] The code of the card just sold, with the button to copy it.*

*[Captura] Once pressed it turns green: the code is already copied.*

The **email to send the card to** is separate, and it already comes filled in with the payer’s if their record has one. That is the usual choice: this way they decide when to give it, and nobody receives the present too early. It can be changed to the recipient’s, and then it reaches them straight away. The email is written for whoever receives it: to the buyer it says “here is the gift card for…”, and to the recipient, that they have a card to spend at the centre.

**The money comes in the day it is sold, not the day it is spent.** The sale is a ticket charged like any other and counts in that day's takings. Spending it brings nothing in: in the cash closing it shows up separately, as *Gift card*, just like a voucher. That is why a gift card cannot be paid for with points or with another gift card.

**Yes, you can pay with credit** that a refund left, as long as it covers the whole card: it is money that already went into the till and was given back to the customer as credit. It appears under the payment method, with the amount available, and comes out of their credit. It lets you redo a sale: sell the card, refund it leaving the amount as credit, and sell another one paying with that credit.

To spend it, choose **Gift card** as the payment method and type the code; below it shows what is left on it: *“Right now the gift card has €100.00 left”*. It is spent over several times until used up, and if it does not cover the whole bill the payment is split: one part with the card and the rest another way, with the sums of what is left done by the POS itself (see Paying by two methods at once). A debt can also be settled with it, split or in full.

At the top of the list is what is **still to be spent**: what is left on all the cards that are still valid. It is money the centre has already charged and owes in services and products, and it is what has to be kept in mind when closing the year. Each card says whether it is valid, spent, expired or refunded.

If something paid with a gift card is refunded, the money goes back to the card. And the sale of a card can be refunded from the buyer's history while it is untouched: it then stops being valid. Seeing the cards and selling them are two permissions; out of the box reception and management have them.

**Resend:** on each card in the list, the button *Resend* asks for the email address to send it to (the card’s delivery address appears, and it can be changed) and whether the amount should be shown. That choice applies only to that sending: the card stays as it was sold.

### Invoices and VeriFactu

Administration → Invoicing and VeriFactu

Turnera can invoice every sale the way VeriFactu requires. **It comes switched off**: until it is turned on, charging works as always and the ticket carries no invoice number. It is turned on by whoever holds the permission *Invoicing and VeriFactu*, which by default only the administrator has, and first the tax ID, company name and registered address (street, postcode and town) of the centre are needed in *Preferences*: the invoice has to say who issues it and where.

With invoicing on, every paid sale comes with its **simplified invoice**, which is the usual ticket with a number, issue date, the VAT breakdown and, at the top left, the QR code with “QR tributario:” above it. Its lines and total are those of the invoice; what the ticket carries and is not invoiced (a session paid with a voucher, the sale of a gift card) appears separately, under *Not included in this invoice*, saying why. A corrective invoice says which invoice it corrects and the reason. Once the centre sends its invoices to the AEAT, “VERI*FACTU” appears below the QR and “Factura verificable en la sede electrónica de la AEAT” next to it; while it does not send them, they do not, because it would not be true. Without invoicing switched on there is no invoice and no QR: the usual ticket is printed. **The invoice goes with the sale, not with the payment**: what is left owing is invoiced right then all the same, and collecting it is the centre's business. If the amount changes when the debt is settled —a discount of the payment method, or a voucher that takes over a session—, the difference goes into a corrective invoice. A refund produces a **corrective invoice**, in negative, stating which invoice it corrects. Sessions paid with a voucher are not invoiced (the voucher was invoiced when sold, with its VAT), nor is the sale of a gift card, which is money on account: what is bought with it is invoiced. What is paid with club points counts as a discount: the invoice is for what is paid with money.

**In production, an issued invoice is never deleted or changed.** If something is wrong, it is refunded and its corrective invoice is issued. And the VAT of every service and product must be decided in the catalogue: without it, the sale cannot be charged and Turnera says what is missing.

Each delegation numbers in three series: simplified, full and corrective. The number is the prefix, the year and a counter that starts again every year, such as `S1-2027/000123`. The centre chooses the prefix on the same screen; while the series has no invoices it can be changed, and after that changing it opens a new series.

If the customer asks for a **full invoice** with their tax ID, it is done from their history, with the *Full invoice* button of the ticket: enter their tax ID, name and address, and an invoice is issued that replaces the ticket. It needs the permission *Hacer factura completa (issue a full invoice)*, which out of the box front desk and management have.

Every invoice is in *Reports → Invoice book* (permission *Libro de facturas (invoice book)*), with how each one stands at the Spanish tax agency. From there the book is downloaded in Excel with the daily summary entry, which is what the accountant asks for every quarter.

### Test mode and moving to production

Administration → Invoicing and VeriFactu → Invoicing mode

Invoicing has two modes, and every centre starts in **test mode**. It is for trying it out calmly before invoicing for real: charging, refunding, issuing a full invoice, looking at the book and downloading the Excel, without fear of breaking anything.

- In test mode, every invoice carries at the top, in red, *FACTURA DE PRUEBA · SIN VALIDEZ FISCAL (test invoice, not valid for tax purposes)*. It is not valid for the tax agency or the customer.
- They are numbered like real ones, but that numbering is provisional.
- In the invoice book they carry the label *Test*, and the Excel says so in the name of its sheets.
- While the centre is in test mode there is a notice at the top of the screen for whoever handles invoicing.
- If sending is on, records go to the AEAT test environment, never to the real one.

When the centre is going to invoice for real, with the catalogue VAT reviewed with its accountant, press **Move to production…**. The screen explains what will happen and asks you to tick that you understand and to type `PRODUCCIÓN`. On confirming:

1. Every test invoice is deleted for good, with its records. Sales and payments are not touched.
2. Every series of every delegation starts again: the next invoice is number 1.
3. From then on every invoice is real and cannot be deleted or changed, only corrected.

**Moving to production cannot be undone.** There is no way back to test mode: the screen shows since when the centre has been in production and who took the step. It needs its own permission, *Pasar la facturación a producción (move invoicing to production)*, which out of the box only the administrator has.

### Sending to the AEAT

Administration → Invoicing and VeriFactu → Sending to the AEAT

**Send** means that Turnera sends the Spanish tax agency, on its own and as soon as each invoice is issued, a copy of its data: what the rules call an «invoicing record». Nobody has to do anything invoice by invoice.

- **What is sent:** the number, the date, the kind of invoice, the description of what was sold, the VAT breakdown and the total; for a full invoice, also the customer's name and tax ID.
- **What is not sent:** the invoice PDF, or anything from the customer's file or clinical record.
- That way the AEAT keeps its own copy, and whoever holds the invoice can check it by scanning its QR.
- While sending is off, records are kept in Turnera in order and, when it is turned on, they are all sent, oldest first: none is lost.

Sending is a second switch, also off. Turning it on needs two things, and **no certificate has to be uploaded**:

1. **Authorise DEVEL before the AEAT**, done by the centre once: with the centre's certificate, on the tax agency's website, an authorisation (apoderamiento) in favour of DEVEL is registered to send invoicing records. It is only good for that, and the centre can withdraw it whenever it wants. Once done, tell support through *Report an incident* and we mark it on that screen.
2. **Turnera's connection with the AEAT**, prepared by DEVEL: it is our certificate, with which Turnera identifies itself when sending on the centre's behalf. It is installed once on the server.

The screen shows how each of the two stands, and the switch cannot be turned on until both are ready. If the AEAT rejects a record or they get stuck, a warning appears at the top of the screen for whoever handles invoicing, and it reaches our support. If the AEAT says DEVEL is no longer authorised, the authorisation goes back to *Pending* and sending stops until it is fixed, without losing anything.

## 5. Vouchers

A voucher is sessions paid in advance that are valid for the services of a **rate group** or for **a single service**. They are sold in two ways:

- **When selling a service marked as a voucher** (the usual way): the voucher is created by itself when it is charged at the point of sale.
- **By hand:** in the customer record, in the vouchers section, *New voucher* creates one without charging it, choosing the voucher from the price list: it brings its sessions and its expiry, which can be changed before saving. Only those with the permission Voucher management see it (by default, the administrator and Management); Reception does not.

### Where the sessions and validity are set

In *Catalogue → Services*, in the service sold as a voucher, tick **Sold as a voucher** and its **sessions** and its **validity**. In *Valid for* you choose whether the voucher pays for the services of a *Rate group* or a single service. The validity is given in two ways:

- **Months from purchase**: the voucher expires that number of months after the day it is sold.
- **Between two dates**, like a hotel: you pick the first and last day on the calendar, and the voucher can only be used between them, both included, whenever it is sold. It is meant for seasonal campaigns. If it is sold before the first date, the customer record shows *Usable from …* and the point of sale does not deduct it until that day.

### Vouchers for a single service

A voucher for one service (for example, “5 underarm sessions”) only pays for that service, even if the service is in a rate group with others. It is created from the service’s record with **New voucher for this service**, which leaves it ready for that service, or in any voucher by choosing *Valid for → A service*. Each service’s record shows under **Vouchers that pay for it** its own and those of its rate groups, and the services list can be filtered with *All*, *Services* or *Vouchers*.

### A service in several rate groups

A service can be in **several rate groups** at once: for example, in its usual one and in a promotion’s. A voucher of any of them pays for it. It can be done from either side:

- In *Catalogue → Services*, in the service’s record, the field **Rate groups** accepts several.
- On the rate groups screen, after choosing a group, **Add services** puts several in at once, and **Remove**, on each row, takes the service out of that group only.

A voucher, on the other hand, is still for a single rate group or a single service.

### How they are used up

A session is deducted **when paying at the till**, for every service on the ticket that belongs to the voucher's group and is left ticked as covered (see Paying with a voucher). Services from other groups and products on the same ticket are paid with the normal payment method. It can also be deducted by hand from the customer's record, for when someone was seen without going through the till.

**If a service can be paid both by its own voucher and by one of its rate group, its own is used first**, which is good for nothing else; the group one is kept for the other services.

**If the customer has several vouchers valid for the same service, the session comes off the one that expires soonest**, even if it was bought later, so that paid sessions are not lost. Those that do not expire go last, and if two expire on the same day, the one bought first is used. In the customer record the vouchers appear in that same order. At first the record only shows the vouchers that can still be used: to also see used-up or expired ones, tick the boxes above the list; each user finds them as they left them last time in that browser.

Deducting a session by hand, correcting a voucher and cancelling it require the permission Voucher management, which is not the one for charging: the default Reception sells vouchers and charges with them, but cannot touch those already sold. If in your centre you want someone to do it, give them that permission from the roles screen.

**Marking an appointment as seen does not use up the voucher.** The voucher is deducted when you charge, not when you see the customer. If in your centre people are seen on a voucher without going through the point of sale, someone has to deduct the session by hand from the record, or the balance will stop adding up.

On the agenda, the voucher icon on each appointment says how many sessions that customer has left. Vouchers that are used up or expired are not counted.

### Withdrawing a voucher

When it is withdrawn from the customer record, the voucher **can no longer be used** and disappears from the record and the POS, but it is not deleted: the tickets that charged it and the transfers still say which voucher they were for. If the money also has to be given back, it is done through the refund of the ticket that sold it.

### Transferring a voucher to another customer

Customer record → Vouchers → Transfer

Someone buys a voucher of ten sessions, uses three and moves away. The voucher can be passed to another customer of the centre: press *Transfer* on the voucher's line, look up whoever receives it and that is it. The reason is optional.

**You can also transfer just part of it.** In *Sessions to transfer* shows everything that is left at first; if it is lowered (for example, 3 of 7), those sessions go to a **new voucher** for the person receiving them, in the same group and with the same purchase and expiry dates, and the original voucher keeps the rest. Nothing is charged or invoiced again.

*[Captura] The transfer dialog. The notice at the top says how many sessions are moving and that there is no going back.*

**A transfer cannot be undone.** If you picked the wrong customer, you have to transfer it back the other way, and then both transfers will show. That is deliberate: the day someone asks why their voucher has fewer sessions than they bought, that trail is all there is to answer them with.

Every transfer records **from whom to whom, how many sessions were moved, who did it and the date and time** (and, if it was a part, the number of the new voucher). It appears in both records, in the block *Transferred vouchers*: on the one who gave it and on the one who received it.

The sessions that were left are stored exactly as they stood that day, because the voucher keeps being used afterwards and otherwise, a year from now, nobody would know how much whoever received it used up.

#### What cannot be done

- **An expired voucher cannot be transferred.** What is no longer good for whoever bought it is no good for whoever receives it either, and transferring it would be a quiet way of bringing it back to life. If you want to give it more time, correct the expiry date first.
- **Nor can a used-up one.** It has no sessions left to transfer.
- **Only between customers of the same centre.**

**A transfer does not move the sale.** Whoever paid for the voucher paid for it: nothing is invoiced again and nothing is credited. It is an assignment between customers, not a till operation, and it does not show up in the cash close.

It needs the *TRASPASAR BONOS*permission, which is not the same as the one for attendance used to sell and use up vouchers: changing the owner of a voucher already in use is not the same as charging for it, and not every receptionist has to be able to do it. It is granted in *Administration → Users*.

## 6. Equipment

Administration → Devices

A device is not a room. The ultrasound scanner is carried from one cubicle to another and the laser goes wherever it is needed, but **there is only one and only one person can use it at a time**. That is what this screen sorts out.

### Adding a device

*[Captura] The centre's equipment, with how many units there are and what gets written down each session.*

For each device you state:

- **The name** and **how many units** there are. With two, two appointments at once are fine and three are not.
- **Which services need it.** They are moved from the *Available* list to the *Use it* list, with a search box in both. Vouchers do not appear: they are sold, not given as treatments. Until one is chosen, the equipment gets in nobody's way.
- **What has to be recorded each time it is used**: the joules on the laser, the power on the sunbed. You add a parameter with “Add a parameter” and remove it with the bin on its row.

Each parameter that is recorded is one of three types:

| Type | What it asks for | When to use it |

| **Text** | Whatever you type | A handpiece, a short note |
| **List of options** | One of the options you type in, separated by commas | Areas, programmes, named intensities |

Marking a parameter as **compulsory** means the appointment will not let you save the session without it. The limits on the number are checked as you record too: if the laser runs from 1 to 40 joules, a 60 will not go in.

A device that a service uses is not deleted:

Turnera says which services use it and it has to be
removed from them first, so that no service is left asking for a device that is no longer there. Switching it off is allowed.

A device that has already been used is not deleted: it is switched off.

When you hit delete, if it has sessions
recorded, Turnera deactivates it instead of deleting it, stops checking it when booking and keeps the settings
each customer was treated with. For the same reason,

a parameter with recorded sessions cannot be removed

from the list:
on saving a warning appears and the device stays as it was. Switching the device off is also the way to carry on
booking while it is away being repaired.

*[Captura] Adding a device says which services need it and what has to be written down every time.*

### What changes on the agenda

If two appointments at the same time need the same piece of equipment and there are not units for both, Turnera **warns before saving the second one**, even if they are in different rooms and with different professionals. The warning says which piece of equipment it is and **which other appointment has it taken**: the time, the customer and the professional. With that in front of you, you decide: change the time, or save it anyway. It also warns when you **drag** an appointment to another time, when you **cut and paste it** on another day and when you **lengthen** it from its edge.

**Equipment warns; the room and the professional still block.** The difference is real: two appointments do not fit in the same room and one person is not in two places at once, but the laser is carried from one room to another and whoever is treating knows whether there is time. Turnera cannot know that, so it says so and lets you decide.

### Recording the session

Agenda → (an appointment) → Equipment for the session

On appointments that include a service with a device, below the appointment details there is a section with the parameters to record, **grouped by service and device**: each group says which service it is for (“Armpit laser · Diode laser”). If the appointment has two services with the same device, its parameters appear twice, once per service, so it is clear how many joules were used on each area. Next to each parameter, **how that customer was treated the previous time in that same service**. It is saved together with the appointment, when you press *Save*; a parameter left blank is understood as “not noted” and is not written. The parameters are asked for here, in the appointment, and not in the POS. If the appointment’s services are changed, the section updates by itself: the lines of the new service appear and those of the one removed disappear.

**The same service several times.** Some services are charged by units, such as laser *pulses* : each shot is one unit, with its own joules and its own session number. In the appointment's list of services, press **+** next to the service to add it again; each time it appears as its own group in *Equipment used in this session* («Laser pulses 1», «Laser pulses 2»…), with what was done last time in that same position, and the length of the appointment adds up the minutes of each one. At the till the pulses are put together in a single line with the units and the total. To remove one, double-click its line in the appointment's list.

**What is noted is not lost when leaving and coming back.** The values already entered, and those typed but not yet saved, stay in their boxes when you return to the appointment — for example after going to the customer's record and coming back with the return-to-appointment icon—, as well as showing in the *Before:* of each parameter. Nobody has to type them again.

If the appointment has no service with a device, the section does not appear.

**No charging without the required ones.** An appointment with required parameters left blank can be saved but not charged: when you press *Charge* it tells you which ones are missing, and the POS cannot be reached by any other route either until they are noted (whoever lacks permission to note them is warned, but can charge).

**Where you see it afterwards.** In the client's record, inside the visit history: under each service that carried parameters a small grey line appears with the device and the recorded values, for example *Diode laser · 18 J · 12 ms · spot 10* (those with a unit are read with it; those without, with their name in front). This is where you follow a laser treatment's settings session by session. Clicking the visit row opens the full detail: each parameter with its value and unit, who attended, when it was recorded and the line's notes. Required ones left unfilled are only seen there, as “—”.

If the service had no parameters, nothing is added. ## 7. The booking portal

This is the screen where the customer books by themselves, at `/reservas` on the centre's address. It is **switched off until the centre switches it on**, and it is switched on by delegation.

### How the customer logs in

There is no password. The customer types in their email address and receives a **six-digit code** that is good for fifteen minutes; the same message carries a link that fills it in by itself, so there is nothing to copy. That gets them in.

They are never told whether that address is or is not on anyone's record: the answer is the same in both cases. If it is not, the message simply does not arrive. That way nobody can work out who is a customer of the centre by trying addresses.

**With no email gateway set up, the portal does not work.** The code cannot leave the server and the customer is left waiting. It is the first thing to get set up before switching the portal on for anyone.

### What they see and what they do not

- Only the services and the rooms marked as bookable online.
- A service that is not given in a room **is not offered to them there**. At the front desk that is a warning; on the portal it is a wall, because the customer knows less than the program and a booking made wrongly is a phone call to undo it.
- Closed stretches do not appear.

### How far ahead bookings go, and with which rules

Administration → Preferences → Agenda and bookings

It is set per delegation, the one of the session, and applies to the portal and to integrations that book, such as a campaign platform:

- **Days ahead.** How far ahead the agenda is open: with 60, today you can book up to 60 days from now. The window moves forward on its own every day. If you leave it as it is, the agenda opens 30 days ahead.
- **Minimum notice.** With 2 hours, at ten the first slot offered is at twelve.
- **Cancellation window**, **open bookings per client**, **what happens without a voucher** and **when the portal closes to clients who owe**. These four apply only to the portal.

*[Captura] The rules for bookings from outside, in Preferences.*

The portal checkbox only opens or closes it to clients. Integrations that book keep working with the days ahead and the notice. None of these rules apply at the front desk.

### Balancing and closing the day's cash

Menu → Cash closing

At the end of each day, what is in the till and what has come in through the card terminal is balanced against what Turnera says has been charged. The entry screen is the **cash search**: one day per row, with what was charged, the difference, what was withdrawn and the status —*Closed*, *Open* or *Not closed*, which are the days on which something was charged and nobody closed the cash—. *Today's cash* opens today's; any other one, by clicking its row.

- **What Turnera has charged**, on the left: the total of each payment method. *See breakdown* shows the payments one by one —time, ticket, customer and amount—, to find the one that does not match.
- **Card**: you type what the card terminal receipts or the bank say, and the card difference appears.
- **Bizum and bank transfer** are neither in the drawer nor in the card terminal: they appear on their own line of what was collected, and are checked against the bank statement.
- **Cash**: the opening cash —what the previous closing left is suggested—, plus what was charged in cash, minus the *payments out of the till* (the courier, the florist), is what *should be in the till*. You type the count and the cash difference appears. What goes to the bank goes in *amount withdrawn*, and what is left is the opening cash of the next day.
- **Given away**: below the takings, a discreet line says how many lines were settled with the payment method *Gift* and what they were worth at list price. It is not money, so it does not go into the till or the discrepancy. *See breakdown* shows each line: time, ticket, client, item, professional and price. When the closing is printed, the line comes out with the total.

At the top, in large type, **the verdict**: in green if the cash balances, in red with how much is missing or over —negative means missing—, split between cash and card. It is recalculated with each figure you type, so you can keep checking until it balances. *Save the count* leaves the cash open to continue later; *Close till* fixes it and goes back to the list of closings, where it appears as closed; it requires having counted the cash and the card. Any closing in the list opens with a click to look it up. To correct it you have to reopen it, and who closed it and who reopened it is recorded.

Balancing and closing the cash is the permission **«Balance and close the cash»**, which Front desk and Management have. Reopening a closed cash is another one, **«Reopen a closed cash»**, which only Management has.

### The cancellations report

It counts against **the appointments in the range, not the ones cancelled in the range**. The difference matters: a March appointment cancelled in April is a March appointment that fell through.

At the top, **how many were cancelled and who called them off**: the customer, the centre or an external cause. The centre's are broken down by professional, and the external ones by cabin or court, which is where you see whether an outdoor court loses many more classes to the rain than a covered one. Those cancelled before this distinction existed appear as *not stated*. Those *created by mistake* are counted separately: they are not cancellations, but if that number grows, it is worth knowing why.

Then, **all the cancelled appointments one by one**, with the most recently cancelled at the top: the day, the customer, the staff member, when it was created, when it was cancelled, **the reason, who cancelled it and with which role** and the comments. To find out what happened to someone's appointment, type in **Customer** their number or part of their name and surnames and press *Apply*: only their cancelled appointments remain, and the ranking below no longer appears. Remember that the range is that of the **day of the appointment**: for an appointment next week cancelled today, the range has to reach next week.

With the **Cause** the list keeps only the ones cancelled for that reason and the ranking no longer appears. It is the way to review what was marked as a mistake.

Below, **which customers cancel the most**. Only what the customer calls off counts here: if a class is called off because of rain, it does not push them up the list. It is sorted by number of cancellations and, when tied, by proportion: someone who cancelled three out of three is more of a problem than someone who cancelled three out of forty.

### A split payment in the end-of-day till

If a visit was charged with two payment methods, the end-of-day till splits the amount between the two. The fifty in cash goes to cash and the fifty on card to card: the till matches the cash counted.

### A refund in the cash closing

A refund counts on the day it was made, in negative and in its payment method: if 12.50 € were refunded in cash today, today's cash is 12.50 less. Those refunded as store credit show up in the payment method *Store credit*, in negative; whatever is later charged with that credit shows up there in positive. The same happens with gift cards: their sale goes to the payment method it was paid with, and what is spent with them shows up in the row *Gift card*, which is not money in the till. And in the best-selling services, one that was sold and refunded does not count as sold.

### Roles and permissions

Administration → Roles

A **role** is a job: reception, professional, management. Each role carries inside it the **permissions** that the job needs, and a person is given roles, not loose permissions. That way, the day someone new starts there is no need to remember twenty boxes: they get their role and that is it. For the exception —the receptionist who also looks after the stock— there are the additional permissions of each user.

The centre comes with ready-made roles, which can be changed and deleted like any other. The first ones are jobs; the rest are **one role per module**:

- **Reception**: gives out times, minds the counter and charges.
- **Professional**: their agenda, their customers and the clinical record.
- **Management**: runs the centre — catalogue, staff, reports and settings — and looks after the users: changing their name or branch, unlocking them and resetting their password.
- **HR**: work calendars, staff and agenda.
- **Maintenance**: handles maintenance orders, their zones and their equipment.
- **Activations**: handles the activation points and their requests.
- **System administrator**: everything, delicate permissions included.

Module roles carry all the permissions of their module, except the delicate ones:

- **Customers**: customer records, their alerts, their points and the customer report.
- **Catalogue**: services, packages, products, suppliers and stock, with their reports.
- **Agenda**: appointments, waiting list, rooms, equipment and the appointments report.
- **Clinical record**: following up cases, that is, seeing, writing and printing the clinical record. Consents do not go with this role.
- **Till**: charging, refunds, debts, vouchers, gift cards, coupons, till closing and their reports.
- **Invoicing**: full invoices, invoice book and accounting draft.
- **Portal and alerts**: the booking portal and WhatsApp alerts.
- **Staff**: work calendars, working-time record and incidents.

To make one to measure, press **New role**, give it a name that does not exist yet and share out the permissions with the two lists: on the left the **available ones**, on the right the **ones of the role**. They are moved from one side to the other with the arrows or by double-clicking, and above each list there is a search box so you do not have to go through the hundred-odd. **Under the name of each permission, always in view, it says what it unlocks** and, where there could be confusion, what it does not unlock. That is not always a whole screen: sometimes it is just a button, or whether that button can be pressed. The search also looks at that explanation. The roles screen has a link at the top to this same section of the manual. Seeing services and changing their prices, for example, are two different permissions. And the **vouchers for sale** in the catalogue have their own, *See the catalogue vouchers* and *Create and edit the catalogue vouchers*, separate from those for services. Selling them at the point of sale needs neither of the two: all it takes is *Charge at the point of sale*.

**Delicate permissions are marked.** They are the ones that take out the data of many people at once — exporting the customer list, the time records of the whole staff — or the ones that cannot be undone, such as wiping the centre. None of the factory roles carries them: give them only to whoever runs the centre. Downloading the history of *one* customer is not one of these: that is printed in the consulting room and handed over.

Roles are given to a person on their record, in Administration → Users → (a user), with the same two lists. A user may carry several: whoever works the counter and also looks after the stock does not need a role invented for them alone.

**Creating a user by copy.** When someone joins who will do the same as another person, in Administration → Users press *Copy* on that person's row. The form of a new user opens already with **their roles, custom permissions, delegation and allowed IP addresses**; you only have to enter the username and a password. Before saving you can remove or add any role or additional permission. The password is not copied, and afterwards each user changes on their own: what is later taken from one is not taken from the other. As copying is handing out permissions, the button is only shown to whoever has the “Manage roles and permissions” permission.

**A user's additional permissions.** When a single person needs something their role does not carry, there is no need for a new role: in that user's form, below the roles, there is the *Additional permissions*section, collapsed and grouped by area as on the roles screen. What their roles already give is shown ticked and locked, with the name of the role that gives it, so you can see where each thing comes from; here you only add, you never take away what a role brings. With many permissions there is a word search. The extras are also shown in the user list, with a “+N custom” mark, so exceptions do not stay hidden. It is the exception, not the rule: if several people need the same thing, a role is better. Giving or removing permissions this way requires, like everything that hands out permissions, “Manage roles and permissions”.

On screen, a permission you do not have shows in two ways: either the option does not appear in the menu, or the button is there but will not be pressed. In the catalogue, for example, whoever can see the services but not change them does not see the button to create one, and the edit and retire buttons come out greyed.

**The change is noticed on logging back in.** What each person may do travels inside their session, so if you change somebody's role while they are working, they will not see it until they leave and come back in.

**Giving or removing roles belongs to whoever governs permissions.** It takes the «Govern roles and permissions» permission, which out of the box only the administrator has: whoever could hand out roles could give themselves the administrator one. For the same reason, nobody can change, disable or reset the password of a user who can do things they cannot.

A role that somebody carries will not let itself be deleted, so that nobody is left without permissions without noticing: first take it off those people, then delete the role.

### Creating or changing a role, step by step

Administration → Roles

1. Sign in to **Administration → Roles**. You will see the centre's list of roles, with how many permissions each carries and how many people have it.
2. For a new one, click **New role**. To change one that already exists —built-in ones too—, click **Edit** on its row.
3. Type the **name** (up to 60 letters, and not already used by another role) and, if you want, what it is for. The description appears in the list.
4. Hand out the permissions with the two lists: on the left the **available ones**, on the right the **ones of the role**. They are moved from one side to the other with the arrows or by double-clicking. Each permission has underneath what it allows; read it before moving it, especially if the name starts the same as another (seeing and changing are different permissions: explained below).
5. Use the search box of each list if you are looking for something specific: it finds by name, by area and by explanation. The delicate ones carry the label *Delicate*.
6. Click **Save**. The button does not light up until the role has a name and at least one permission.
7. For someone to have the role, go to **Administration → Users → (a user)** and move it to that person's list of roles. The change shows when that person signs in again.

Editing a role changes what **everyone who has it can do**, not just one. If what is needed is for a single person to do something different, they are given additional permissions from their user record, or a custom role is created if what they need is a whole other job. A role someone holds cannot be deleted: first it is taken away from those people. A user's additional permissions are copied when another user is created by copying.

### Seeing is not the same as changing, cancelling or downloading

Most things have one permission for looking and another, or several, for touching. Having the first does not give the second, which is why a role can let someone look things up without being able to break anything. Some examples of what the manual repeats more than once:

- **Agenda.** “View the agenda” only shows. Booking or moving is “Book and move appointments”; cancelling is “Cancel appointments”; cancelling one already charged also needs “Cancel charged appointments”; going to a past day, «Citar en días pasados» (book on past days); moving an attended one, «Mover citas atendidas» (move attended appointments).
- **Clients.** “View clients’ records” lets you see the record read-only. Changing it is “Edit clients’ records”; the phone and email are hidden without “See phones and emails”; notes are written with “Write customer notes” and deleted with “Delete clients’ notes”: they are two different permissions.
- **Reports.** Opening a report is that report's own permission, for example “Payments report”. Downloading it to a file also needs “Download reports as files”; and the clients report to a file needs “Export the clients list”, which is delicate.
- **Catalogue.** “See services” shows the services and their prices; “Create and edit services” is what lets you change them. With view-only access, the buttons to add, edit and remove are switched off or do not appear. Choosing services with the quick buttons when booking does not need to see the catalogue: that is another permission, “Quick service selection”. The rate groups screen does not open with seeing the services either: it needs “See voucher groups”, which out of the box only Management has.
- **Till.** Charging is “Charge at the point of sale”; applying a discount, “Apply discounts”; leaving something owing, “Leave an amount owing”; printing the receipt, “Print receipts”; giving a quote, “Give quotes”; refunding a ticket, “Refund a ticket”; seeing what has been charged today and this month on the Home screen, “See the till on the Home screen”. Nobody at reception reopens a closed till without «Reopen a closed cash».
- **Maintenance.** “See maintenance” reads the orders; opening one is “Report a problem”; moving it from one state to another is “Manage orders”; closing it, “Close and reopen orders”.

### Three typical examples

**Reception.** The built-in role “Reception” books and moves appointments, looks after the client record (it also edits it and sees the phone number), charges, applies discounts and balances the till. It does not refund tickets, does not reopen closed tills, does not cancel appointments already charged or move attended ones, does not merge or delete clients and does not see the reports. If in a centre the front-desk person must also refund tickets, they are given a custom role with “Refund a ticket” instead of giving them the whole “Management” role.

**Professional.** The “Professional” role sees and moves its own agenda, looks at the records, writes and prints the clinical record and asks for consent signatures. It does not charge, does not edit the record, does not see the phone number or email and does not cancel appointments. It is what is usually needed for whoever provides the service and does not work the front desk.

**Someone who only sees reports.** There is no built-in role like that. Create it with **New role** (for example “Reports only”) and give it the permissions of the reports it should see —“Payments report”, “Daily totals”, “Scorecard”, etc.—. If it is not given “Download reports as files”, it will see them on screen but will not be able to download them. Reports of a module that is switched off do not appear in the list.

### Delicate permissions: which they are and why

A permission is **delicate** when it takes the data of many people out of the centre at once or does something that cannot be undone. On the roles screen they carry the label *Delicate*. **No built-in role has them, not even the module ones**: only the System administrator. To give them to someone they have to be put by hand into a custom role, and it is best to do so only for whoever runs the centre. They are these:

- “Export the clients list”: takes away the whole list of clients with their contact details.
- “Adjust points by hand”: allows giving or taking away points with no purchase behind them.
- “Export the working-time register”: takes out the working-time register of the whole staff.
- “Give access to the working-time register”: opens the working-time register to people outside the centre through a link.
- “Give access to the maintenance book”: opens the maintenance book to people outside the centre through a link.
- “Delete activation points”: really deletes a point with all its requests and cannot be recovered.
- “Connect external programs”: gives the keys with which an external program gets into the centre.
- “Who opened which record”: shows who has looked at whose record or clinical record.
- “Invoicing and VeriFactu”: switches invoicing on and governs what is sent to the AEAT.
- “Move invoicing to production”: moves invoicing to production: it deletes the test invoices and there is no way back.
- “Empty the centre”: deletes all the data of the centre and there is no way back.
- «Import from another program»: brings in at once the data from another program.
- “Manage roles and permissions”: whoever hands out permissions can give them to themselves.

Downloading the history of *one* person is not delicate: it is printed at the consultation and handed to them.

### A permission depends on the centre's modules

Each permission belongs to a module: agenda, clients, till, invoicing, staff, maintenance, and so on. The roles screen **only shows the permissions of the modules the centre has switched on**, and if a module is switched off later, its permissions stop working for everyone, including the administrator, whatever roles they have. Some modules need others: the diary, the till and the clinical record need Customers; the diary and the till, Catalogue; invoicing and promotions, Till; the portal, Diary; and IoT lives inside Maintenance. Activations and IoT are not switched on by default. If you are missing the permissions for a part of the application, the first thing to check is whether the centre has that module.

### What happens when permissions change

What each person can do travels inside their session. When a role, or someone’s roles, are changed, the change **shows when that person signs out (*Sign out*) and signs in again**. Until then they carry on with what they had. If the urgency is to take something away from someone who is working, ask them to sign out and in again.

### What is added or removed when you give or remove a role

A person **can do everything that any of their roles gives them**: the permissions of their roles add up. Three consequences follow, which are worth being clear about before touching anything:

- **Giving a role** gives them at once all the permissions it carries, that is, all the screens and buttons those permissions open.
- **Removing a role** takes away the permissions that role gave and that *none of their other roles* still gives them. If someone who has “Agenda” and “Management” loses “Agenda”, they can still see and move appointments, because Management also carries it. For a person to stop seeing a module, you must remove *all* the roles that carry it.
- **Removing all the roles** from a person leaves them without permission to do anything: they cannot sign in to the application.

Each module has its **module role**, which carries all the permissions of the module and only those: it is the quickest way to give or remove a whole module. To fine-tune, do not remove the role and improvise: create a custom role (**New role**) with only the permissions you want and give it to the person instead of the module role. For example, so that someone can see the services but not change the prices, a role with “See the services” and without “Edit the services”.

**Delicate permissions are not in any module role.** Only the System administrator carries them, so giving “Till” or “Customers” never gives, for example, the export of the customer list. If needed, they are given by hand in a custom role.

If a module is switched off in the centre, its permissions are of no use to anyone, not even the administrator, whatever roles they have.

### Which IP addresses each user can sign in from

Administration → Users → (a user)

*[Captura] On the user's form, under the permissions, is the list of addresses they can sign in from.*

When you edit a user there is a field **Allowed IP addresses**: you type one, press Enter, and you can add several. With the word `any` the user signs in from anywhere, which is what every account starts with; leaving it empty puts `any`.

It does what it looks like it does: it keeps the front desk account working inside the centre and not from home. Whoever tries from somewhere else sees the same “could not sign in” as with a wrong password, and the attempt is recorded.

**This needs the centre to have a fixed IP.** With an address that changes, the day the provider changes it that user is locked out. Always leave one administration account on `any`, or there will be nowhere to fix it from.

### How the application looks

Administration → Preferences → Appearance

*[Captura] The agenda with the dark theme, meant for rooms that work in low light.*

There are five themes and one is picked for the whole centre. Four are colour ones —**Turnera green**, **Night blue**, **Plum** and **Terracotta**— and they only change the left-hand bar, the tone of the background and the lines of the agenda; they are there so the application does not clash with the identity of the centre. The fifth is **Dark**, and that one does turn the whole screen around.

The dark one is for the front desks and rooms that work in low light on purpose, where a white screen dazzles. In it the agenda status colours are lightened so they still read, so **the legend changes tones but not meanings**: what was blue is still blue, and what was almost black becomes almost white.

The green of the buttons does not change with the theme: it is the colour of an action, and it is in all five.

You see the theme as soon as you pick it in the dropdown, before saving, so you can compare them. **It is not kept until you press Save**: if you leave the screen without saving, the previous one comes back.

Below the theme is the **stripe colour**: the diagonal stripes of the diary’s closed periods. What falls outside the shift of the work calendar has a colour of its own, the **colour of the out-of-shift stripes**, with its sample and its **Reset**: they are separate because you can book outside the shift and not in a closed period. The background of those gaps is always grey and only the stripes change; out of the box they are **salmon**. It is chosen with the colour picker, it shows on the sample and on the agenda straight away, and **Reset** puts the salmon back. As with the theme, it is not kept until you press Save.

The **Agenda grid** sets how strongly the lines of the hours and slots show: *Soft*, *Normal* or *Strong*. If it is hard to tell where each slot begins, choose *Strong*. It also shows in the agenda as soon as you choose it and is not kept until you save.

The logo of the centre stays on a white background in every theme, the dark one included. That is on purpose: a mark meant for white paper would disappear on the bar.

### The VAT of each thing that is sold

Administration → Catalogue → Services / Products

On the form of each service and each product, under the price, there is a **VAT** dropdown with five options: *21 %*, *10 %*, *4 %*, *Exempt* and *Undecided*.

**It is decided here, when the item is created.** That is on purpose: whoever is taking the money with the customer in front of them has no reason to know whether a session is exempt, and asking at that moment is the quickest way to end up with books that do not add up. The catalogue carries it and the ticket inherits it.

**Exempt is not «at 0 %».** A session with a therapeutic purpose given by a healthcare professional is *exempt* under article 20 of the VAT Act, and that is a different fact from a rate of zero per cent: the invoice has to say why. That is why there is a separate *Exempt* option and not a zero in the list of rates.

**Undecided** is not a fault, it is what the first day looks like. Leave it there for whatever you are not sure about and go through it with your accountant; the draft accounting summary will tell you exactly what is missing.

### The draft accounting summary

Reports → Draft accounting summary

*[Captura] One entry per day and VAT rate, with its base, its tax and the range of tickets.*

**A draft to review with your accountant. It does not replace your books.** No invoice is issued or numbered: this screen only reads the tickets that already exist and adds them up. Each ticket line keeps the VAT the catalogue had on the day of the sale, so changing a rate does not change what has already been sold. Whatever was sold while the service or product was *undecided* takes the rate you give it later.

It is the **summary entry** your accountant asks for: one entry per day and rate, with the base, the tax and the range of tickets of each, instead of four hundred loose lines. It downloads to Excel.

At the top it says how much money there is **with no VAT set**. Those lines add no base and no tax on purpose: making them up would be the very opposite of what this screen is for. They are the list of what to go through in the catalogue.

The screen comes switched off. It is switched on under *Administration → Preferences → Payments and loyalty*, and it is worth doing once the VAT of the catalogue has been gone through: before that almost everything comes out «not set».

### WhatsApp reminders

Appointment record → WhatsApp · Administration → Preferences → Messages to customers

Turnera **does not send anything on its own**. When you open an appointment there is a WhatsApp button that opens the conversation with the customer, in the WhatsApp of whoever is attending, with the notice already written. You read it, change it if need be and send it. It is what the centre already did — pick up the phone, open WhatsApp and type — without having to copy the number or key in the sentence.

The button carries the customer's phone number inside, so it only works for whoever has the permission to see phone numbers and emails. Everyone else sees it switched off.

**There is no automatic reminder.** Nobody sends a message without having read it first, and in exchange there is no sending N hours ahead, no log of what was sent and no delivery confirmation. If your centre needs that, talk to us.

*[Captura] The WhatsApp card in Preferences: the country code, the text of the notice and how the customer will read it.*

#### The country code

WhatsApp needs the phone number with its international code. The one you put here is prepended to customers' numbers that do not carry it; a customer whose number already starts with `+` is left as it is. Without a code the button cannot open the conversation.

#### The appointment message

This is the text that comes already written when you press the WhatsApp button of an appointment: it serves to confirm it or to remind about it, depending on how you word it. Above the text are the appointment fields; drag one to the spot in the text where you want it, or click it to put it where the cursor is.

You write it, and the appointment data (first name, surname, service, professional, date, time, centre, centre phone and link) appear inside the text as **coloured tags** that fill themselves in when the conversation opens. To remove one, delete it as if it were a letter. Below you will see how the customer will read it.

#### The birthday greeting

Below the appointment message there is another like it for congratulating on a birthday, with its own tags (first name, surname, centre, centre phone and link). It is not sent by itself: on **Home**, each customer on the birthday card has a WhatsApp button that opens the conversation with the greeting already written, and you send it by hand. The button only appears for people who can see the customer’s contact details; if the customer has no phone, it warns instead of opening anything.

#### The reminder in other languages

Below the main text there is an optional section per language. A customer who has one of those languages on their record gets the conversation opened with that text; everyone else, with the main one. The tags work the same in each language, and the date is written as it is read there: in German, with dots (22.09.2026). The preview of each language appears on saving.

### The centre's email

Everything to do with the centre —what it sends to its customers, its staff and its payroll advisor (gift cards, payment links, portal access, bookings, surveys, payslips and labour documents) and the notices to its Management or HR— goes out **from the centre's email**: through the server in **Preferences → Centre mail server** and with the address in «Address emails are sent from». Whoever receives it sees the centre's name as sender and, if they reply, writes to the centre.

While the centre is still testing and has no server set up, those emails go out through the platform's. **A centre in production with no server set up does not send them**, and Turnera says so: a red notice appears at the top of every screen, with a link to Preferences for whoever can set it up, and anything sent by hand (resending a gift card, a survey, the hours summary, the send to the payroll advisor) answers that it cannot go out and why. Only Turnera's notices to the centre go out through the platform: fee changes, technical incidents, the data export or the integrations.

#### What reaches each address

Administration → Preferences → Email addresses

- **Contact**: the one customers see. It goes at the foot of the emails they receive, in the booking portal and on gift cards. The notice of a bill paid twice by link also arrives here.
- **Management**: club sign-ups, cancelled visits and low survey ratings. Also staff matters (holiday requests, expiring contracts, missed clock-ins, locked PINs, sends to and downloads by the payroll advisor) if no separate HR address has been set.
- **Board**: deleted appointments, services removed from a visit and non-conformities. If left empty, these notices are not sent.
- **Address emails are sent from**: the sender of all of the above. It must be an address the centre's server lets you use; usually its own account.

With port 465 the connection is SSL; with 587, tick «Encrypted connection (STARTTLS)». The user is usually the full email address. The button **«Send a test email»** asks which address to send it to and tests what is on screen, without saving; if the server does not accept it, it shows what the server answered.

### Payment links with Stripe

Administration → Preferences → Payments and loyalty

To send a customer a link they can pay from their phone, the centre connects **its own Stripe account**. The money goes straight from your customer to your account: Turnera takes no fee, never stands in between and does not appear on the receipt. Whatever Stripe charges per payment is between you and Stripe.

#### Connecting it, step by step

1. Sign in to dashboard.stripe.com with the centre's account. If you do not have one yet, create it there: Stripe will ask for the business details and the bank account where you want to receive the money.
2. Go to *Developers → API keys* and copy the **secret key**, the one starting with `sk_live_`. The publishable one will not do (`pk_`).
3. In Turnera, open *Preferences → Payments and loyalty*, paste the key into the card *Payment links with Stripe* and press *Connect*.

That is all. Turnera checks with Stripe that the key is valid and registers in your account the **payment notice**: it is what tells Turnera that a customer has paid. You will see it in Stripe, under *Developers → Webhooks*, named *Turnera: pagos por enlace*; do not delete it, or payments will stop being marked automatically.

The key is stored encrypted and never shown again: the card only shows which account it is connected to, the last four characters of the key and since when. If you want to try it before charging for real, connect the test key (`sk_test_`, in Stripe's test mode): the card will remind you that those payments are not real until you replace it with the live one.

#### Charging with the link

With Stripe connected, the point of sale offers the payment method *Payment link* (without Stripe it does not appear anywhere). You choose it like any other and the button changes to *Close and send link*. The sale is closed as a **debt**, because the customer has not paid yet, and the window that follows brings the link:

- if the customer has an email address on their record, **it has already been sent**, and the window says where to;
- *Copy* puts it on the clipboard, to paste it wherever you like;
- *Send by WhatsApp* opens the WhatsApp of whoever is at the desk with the message already written, in the customer's conversation if they have a phone number.

You can also send it for a debt that already existed: in *Unpaid visits*, the button *Send link* on its row. Sending it again sends the same link, not a new one.

The customer opens the link, sees the centre's name and the amount, presses *Pay* and pays by card on the Stripe page. When done, they come back to a page of the centre confirming the payment. At that moment the debt **settles itself**, with the payment method *Payment link*, which is how it later appears in the reports. What marks the debt as paid is the notice Stripe sends, not the customer coming back to the page: nobody can mark it as paid by typing an address by hand.

The link does not expire and always charges whatever is still pending. If in the meantime the bill is paid at the counter, the link says it is already paid and does not let anyone pay again.

**If they still pay twice.** It can happen if someone charges it at the counter just while the customer is paying in Stripe. Turnera does not touch the till: the bill stays as it was charged at the counter, and the centre's contact email receives a notice with the bill, the amount and the Stripe payment reference. Refund it from your Stripe dashboard, under *Payments*.

#### Changing it or disconnecting

*Change the key* connects the new one and removes the payment notice of the old one. *Disconnect* forgets the key and deletes the notice from your Stripe account; links you had already sent will no longer be marked as paid. Visible only to people with the permission *Connect external programs*.

**Would you rather not give Turnera the full secret key?** Create a *restricted key* (`rk_live_`) in Stripe with write permission only on *Checkout Sessions* and *Webhook Endpoints*, and paste that one. It works the same.

### Informed consent

Customer record → Signed paperwork · Administration → Consents

The customer signs on the **front-desk tablet**. Nothing is sent to them by e-mail or by WhatsApp, and they do not sign on their own phone: small screens, different browsers and older customers.

#### How it gets signed

1. The tablet is always open at *Signatures*, with its own reception session. It asks by itself every few seconds whether there is anything to sign.
2. On the customer record, reception presses *Ask for signature* and chooses the document.
3. The document appears on the tablet by itself, with the name and tax ID already filled in. The customer reads it and signs with a finger.
4. The PDF, the stroke, the time, the version of the text and a fingerprint of the document are stored.

#### Setting up the signing tablet

Administration → Users · tablet → /firmas

The tablet needs nothing special: it is a browser with the *Signatures* open and a user with the built-in role **Signatures**, which can only collect the signatures requested from the front desk.

1. In *Administration → Users*, create a user just for the tablet (for example, `tablet`) with the role **Signatures** and the front-desk branch. That way it doesn't share a session with anyone and, on signing in, it goes straight to the signing screen: it doesn't open signed documents or any other screen.
2. On the tablet, open the Turnera address followed by `/firmas`. When you request a signature from the customer record, Turnera shows you that full address and you can copy it.
3. Log in with that user, choose the delegation and tick *Remember me on this device* so the session does not end when the browser is closed.
4. Optional: in the tablet's browser, add the page to the home screen to open it full screen.

- Every three seconds the tablet asks for the oldest pending signature **of its delegation**. Each request is valid for five minutes and is signed only once.
- The tablet user does not open consents that have already been signed.
- The *Signatures* screen is shown **on its own**: it only carries the Turnera logo, with no menu, no language switch and no way to change user or log out. Whoever has the tablet in front of them is the customer. To change user or log out, open `/inicio` from the tablet's browser.
- After signing, the document disappears and the tablet shows, with a big green tick, a «Document signed» in the language that was read, next to a *Close* button that closes the tab. If nobody presses it, after 20 seconds it goes back to waiting by itself. If the browser does not allow closing the tab, the button leaves it blank.
- The customer record that reception has open **finds out on its own** of the signature, within a few seconds: the signed document appears in *Consents* without leaving the record and coming back in, even if the pending-signature notice has been closed.
- If the tablet loses its connection to Turnera, it says so («No connection») instead of saying there is nothing to sign, and it recovers by itself when the connection returns. If it drops while signing, it warns after 20 seconds and the customer can sign again.

#### Consent forms in other languages

Whoever signs has to understand what they sign. When creating or changing a template, below the main text there is *Text in other languages*: one optional section per language, with its name and its text. The gaps are written the same way in all of them, with their Spanish names: `{{cliente}}`, `{{codigo_cliente}}` (the code of the customer's record in Turnera), `{{nif}}`, `{{centro}}`, `{{fecha}}`, `{{tratamiento}}`, `{{representante}}`, `{{representante_documento}}` and `{{representante_parentesco}}`.

**Under-age customers.** If the date of birth says the customer is not yet 18, their legal guardian signs, and Turnera handles it by itself: the tablet says “Marta Gil (mother) signs as legal guardian of…” and the signed PDF carries, below the customer, a block *Legal guardian* with their name, relationship and ID document, even if the template has no such gaps. The gaps `{{representante}}`, `{{representante_documento}}` and `{{representante_parentesco}}` are for naming them inside the text too; for an adult they come out as a dash. If the minor has no guardian in their record yet, the signature cannot be requested: the notice says to fill it in first.

- If the customer's record has a language the template is written in, the tablet shows them that text, and the buttons and the notice below are in their language too. The signed PDF comes out the same.
- If not, they sign the main text, in the centre's language.
- Each signature keeps the language it was signed in and the exact text that was read.

**The request expires after five minutes** and works once only. That is deliberate: a request left lying on the counter is a signature waiting for the wrong person. If it expires, ask for it again from the record.

*[Captura] The front-desk tablet with the document loaded: the name and tax ID are already filled in.*

#### The templates

They are written in *Administration → Consents*. In the text you can leave placeholders that fill themselves in: `cliente`, `nif`, `centro`, `fecha` and `tratamiento`, written between double braces.

*[Captura] The templates, with their version and which ones are compulsory for every customer.*

**A template is never modified.** Saving changes creates the next version and retires the previous one. What has already been signed has to keep existing exactly as it was read: it is half of what defends this signature the day someone disputes what they were told.

If you mark a template as **required**, the record of every customer who has not signed it says so. Without that warning this would be a folder of papers: the point is finding out before doing the treatment, not afterwards.

#### Consents required by certain services (optional)

Administration → Consents → New version → Services that require it

**It is an extra that can be used or not.** If no service is chosen, everything works exactly as before and nothing changes day to day.

If services are chosen (for example, the diode laser consent is required by “Diode laser underarms” and “Diode laser legs”), Turnera asks for it from **whoever has or has had an appointment for one of those services**, without having to mark the template as mandatory. Other customers see nothing. The services are saved with the template: making a new version of the text does not remove them.

What is left to sign shows in the customer record, in the report *Pending consents* and at the **Point of sale**: when the customer is chosen, a notice shows what is missing and a button *Ask for signature* for each document, which sends it to the front-desk tablet. In the strip of *To charge*, whoever has something pending carries a small icon with the number. **The notice never prevents charging.**

#### Fields to fill in the document

Administration → Consents → New version

Besides the data Turnera fills in by itself (customer, tax ID, centre, date and treatment), each document can carry its **own fields**: for example the *areas* the customer agrees to have treated, an acceptance tick box or a date. Whatever is chosen appears written in the signed PDF.

1. When editing the text, on the right is the *Fields* panel. The **green** ones are filled in by Turnera; the **orange** ones belong to the document.
2. Press *New field*, give it a name and choose the type: text (one or several lines), date, tick box, drop-down or multiple choice. In drop-downs, the options are written one per line.
3. Drag the field to the spot in the text where it should appear, or press *+* to place it where the cursor is. Turnera's fields are placed the same way.
4. Choose *Who fills it in?*. By default, checkboxes, dropdowns and multiple selections are ticked by **the signer, on the tablet**; text and date are written by **reception** when asking for the signature. You can change it field by field.
5. When asking for the signature, reception fills in the fields that are theirs. The tablet shows the text with the customer's fields blank and, before the signature box, a form with large buttons to tick them. “Sign” is not enabled until the required ones are done.

*[Captura] The tablet before signing: the checkboxes and areas are ticked by the signer and appear written in the document.*

- The values appear **in bold** within the text: a multiple choice, separated by commas and in the order of the list; a date as dd/mm/yyyy; a tick box as «Yes» or «No»; an optional field left empty, as «—».
- A yes/no question or a checkbox ticked by the signer can also **update the customer's file**: tick *When signed, update the customer record: accepts marketing communications* on it. When signed, "Yes" records in the file that the customer accepts marketing communications and "No" removes it. If that signature is withdrawn, the file is set to "Does not accept". Only one question per template can do this.
- If you rename a field, it changes in every text and every language. If you delete it, it disappears from all of them. Signatures already made never change.
- A text that uses a field the template does not define cannot be saved.
- Like any change to a template, this creates a **new version** and the previous one is switched off.

#### Bringing in those already signed on another platform

Consents signed before using Turnera, in another program or on scanned paper, are uploaded as PDF to the customer record with *Import signed*. You can choose **up to 5 at once**, and for each one set the name, the **document date** and the **template it matches**. If the template is given, it counts as signed and the missing-consent warning stops appearing. In the list they are marked as *Imported*, with the file and the day they were uploaded; they are downloaded and withdrawn like those signed here. Each PDF can be up to 20 MB. Importing is the permission **«Import signed consents»**, which Reception and Management have.

#### Bringing in many at once

If there are dozens or hundreds, in *Import from another program* there is a card **Signed consents as PDF**. Choose the folder (or the PDFs) and press *Try it*: each PDF is matched to its customer by the **customer number** carried by the folder that contains it (a folder called *26033*) or its name, and the customer's name is checked to appear in the file. If the file name starts with a date in *yymmdd* (*261016_…*), that is the date of the document. The trial run says, file by file, which go in and which stay out and why (different name, customer that does not exist, no number, invalid PDF); it saves nothing. When importing, each one ends up in the record just as if it had been uploaded by hand, and importing the same PDF twice does not duplicate it. It needs the permission to import from another program and the one to import signed consents. This import is not undone in one go: a consent put in the wrong place is removed from the customer record.

#### Who can see what has been signed

Reception can ask for the signature and accompany it, but **downloading a signed consent needs the clinical history permission and is written down** in the access trail, just like opening a case. A signed consent is health data. That way the front-desk tablet can stay open all day with its session without that session opening anyone's documents.

#### Withdrawing a signature

It happens: the wrong document gets signed, or another customer's, or the patient **withdraws their consent**, which is a right of theirs and nobody's mistake. On the line of the document there is a *Withdraw* button that asks for it with a compulsory reason.

**Withdrawing does not delete.** The document stays where it was, with its date and its fingerprint, and beside it goes who withdrew it, when and why. A signed consent is the proof of a conversation that took place, and deleting it leaves the centre without it the day somebody argues about what they were told. What does change is that it stops counting: if the template was compulsory, the record warns again that it is missing.

It needs the **WITHDRAW CONSENTS**, which is its own and not the attentions one: whoever asks for a signature at the counter does not have to be able to undo it. It is granted in *Administration → Users*, normally only to management.

It is a **simple electronic signature**. What holds it up is not the stroke: it is the versioning of the text, the time and the fingerprint of the document, which is what lets you prove that the PDF being shown is the same one that was signed.

### Connecting an outside program (ERP)

Administration → Integrations

If your accountant or your ERP wants to read the centre's data directly —the catalogue, the customers, the sales— there is no need to give it a Turnera user. You register an **integration**, which is a credential meant for a program and not for a person.

*[Captura] The centre's integrations, with what each one can read and from which addresses.*

When creating it you decide:

- **Name.** How you will recognise it in the list. For instance, "the accountant's ERP".
- **Contact email.** The person who maintains the integration. Used to warn them about maintenance, version changes or API incidents.
- **Delegation.** The only one that program will see. It is fixed here because a program cannot choose one on a screen, the way a person does when logging in.
- **Scopes.** What it can read: the catalogue, customers, sales or the free slots of the diary, whether it can book appointments and whether it can check if an email or a phone number belongs to a customer (it only answers yes or no). Give only what it needs.
- **Allowed addresses.** Which IP it can connect from, one per line. It accepts a range written in CIDR, for example `203.0.113.0/24`.

**The secret is shown once only.** On saving, the screen shows a `client_id` and a `client_secret`. Copy them and pass them to whoever programs the ERP over a secure channel: they are not stored in the clear and they cannot be looked up again. If they are lost, they are rotated.

**You find out about every new integration.** When an integration is saved, the centre owner receives an email with its name, contact, permissions and addresses (never the secret). If you get one you did not expect, switch that integration off and change the administration password.

**Booking from outside.** A campaign platform that brings you clients can book their appointments directly, with the permissions *free slots* and *book appointments*. It only sees and books the same as the portal: the services and places that can be booked online, with their notice and the days ahead. If the customer already has a record with that email, the appointment goes to their record; if not, a new one is opened. Every appointment that comes in this way carries in its comments which platform it comes from and with what reference. If the service needs a piece of equipment and it is busy at that time, the platform receives its own message, different from «slot taken», so it can offer another time.

**Notifications to your system (webhooks).** Instead of asking every few minutes whether there are new appointments, the integration can receive a notification at an address of its own when an appointment is created, changed or cancelled. In the list of integrations, the *Webhooks* button lets you add the address (always `https://` and public), choose which events it wants and test that it answers. By default it only reports appointments created by that same integration; if you untick that box, it also reports those made at reception and in the portal, and with the customers permission it adds their contact details. Every notification is signed with a secret that is shown only once, like the integration's. When a webhook is added, the centre owner receives an email with the address and the events, and the address cannot be changed afterwards: to point somewhere else, create another webhook. The *Latest deliveries* button shows what was sent and what the other side answered.

**Monitoring.** Turnera counts what each integration does without ever storing what is asked. The check of whether an email or a phone number belongs to a customer allows 20 queries per minute and 2,000 per day, and if the use looks like someone going through a list of people or guessing passwords, the centre owner and the integration's contact receive an email.

**What to give whoever does the programming.** The API guide, with examples and the OpenAPI file, is at devel.es/turnera/integradores. With it and the credentials they have everything they need.

**There is no "from anywhere".** An ERP runs on a server and that server has a fixed IP. If they tell you it does not, that is a conversation worth having before handing over your customers' data.

**Clinical records cannot be granted.** It is not a permission that comes switched off: it does not exist. The customers scope returns the administrative record —name, contact, address— and nothing of history, cases or progress notes.

**Almost everything is read-only.** The only thing an external program can do is book a new appointment in a free slot. Nothing the ERP does changes a payment, a record or an existing appointment.

#### Changing the secret without cutting the service

The *Rotate secret* issues a new one and **keeps the previous one alive**. That way the ERP is reconfigured without a rush; once it answers with the new one, press *Retire the previous one* and the old one stops working. While the rotation lasts, the list marks it.

**Data protection.** By authorising an integration, that program's provider starts processing your customers' data on the centre's behalf. Note it in your register of processing activities and check that you have the right processor agreement with them.

Refunds reach the ERP as negative sales, each one saying which sale it undoes, so what it takes to the accounts matches the centre's takings.

Whoever programs the other side will find everything they need —how to authenticate, what is available and how to paginate— in the technical guide `docs/api-integracion.md`.

### Disabling a centre

Administration → Delegations → Edit

When a centre closes, or stops working with Turnera for a while, there is no need to delete it: it is **disabled** by unticking *Delegation active*. Its customers, appointments and payments stay where they were, but nobody can get into it, and whoever was in it is signed out straight away.

When signing in to Turnera, if only one centre is left enabled you go straight into it; if several are, you keep choosing. Whoever only worked in the disabled centre can no longer sign in, and the sign-in screen tells them so.

The disabled centre stays in the list of delegations, marked as inactive, and is enabled again from there. Two cannot be disabled: the one you are working in —switch to another one first— and the last one left enabled.

### Bringing in data from another platform

Administration → Import from another program

For a centre coming from another platform. It brings in, in one go, the customers (with their notes), the price list of services, packages and products, the packages customers have half used, gift vouchers with a balance, the diary —what lies ahead and the last year—, the stock of each product, each customer's treatment history and what they left owing. It needs the permission **«Import from another program»**, which is sensitive and only the administrator has.

1. In the source platform, export each screen to CSV: customers, service history, price list, diary, packages, deferred payments, gift vouchers and stock. Where each one is, the import screen itself tells you, under *How to get the sheets out of the source platform*. Extra sheets —daily cash, invoices, advance payments— do no harm: the report says they are not imported.
2. Press **«Choose files»** and choose them all at once. Their names do not matter: each sheet is recognised by its columns.
3. Press **«Try it»**. Turnera runs the whole import and throws it away at the end: the report says how much of each thing would be created, how much was already there and what is left out, with grouped warnings and examples to look them up in the source. Nothing is saved.
4. If the report looks right, **«Import»**. The files are not stored anywhere.

The **code** of each service in the source arrives as its short code. There, one code can be used for several services, and in Turnera it cannot: those get what sets their name apart added —IDEE becomes IDEE20, IDEE30 and IDEE50 for the 20, 30 and 50-minute Indiba— and the trial report says how each one ends up. They can be changed later in the service record.

The **vouchers that have already expired** with unused sessions arrive disabled: they are kept on the customer's record, but cannot be used. The report counts them separately.

With the **stocks**, each product arrives with the units the source had in the centre; negatives arrive as zero. If a product is in the stock but no longer in the price list, it is created anyway, with 21 % VAT, so its units are not lost. Without that sheet, products arrive with zero stock and are set with a stocktake from *Stock*. If someone has already done the stocktake in Turnera, a new import does not overwrite it.

**Past appointments** arrive attended or cancelled, as they were in the source, and the diary does not ring them in red: they were charged there. **The service history** —years of sessions— arrives as the customer's visits: those on the same day are one visit, with one line per service, and they show in the history of their record. The device, its settings, the session and the comments go on each line. The sheet does not say how much was charged, so they arrive at zero and paid: they do not move the till or show up as debt. Postponed ones and no-shows are not brought in, because they did not happen. **Pending payments** arrive in *Unpaid visits*; those already paid do not, because they are in the source platform's till.

Importing twice duplicates nothing: what was already brought is recognised and counted as «already there». That makes it possible to import a newer export on the day of the switch and bring only what is missing.

**It can be undone** from «Imports done», and it takes away everything that import brought without touching what was already there. But if someone has already worked with what was imported —an appointment, a charge, a note— Turnera does not undo it and says what prevents it: undoing it would take that work with it.

**Packages** are brought in with what their name says, which in the source lists the services separated by «+»: «PAQUETE 7 LPG + PRESOTERAPIA» is seven sessions of each, and «TRIO DE ZONAS (AX+ING+BP)» one of each area. The import report shows which service it understood in each part, so it can be checked. If a part is not recognised («RFN400» is a device, not a service), that package is not brought in until it is stated in a sheet *Contenido de paquetes* with the columns `ID Paquete`, `ID Servicio` and `Cantidad` (the IDs are those of the source price list), which is uploaded with the others and overrides the name.

**What is not brought:** the source platform's portal passwords, vouchers with no balance, used-up packages, the old slot of moved appointments, daily tills and invoices, which are closed accounts and are looked up in the source platform. Signed consents are not in any sheet: they are brought in as PDFs from each customer's record (see how).

**After importing:** professionals arrive with the code they had in the source and need a name, and customers with a repeated ID are all brought in and reviewed with the record merge (a minor with their mother's ID is not the same person).

### Taking the centre's data

Administration → Take your data

The data belongs to the centre, not to Turnera. From here the **admin** account downloads everything the centre has stored, in a single zip file and whenever it wants: to move to another program, to keep its own copy or to hand it to whoever looks after its IT.

Inside there are three things and a *LEEME.txt* that explains them:

- **turnera.sql**, the whole database. Any IT person loads it into MySQL with one command, and with it you can come back to Turnera or move the data to another program.
- **One spreadsheet per table** —customers, appointments, payments, vouchers, clinical records…—, which opens in Excel with a double click.
- **All stored images and documents**, each in its usual file (JPG, PNG or PDF) inside the folder *ficheros/*, sorted by table: photos of customers and professionals, clinical record photos (progress notes), signed consents and their signatures, the professionals' signed data protection documents and the logo. The photos of maintenance orders and of work calendar incidents, and the signatures of the monthly working-time summaries, go too. Each table's spreadsheet says which file belongs to each row, and all of them are also inside *turnera.sql*, for whoever restores the database.

Passwords, clock-in PINs and integration secrets are left out: outside Turnera they are useless, and in the wrong hands someone could try to crack them.

**Why two keys are needed.** It is the most sensitive download in the application, because it takes the data of every customer at once, clinical records included. Only the admin account can ask for it —no other, even with every permission— and on top of that it needs a link that goes to the email of the centre's owner, the one given when signing up, which cannot be changed from the application. Whoever steals the password does not have the email, and whoever reads the email does not have the password.

1. Click *Send me the link*. The screen tells you which email it goes to, with part of the address hidden.
2. Open the email in the same browser, signed in as admin, and press *Download*. The button shows how far it has got.
3. Keep the zip somewhere safe: it holds the data of all your customers.

The link works only once and expires in 30 minutes; if the download is interrupted, ask for another, up to three an hour. When it finishes another email arrives saying it has been downloaded, with the time and the address it was done from: if it was not you, change the admin password and write to us. The screen also keeps the list of exports requested and downloaded.

## 10. Staff

Menu → Staff

Everything to do with the team: who works at the centre, when they come and go, and how their hours are spread over the year. In the menu it is grouped under **Staff**, except *Clock*, which is at the top, next to the agenda, because it is what is used every day at the front desk.

### Staff

Menu → Staff → Practitioners

Who sees clients at the centre. The record holds their details, their **colour in the agenda** and, if wanted, their **column**: the fixed slot they take in the delegation’s agenda. Left blank, the agenda gives them the next free one in order. You can upload their **photo**, which is reduced before uploading, and state their **gender**: without a photo, lists and the roster show a woman’s, a man’s or, if not stated, a neutral avatar.

The same record is where you reset their **clocking PIN** (see Working time) and you tick whether the **summary of their working day** is emailed to them every month, which needs an email address on the record.

**Not all staff appear in the agenda.** For anyone who does not see people by appointment (in a care home, for example, care assistants, cleaning or kitchen staff), untick on their record the *Shown in the agenda*box. They remain active: they clock in, appear in the roster and can be chosen at the till or in the clinical record, but they have no column in *Day by member of staff* or in *Week by member of staff*, and online booking gives them no appointments. If they already had any, their column still appears on the days they have one, so no appointment is lost from view.

The record has the **start date**: the day the person started working at the centre. It is not mandatory, and with it the record shows their **length of service** (for example, «3 years and 2 months»).

The **age** is no longer typed in: it comes from the **date of birth**. In old records that only had the age typed in by hand it is still shown, with a notice to enter the date.

**When registering someone new**, with the start date filled in, you can choose *Same shifts as* a colleague from their team: from their start date until the end of the year they will work the same shifts, with their collective agreement and working hours, and they already appear on the rota. You need to be able to manage work calendars. If the person already has a calendar that year, it is changed from their own.

The collapsible section **Family and emergency** keeps the contact for an emergency (name, relationship and phone), whether they look after a dependent older person, and their children under 18, or adult children with a court-ordered support measure, with their date of birth. The details of their *usual room* only appear for people who appear in the diary.

**Employment history and contract.** With the Employment module, the button *Employment history and contract* on the record keeps each period of employment of the person: if they leave and come back years later, you see them all. Each departure has its **reason** (end of contract, not renewed by the company, voluntary resignation, dismissal, did not pass the probation period, end of seasonal call-up, retirement, death or other) and a note for the circumstances.

**Age and gender of the workforce.** The *Age and gender* in the staff list counts the active workforce by age band (by decades) and by gender, which is what the yearly employment statistics and the equality plan ask for. You can filter by gender, by age or by date of birth, and *Download the list* downloads the people who match the filters into a file for Excel.

**Deactivating does not delete.** The practitioner leaves the agenda, but their appointments, visits and clinical cases still show them, and they can be reactivated by editing them. If they have appointments from today on, Turnera asks which practitioner takes them over and moves them all at once; if one clashes with another the receiver already has, it says so so you can rearrange it afterwards. The list shows only those who are active: by clearing *Active only* the inactive ones show too.

#### The professional's data protection

When a professional is registered, the confirmation offers *Download data protection document to sign*: the information the law requires them to be given about the processing of their data (art. 13 GDPR), already filled in with their name, surnames, ID number, workplace and date, and with the centre's details held in the preferences. **They are not asked for consent** for what the job itself requires —the basis is the contract and the law—: they are informed and sign that they have received it. The only voluntary part is a box to authorise, or not, the use of their image and name towards the public, and refusing has no consequence at all. If the centre has time clocking switched on, the document always includes the notice about location when clocking in with a mobile phone required by article 90 of the LOPDGDD, even if there is no centre zone or clocking yet. What Turnera does not know —the job title, the data protection officer, or the tax ID and contact if they are not in the preferences— appears as a blank to fill in by hand.

The same download is always available in their record, under *Data protection*, where the language of the document can be chosen. When they bring it back signed, *Record signed delivery* records the date, whether or not they authorised their image and, optionally, the scanned paper (PDF, JPEG or PNG, up to 20 MB). Who recorded it and when is kept, and it **is never deleted**: if they change their mind about their image, another delivery is recorded and the latest one counts. The scanned paper also goes in the export of the centre's data. Downloading the document and recording the delivery require the permission to edit professionals.

The document is a **guidance template**: review it with your data protection adviser before using it.

### Working time (clocking in)

Menu → Clock in · Menu → Staff → Working time

**The law requires this.** Article 34.9 of the Spanish Workers' Statute has required since 2019 that a daily working time register is kept, held for four years and made available to the labour inspectorate. Not keeping it is a fine.

**Clock-in number.** In a centre with a large staff nobody wants to look for themselves in a long list. On each member of staff’s record you can set a *Clock-in number* (short, not repeated within the branch). When there are more than eight members of staff, the clock shows a search box at the top: type that number and only their card remains; it also searches by name. The number does not replace the PIN, which is still asked for: it only saves searching. In *Staff → Professionals* you can also search by it.

In *Clock*, in the menu, there are all the professionals of the centre, each with their button *Clock*. The list **only shows names**: who is in and since when is part of the working time record, and anyone walking past reception sees the screen. Each person sees that after typing their PIN, together with what they have to do: *In*, or *Out* and *Break* if they are already in. There is no choosing between in and out: the application knows from that person's last clocking.

**Anyone can open the screen**, and on purpose. A Turnera user is not a person: at the front desk the whole shift usually shares one, and the working-time record belongs to each practitioner. That is why here you press the name instead of looking at who signed in to the application, and why it is **the only place where people clock in**: always as an employee and with their PIN, never as a user.

*[Captura] The clock-in screen: one professional per card. Each person's own opens with their PIN.*

#### The PIN

Every professional needs a **PIN**, four digits unless the centre decides otherwise, which administration resets in *Professionals → Clocking PIN*. Pressing their button brings up a keypad and they type it: with the last digit their screen opens, with nothing else to press. From a computer the PIN can also be typed on the keyboard, numeric keypad included: *Backspace* deletes the last digit and *Enter* confirms. This is what makes pressing someone else’s button not the same as clocking in for them, and without it the list of names would not prove much to an inspector.

The *Reset PIN* button generates a random one, with the digits the centre asks for, and shows it **only once**: tell it to the person, who will have to change it the first time they clock in. Administration cannot choose anyone’s PIN; the only thing it decides is when to reset it.

**Without a PIN there is no clocking in.** Whoever does not have one sees their button disabled, with a notice that administration resets it on their record. That way the register always says who clocked in and not only from which counter, which is what the law expects of a working time register. When you start using the clock in a centre, hand out the PINs first.

The PIN is stored encrypted and **cannot be read again**, not even from administration: if somebody forgets it, it is reset. It is cleared with *Clear the PIN*, and that person cannot clock in until it is reset. Resetting it, clearing it and the person changing it are recorded with who did it, when and from which IP address, and that trail is not deleted.

**Everyone changes their own** from the clocking screen itself: when the keypad comes up, at the bottom there is *Change my PIN*, which asks for the one in force and the new one twice. You have to know the one that is set, and whoever knows it could already clock in for that person, so it opens no door; what it saves is having the change depend on administration being around that morning.

#### How many digits the PIN has

Administration → Time tracking → Time clock

The centre decides whether the PIN has from **four to eight digits**. Four are typed in a moment, and Turnera only allows ten tries a minute and **locks the PIN at the fifth failure in a row** (see Locked PIN), so guessing a colleague's by trying is not worth it. Each extra digit makes it even harder to get right first time. It is changed by whoever has the «Configure the clock-in device» permission, which Management and HR have out of the box.

It applies to the PINs set from then on —the ones administration hands out and the ones each person changes—. **The ones already set keep working**: they can be typed on the keypad and confirmed with the button. If you lower the digits, whoever has a longer one will have to change it from the clock.

The record keeps what it always kept and one thing more: the working day belongs to the professional who typed their PIN, and next to it stands **which user it was recorded from**. They are two different facts on purpose, and together they are what you show when somebody asks.

#### Temporary PIN

Staff → Professionals → Clocking PIN

A PIN that **administration resets** is **temporary**, because whoever reset it knows it. With it the person cannot clock in, neither on the web nor in the professionals’ app, nor sign their monthly summary: when they type it, Turnera asks them to **choose their own** (different from the temporary one and from the digits the centre requires), and from then on it is theirs alone. That way HR never gets to know the PIN someone signs with. PINs that were already set before this improvement are left untouched.

#### Locked PIN

Staff → Professionals → Clocking PIN

If **a wrong PIN is typed five times in a row**, that person's PIN becomes **locked**. Failures add up wherever they happen —the web time clock, the professionals' app and the monthly summary signing link— and the lock applies to all of them: while locked, the PIN does not work to clock in or to sign, even if the right one is typed afterwards. A correct PIN before the fifth failure resets the count. On the third and fourth failure the screen says how many attempts are left, and when it locks it says «The PIN of *name* is locked after too many wrong attempts. Ask HR to unlock it».

When it locks, **an alert reaches the HR email** (the work calendar one; if there is none, the summary signing one, and failing that, the administration one) saying who, when and from where: the clock, with the user and IP it was typed from, or the signing link, with its IP.

**Anyone at reception can lock a colleague's PIN** by deliberately typing five random PINs under their name. It cannot be prevented —without the lock, the PIN could be guessed by trying—, so when the alert arrives it is best to **unlock it straight away** and, if it was not that person, reset their PIN and find out who was at the desk.

In *Staff*, the button *clocking PIN* of anyone whose PIN is locked shows a warning, and inside it shows **«PIN locked since …»** with the button **Unlock**, which keeps the same PIN and resets the count. It needs the «Unlock the clocking PIN» permission, which Management, HR and Staff have out of the box (and the System administrator); without it, the window says which permission is missing. **Resetting the PIN also unlocks it** (and it is temporary, as always when administration resets it). Every lock and every unlock is recorded —who unlocked it, when and how— and that trail is not deleted.

#### Signing the monthly summary

Administration → Attendance control → Monthly summary signing

If it is switched on, on the **1st of every month**, with the month already closed, every professional with an email address receives a link to their summary of hours. Opening the link and entering **the PIN they clock in with** they see their days and the month’s total, tick the two boxes —that they have read it and that they accept what it says is correct— and **sign with their finger**. The signed PDF, with the date and time, reaches their email and the **HR email address** given in the same section (it cannot be switched on without it).

The link is valid for 30 days and can be signed only once. If someone does not sign, **it is not sent to them again by email on its own**; even so, the time clock reminds them every time they type their PIN (below). Anyone who still has the provisional PIN has to change it at the time clock before they can sign.

If **a wrong PIN is typed five times in a row** on the link, the link is cancelled and an alert reaches the HR email: it may be a slip, or the link may have reached the wrong person. Those failures **also count for the PIN**, added to the clock's: at the fifth in a row the PIN is locked and cannot be used to clock in or to open another link until HR unlocks it (see Locked PIN). So asking for a new link does not give new attempts.

In the same section, below the HR email, is **How each month's signing is going**: pick the month and each professional appears with the state of their link —not sent, waiting for signature, signed, expired or **cancelled after wrong PINs**—. If someone's link expired, got lost or was cancelled, press **Resend link** on their row: they get a new email, just like the one on the 1st, and the previous link stops working. It can be resent at most **five times per person and month**, and never for a summary already signed or to someone with no email in their record.

**It can also be signed at the centre's time clock.** When the month is already closed in the work calendar, whoever has their summary unsigned sees it as soon as they type their PIN to clock in (see What each person sees after their PIN): *Review and sign* opens the same review as the link —the days, the total, the two boxes and the signature with a finger— and it is recorded just the same, with the channel *signed at the centre time clock*. This way, **whoever has no email** can also sign, which they could not before: their signed PDF goes only to the HR email. The time clock offers the last three closed months that are still unsigned, from the oldest to the newest. In *How each month's signing is going* and in the send to the payroll advisor you can see where each person signed.

#### Where clocking in happens from

Administration → Attendance control → Centre zone

When someone clocks in with the phone, Turnera records **where from** it was done, so that whoever reviews the record can see whether it was at the centre or away. **It never prevents clocking in**: some people go out to another premises, to a home or to a course, and clocking in is the centre’s obligation. If the device does not give the location, or the person does not allow it, they clock in all the same and the entry is left as “no location”.

The place is taken **only at the moment of clocking in**; nobody is tracked and nothing is looked at between clockings. It is not part of the working-time record: not in the PDFs, not in the spreadsheets, not in what is shown to the Inspectorate with a link. It is stored separately and only whoever has the permission “See where it was clocked” (by default, Management and HR) sees it, in a column *Where* of the list of clockings: *At the centre*, *Away from the centre* with the distance, or *Location saved* if the centre has not yet defined its zone.

The **centre zone** is a point with a radius (150 m by default, up to 5 km). Whoever has the permission “Configure the clock” sets it while at the centre with *Use my current location as the centre*, changes the radius with *Save radius* or removes it with *Remove the zone*. The phones belong to the centre, shared or not; each professional clocks in with their PIN.

Turnera also records **from which device** clocking in is done, and in the same column *Where* it is seen by whoever has the permission «See where clocking was done». If clocking is done from a shared device of the centre (the tablet at the counter), it shows *Device: Counter*. If clocking is done with a mobile phone browser, the browser stores a random code in itself, which is not any data of the phone, and the list marks *New device* when that person had already clocked in from other devices and never from this one. A person’s first device is not marked, nor is a shared device, because the centre itself registered it. **It never prevents clocking in**: it is a hint for whoever reviews, not proof, and if the browser clears its data the device shows up as new again. It is stored apart from the working-hours register, like the location, and the staff privacy notice mentions it.

#### What each person sees after their PIN

The right PIN opens a window with that person's own information, which **goes back to the list by itself after a minute** if nothing is touched, and as soon as they clock:

- **Whether they are in or out**, and since what time, or whether they are on a break.
- **In**, or **Out** and **Break** if they are in. A break is time that is not working time (lunch, for instance): it is clocked with *Break* when leaving and with *Back from the break* when coming back. It adds no hours and shows in the record between the two stretches of the day.
- **The hours HR has recorded for them** for a forgotten clocking and that they have not said anything about yet. With *I agree* or *I do not agree*, explaining why in this case, their answer is recorded next to those hours, and it cannot be changed afterwards (see *Incidents*, further down).
- **My records**: their month, day by day, with the breaks, the hours and the total, and the previous months. They can **email it to themselves** as a PDF or download it.
- **The unsigned summary of the month**, if there is one: “You have the summary of September 2026 unsigned”, with *Review and sign*. While they review and sign, the window **does not come back to the list on its own**: signing takes a while. See Signing the monthly summary.

In *Staff → Working time*, in the menu, is everything else: choose a range and, if you like, a person. At the top come the **totals of each person and month**: days recorded, planned hours, worked, ordinary and overtime according to their work calendar, the overtime paid or given back as rest that month, and how many hours declared by HR are awaiting their agreement or have their disagreement. #### Access for the labour inspectorate and the worker representatives

At the end of *Time and attendance*, whoever has the permission “Give access to the working time record” creates **read-only links** to give to the **labour inspectorate** or to the **worker representatives**, without creating a user for them. You choose who it is for, write exactly who (for instance, the inspector's name and the number of the request) and how many days it is valid, up to 90.

Whoever opens it sees the record of any period of the last four years, a year at most each time, and can download the same files. The representatives see it **without the tax ID**, for data protection. The link **is only shown when it is created**: copy it then and send it. If the person is present, below the link comes its **QR code**: they scan it with the phone camera and open it without it having to be dictated. It can be withdrawn before it expires, and each visit is logged with when, from where and what was looked at.

This is what the draft Royal Decree on working time records being prepared by the Spanish Ministry of Labour asks for: immediate and remote access for the labour inspectorate and access for the worker representatives. Until it is approved, it is enough to be able to show the record when asked; the link makes that easier.

#### The monthly summary by email

In each professional's record, whoever has the permission “Monthly working time summary by email” (by default, HR and Management) ticks *Email them the summary of their working time every month*. From then on, at the start of each month they get the PDF of the previous month. Their record needs an email. It is the copy the law requires handing over with the payslip to part-time workers, and that the draft Royal Decree extends to everyone.

#### A clocking is not corrected

An entry **is not edited, not deleted and not corrected**: it stays as it was. That is exactly what makes this record worth something, because one that could be rewritten would prove nothing. When someone forgets to clock, HR opens an **incident** in their work calendar that explains what happened and declares from what time to what time they actually worked (see *Incidents*, below). *[Captura] Clockings one by one, as they were clocked. Those corrected before corrections were removed are still shown, marked.*

#### The days that come out marked

If somebody clocks in and leaves without clocking out, that day comes out as an **open shift** and **with no hours**. It is not given a made-up closing time on purpose: a record with hours nobody clocked is worse than an incomplete one, because the incomplete one shows and the made-up one does not. The entry stays open, and after **16 hours** it is taken as forgotten: that person's next clocking is a clock-in again, not the clock-out of the day before. For that day to count, HR declares the hours in an incident.

A shift that crosses midnight is counted whole on the day it started, which is how payroll counts it, and what is worked after coming back from a break still belongs to that day. A break with no return within 16 hours is left without an end: none is made up.

#### The centre's clock

A clocking is not a time somebody writes, like an appointment's: it is the exact moment the button is pressed. To know which day and which stretch it belongs to, the application needs to know which time zone the centre lives in, and it comes set to **Europe/Madrid**. It is the time zone of the whole installation: that of the application, that of the database and that of “today”. A centre outside mainland Spain goes on its own installation with the variable `TURNERA_TZ` set at installation time, and it is not changed afterwards.

### Work calendar

Menu → Staff → Work calendar

The work calendar spreads over the year **the hours set by the agreement** of each professional: which days they work and on which shift, their holidays, their absences and the public holidays. It is kept by whoever has the role **HR** (or the permissions «Ver los calendarios laborales» and «Planificar los calendarios laborales»). It is a plan: what was really worked is still what was clocked, and the screen compares one with the other.

Above the calendar you switch between **Planning** (what has been planned) and **Actual** (what has been clocked). They are two different things: someone working on a holiday is not planned, it is clocked, and those hours are not regular but overtime.

#### What is set up once

- **Agreement**: set by the centre, it is **one a year for the whole staff** and all its sites: the name, the working hours per year for a full-time job and the holiday days, calendar days (the 30 of the Workers' Statute) or working days (not counting weekends or public holidays). Each calendar takes the one for its year; it is not chosen person by person. When creating it, the *Agreement* drop-down offers the most common ones: hairdressers and beauty, sports facilities and gyms, care for dependent people, veterinary centres, care homes in the Valencian Community, and private healthcare and offices in the province of Valencia. It suggests their hours and holidays, which can be changed before saving, and shows the main points, its validity and the official text. It is an indicative summary: what was signed is confirmed by your employment adviser. If yours is not there, choose *Another agreement* and fill it in by hand.
- **Shifts**: «Morning 9-14», «Afternoon 15-21»… One that ends before it starts is a night shift and finishes the next day. **A day takes a single shift**: for someone who works morning and afternoon, create a split shift, with *Add stretch (split shift)* or with *Start as a split shift*, which sets it to 09:00-14:00 and 16:00-20:00 for you to adjust. The break is the gap between one stretch and the next, and does not count as worked.
- **Absence types**: sick leave, paid leave, unpaid leave… Each one says whether that day counts as worked for the agreement hours. For a sick leave only that it is a sick leave is written down, never the diagnosis.
- **Public holidays**: in *Settings* the region of the centre is chosen, and in *Public holidays* the button brings the national ones and those of that region for the year.

*[Captura] The centre’s shifts. A split one has its two stretches, and the break between them does not count as worked.*

**Local public holidays are not imported.** Nobody publishes the two of each town reliably, so they have to be added by hand in *Public holidays → Add local holiday*. The screen shows how many local ones are set: if it says zero, they are missing.

#### The calendar of each professional

In *Calendars* lists every professional of the centre with how theirs stands in the chosen year. *Create* takes the agreement of the year and asks for the workday (100 % full time, 50 % half time) and, if they fall within the year, the **start and leaving** dates: with them hours and holidays are prorated automatically, which is what a final settlement needs. Part-timers get the same holidays and half the hours.

*[Captura] The year’s calendars: how many hours the collective agreement sets for each person, how many are planned and how many are left to allocate or are over.*

To spread the year, choose at the top what to put —a shift, holidays, an absence or *Free* to remove— and you click a day, or drag from one day to another to mark all those in between. Each day has one thing only: if it has a shift it cannot also be vacation. **Nothing is planned before the start date or after the end date**: those days do not count towards the hours, so the screen skips them and says how many. When you choose what to put, the days that already have it **are highlighted** and the rest are dimmed, and next to the button it says how many there are: you see at a glance where each shift falls. In the list of calendars and in the roster you can search by first name, surname or the payroll code of each member of staff. The roster is also filtered by position and by location, and more than one of each can be chosen at a time. *Fill by weekdays* puts it in one go on, for example, Mondays to Fridays from January to June, skipping public holidays and without touching what is already there. With *Repeats every* 2, 3 or 4 weeks, each week of the cycle has its own days: this is for **teams that take turns**, for example one week Tuesday, Thursday and Friday and the next Monday, Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday. Team 1 gets the cycle starting on week 1 and team 2 the same cycle starting on week 2, so one covers what the other does not; then it is copied to the rest of each team with *Copy to others*. On the right, **the figures are redone with each change**: agreement hours, planned hours, those still to be distributed (or those in excess), vacation days set against those owed and how many are left, and, up to yesterday, what was planned against what was clocked.

Below are the **warnings** of the Spanish Workers' Statute: fewer than 12 hours of rest between two working days, more than 9 hours in a day, more than 6 hours in a row without a 15-minute break, fewer than 3 days off in 14, a shift on a public holiday, more vacation than is owed, or days marked outside the start and end dates. If someone works at night (from 22:00 to 06:00) it also warns that the Statute treats it as night work, with a maximum average of 8 hours over 15 days and no more than two weeks in a row on night shift, unless voluntary. Each warning carries the link *Read the article*, which opens the text of the Statute so you can check it. Saving an agreement with fewer than 30 calendar days of vacation (22 working days) also shows the warning. These are warnings and do not prevent saving: some agreements allow otherwise, and what was signed is for HR to know.

*Duplicate to another professional* copies the calendar as it is to others in the centre (if they already had one that year, it is replaced, and the screen warns first). *Download PDF* puts the year on one sheet, to pin on the notice board or give to the accountant.

#### Request vacation

Requesting vacation is **switched on by HR** at each location: in *Work calendar → Settings*, *Professionals can request vacation*. It comes switched off. While it is off, nobody can request days, neither from the web nor from the mobile app, and *My calendar* it does not appear in the menu; if it is switched off with pending requests, HR can still approve or reject them and each professional can cancel their own.

Once switched on, each professional with a linked user can request vacation from *My calendar*, if they have the permission *Request vacation days* (Reception, Professional, Management, HR and Maintenance come with it by default). Click on the calendar the days with a shift that you want, from tomorrow onwards —one single day or several, consecutive or not—, and they are marked **marked** with a border in the brand colour; clicking a day again unmarks it. Below the calendar it shows how many are marked, *Clear selection* and **Request N days**, which shows the days and how many will be left if it is approved, before sending. The days sent stay **requested**, with a dashed green border, and remain working days until HR replies. Next to it appears *My vacations*: the days left of those the collective agreement grants, each request with its days and its status, and *Cancel* to withdraw in full the one still awaiting a reply.

A request goes **complete or not at all**: if any day cannot be requested —it has passed, has no shift, is already requested or belongs to a closed month—, none is requested and the notice says which days fail and why. Nor can more days be requested than remain: what is requested and pending already counts as spent. If the collective agreement counts vacation in working days, the marked Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays do not use up days.

The same can be done from the **mobile app**, in *My vacations*, after typing the PIN: the balance, a calendar by months where the days with a shift are tapped, *Request N days* and the list of requests with their status, the reason if they were rejected and *Cancel* on the pending ones. The menu shows it if the user the app was signed in with can request vacation. If that user is linked to a professional (*Administration → Users → Professional*), the app belongs to that person: on opening it does not show the list of colleagues but goes straight to their PIN screen, and after the PIN to their usual panel. An unlinked user (the counter’s) remains a shared device, with the list of everyone. Once the PIN has been typed (for example to clock in), opening another menu option does not ask for it again while the pass remains valid (a few minutes). The language, «Sign out» and «Change centre» are in *Settings*, at the end of the menu, folded away.

HR receives an email for each request, with consecutive days grouped, and in *Work calendar*, above the sections, there appears *Pending vacation requests* with who is requesting, their days and the days they have left. Each request is decided **in full**: **Approve** paints all its days as vacation in their calendar, just as if they were marked by hand. **Reject** leaves them as they were and accepts a reason. In both cases, the professional receives an email with the reply if they have one in their record. If on approving they have no days left, it is approved anyway and the screen warns of it: as with the rest of the notices of the Statute, HR decides. In a closed month or year nothing is requested or approved.

#### Closing the year

When a year has ended and has been reviewed, in *Calendars*, with that year selected, *Close* leaves it **as it is, for the labour inspection**: from then on nothing of that year changes in the centre — not the calendars or their days, the incidents, the compensations, the holidays or that year's agreement. It can be viewed and printed. If a shift that year used is edited later, the closed year **keeps the hours it had** (Turnera keeps a retired copy of the shift), and the out-of-shift hours margin that applies is the one in force when it was closed. You confirm by typing the year and **cannot be reopened**. A year cannot be closed before **1 May of the following year**: until 30 April rest days can still be given for its out-of-shift hours (see below).

#### Close each month for payroll

In *Closings*, with the year chosen at the top, the months of the year appear with their status. When a month is sent to the accountancy firm for payroll, *Close the month* leaves it **as it was sent**: while it is closed its incidents cannot be changed, nor the days of the calendars for that month, the compensations dated in it, or its holidays. Nor anything that changes several months at once and affects it: the calendar’s working hours, moving the start or end date past it, duplicating or deleting that year’s calendar. Clock-ins were never touched. If a shift that month used is edited afterwards, the closed month **keeps the schedule it had**, just like a closed year.

A month is closed from day 1 of the following one; the one that is *In progress* is not yet. Unlike the year, **a month can be reopened**, because a payroll gets regularised: *Reopen* asks for the reason, and it is written in the list with the date and who did it. When the correction is finished it is closed again. If the year is already closed, its months can be neither closed nor reopened.

**The collective agreement is not locked when a month is closed**, because it is often signed with the year already under way. Changing its hours for the year changes the figures of the whole year, closed months included: if that happens, reopen them and tell the accountancy firm.

#### What was done: normal hours and out-of-shift hours

In *Actual* each clocked day is coloured: green if it was all **regular hours** and orange if there was **out-of-shift hours**. Hovering over it shows the hours clocked. The rules:

- Time clocked **within the shift** planned is regular time.
- Time clocked **outside the shift** counts as out-of-shift hours only if it goes beyond the **margin** of the centre (15 minutes by default, changed in *Settings*): coming in five minutes early is not overtime. If it goes past the margin, all of it counts: whoever stays forty minutes has done forty.
- Everything clocked on a **holiday**, on vacation, in an absence or on a day without a shift is out-of-shift hours.
- A clock-in without a clock-out adds nothing until HR declares the hours in an incident.
- A night shift, which crosses midnight, is counted whole on the day it began. On the night the clocks change it counts the hours that really went by: nine in October and seven in March for a shift from 22:00 to 06:00.

*[Captura] The same year in *Actual*: in green what was clocked within the shift, in orange the days with hours outside the shift, such as a public holiday worked, and the “!” of an incident.*

Out-of-shift hours go to the professional's **overtime bank** on the right. Whoever has the permission «Compensate out-of-shift hours» (by default HR and Management) deducts them with *Compensate hours*, as **paid in payroll** or as **compensatory rest**. The pending balance is what is left to compensate. A rest day compensates **the oldest hours first**.

Turnera does not decide whether some out-of-shift hours are *extraordinary*: that is for HR to decide according to the contract and the agreement. What it does watch is the cap the Workers' Statute sets on extraordinary hours, **80 hours a year** (art. 35.2; for a part-time contract, proportional to its working hours). If the year's out-of-shift hours, not counting those returned as rest **within the deadline**, exceed that cap, a warning appears. The deadline is **four months** from when the hours were worked (art. 35.1): the rest for September's hours can be given in January. That is why the date of a compensation can fall as late as **30 April of the following year**. If a rest day arrives after the deadline, it is saved anyway, marked *out of time* , and those hours still count towards the cap. It is a warning: Turnera does not prevent recording anything.

#### Incidents

With the permission «Record incidents» (by default, HR and Management) *Incident* appears next to the shifts. With it selected, clicking a day opens its incidents: you write what happened and can upload **up to 3 photos**. The incident sits **on top of** whatever the day has —a shift, vacation— without changing it, and the day is marked with a red **«!»** red. They can be corrected and deleted (except those that declare hours, below), and they show who wrote them down and when. They belong to HR only: the professional does not see them in *My calendar*.

**A forgotten clocking is fixed with an incident.** When writing it, tick *Declare the actual working hours (forgotten clocking)* and enter from what time to what time that person worked (if the end time is earlier than the start, it is a shift that ends the next day). The clocking is not touched: those hours are added to what was clocked that day, the day no longer shows as an open working day and normal and overtime hours are counted with that. It cannot overlap what is already clocked that day, because that already counts.

An incident that declares hours is part of the time record, so it **cannot be changed or deleted**, nor can photos be removed from it (they can be added). If it was wrong, open another one for the same day: the new one is the one that counts and the previous one stays visible, marked as *Replaced*. When it is saved, the professional receives an **email** with the day, the declared hours and the explanation, so they know what is on their record.

Also, the next time they type their PIN at the time clock, they are asked whether they **agree** with those hours. Until they answer, the incident shows as *Awaiting agreement*; if they say no, it shows as *Disagreed* with their reason, here, in the time and attendance and in everything downloaded. The hours count the same in both cases: if there is no agreement, the record reflects what the company declared and the person's disagreement, which is what the draft Royal Decree on working time records asks for.

Today is highlighted and marks who is **in right now**, according to what was clocked. Whoever has no work calendar is not shown in the roster.

At the top there is a search by first name or surname, and the staff can be sorted by *First name* or by *Surname*; by surname, each person appears as «Surname, First name». The same in the list of calendars. The chosen order is remembered in that browser and applies to both screens, also when printing the roster.

#### Partial absences

A stretch closed for one person (*Agenda → Close a stretch*) counts as a partial absence in their work calendar. If you ticked *Deduct from hours*, the hours summary adds the column **deducted**: those minutes are subtracted from the **regular** hours of the day (the clocked time is not touched), and also from the agreement calculation and the year-end projection. If it was not ticked, it shows in the calendar as an informational absence. It only deducts what falls inside the shift of a working day; on a public holiday or off shift it deducts nothing. The monthly working-time record and the CSVs also carry the deducted minutes.

**In the year calendar every partial absence has a dot on its day**: filled if it deducts, hollow if it is only informational. Hovering shows the hours. Whoever can close stretches in the agenda can also **add them from here**, with *Add partial absence* next to the summary of what was worked: you choose the day, the hours, the reason and, if you have permission, whether it deducts. It is the same closure as in the agenda, so that stretch is not offered for booking either. To remove one, use the bin on its row: it also reopens the stretch in the agenda and gives the hours back if they had been deducted. In a closed year they can be neither added nor removed.

#### The year-end projection

Below each professional's calendar, a chart accumulates what is planned month by month, what has been clocked up to today and, **at their current pace**, how much it would add up to at year end, against the hours of the agreement. If at that pace they would reach the hours of the agreement before the year ends, it says on which day: the moment to give them vacation or to stop counting on that person for more hours. Only whoever plans sees it, not the professional.

#### Forgotten clock-in warnings

If, according to their calendar, someone should have come in and after the **margin for out-of-shift hours** they have not clocked in, or should have left and after the margin are still in, an **email** goes out. With the factory margin that is 15 minutes; a notice never goes out before 15, even if the margin is smaller. That way, whoever stays a few extra minutes within the margin, which is still a regular working day, does not get a false notice. In the *Settings* of the work calendar you decide whether to notify, and whom: the professional (at the email in their record), HR or both. That is also where you enter the **HR person's email**; if left empty, HR notices go to the centre's management email (*Preferences*). A shift gives at most one clock-in and one clock-out notice. No notices on public holidays. When the mobile app exists, the notice will reach it too.

#### In the diary

In the per-professional views, the agenda shades in grey, with white stripes, whatever is **outside their shift** and days of holiday, absence or bank holiday. Off-shift time carries no text on top: hovering over it says *Off shift*. Holidays, absences and bank holidays do have it written. At first it only marks it and still lets you book. If in *Work calendar → Settings* you switch on *The work calendar blocks the diary*, it no longer lets you book or move an appointment outside the shift. Those without a calendar that year are not affected.

#### What each practitioner sees

Every professional has the right to know their schedule and vacation without needing any permission, and to see what they have clocked, their regular and overtime hours and their hours bank, but not the incidents or the projection. This is meant for the professional’s app. On the web, *My calendar* only appears in the menu for whoever signs in with their user linked to a professional (for example with their PIN at the shared device) and has the permission *Request vacation days*: the office user belongs to no professional. From there vacation is requested (see Request vacation).

### Payslips and certificates

Menu → Staff → Payslips and certificates

The payroll advisor prepares the payslips; Turnera **distributes them**. You upload what the advisor sends, Turnera separates each practitioner's document by their ID number, HR checks who each one goes to and delivers it. Each person opens it in Turnera and **confirms they have received it**, and HR can see at any moment who has it and who has not. The same goes for the **tax withholding certificate** for each year.

It is part of the **Labour**module: a section that the centre has or does not have depending on what it has contracted, and which always goes together with Staff. It needs the permission “Distribute payslips and certificates”, which Management and HR have by default. It is a sensitive permission: whoever has it can open anyone's payslip, and every time they do it is recorded who and when.

#### Upload and review

1. Choose *Payslips* or *Tax withholding certificates*, and the month or the year.
2. In *Choose files*, whatever the payroll advisor has sent: **one PDF with all of them**, **several PDFs** (one per person) or **a zip**. Then, *Upload and split*.
3. Turnera splits them and shows the result, without delivering anything yet:
  - In a PDF with everyone, it reads the ID number on each page. Consecutive pages with the same ID number are a single payslip, and a page with no ID number goes with the previous one (two-page payslips). The company's tax ID is not mistaken for anyone's.
  - In a file with the ID number in its name (`12345678Z.pdf`, `payslip_12345678Z_october.pdf`), the whole file belongs to that person, and it is also checked that the file **inside** shows the same ID number.
4. Whatever is in doubt appears at the top, in orange, and **is not delivered to anyone** until it is assigned by hand or discarded: an ID number that belongs to no practitioner, a page with no ID number, a file whose name says one ID number and inside says another, or a scanned PDF with no text. With *View* the document is opened so it can be checked.
5. Below it says who has been left out **no document** of that upload, in case anyone is missing, and who has **more than one** (a separate extra payment, for example).

If something else for the same month is uploaded before delivering, it is added to the same upload.

Each recognised document says **how it will reach them** to that person: *My documents* if they have a user, *Link by email* if they have an email on their record and a clock-in PIN, or *No digital route: in person* if they have neither. Those handed over in person are listed at the top, with their name, so that their record can be completed (email and PIN, or a linked user) **before** delivering. If there is no time, it is delivered anyway and afterwards marked as *Handed over on paper*.

#### Having the payroll advisor upload them

Instead of waiting for the payroll advisor to send the payslips and uploading them, they can be asked to upload them themselves: *Ask the payroll advisor*, below *Upload and split*, sends them an email with a link for that month (or that year, if they are certificates). The email fills itself in with that of the payroll advisor who prepares the payslips, if it is set in the hours submission, and it can be changed.

- The advisor uploads without a username or password: one PDF with everyone, several PDFs or a zip, and can upload more than once while the link is valid (10 days).
- What they upload goes to that month's batch **not yet delivered**: HR reviews it and delivers it as usual. The advisor sees nothing of what was uploaded, nor who each document goes to.
- Every time they upload, an email reaches HR with how many documents have arrived and from where.
- Below the button you can see the state of the latest link (when it was sent, until when it is valid and how much has been uploaded) and you can *Cancel*. Asking for another for the same month cancels the previous one.

#### Deliver

*Deliver* sends the whole batch, or nothing: with a single unreviewed document none goes out. On delivering:

- Whoever has an email on their record receives a notice with a **link**, never with the payslip attached: an email that reaches the wrong person does not leak salary, ID number or bank account. The link asks for the PIN the person clocks in with and expires after 30 days. The notice always goes to their personal email, never to the centre's.
- At the end, Turnera says how many have been notified by email and how many have to be **handed over in person**; in the follow-up, those appear with *No digital route: hand it over in person* until they are marked.
- Whoever signs in with their user, on the web or in the Android app, sees it in My documents.
- At **3 and 7 days** whoever has not confirmed is reminded by email. After that it is up to HR.
- If the person already had a payslip for that month, the new one **replaces**it: the previous one is not deleted and its acknowledgement stays attached to it.

#### Who has it

In the delivered batch each person is shown with their status: *Not opened*, *Opened, not confirmed*, *Received*, *On paper* or *Refused to sign*. In *Details* everything that has happened with the document is recorded (delivered, notified, opened, confirmed, with whom, how they identified themselves and from where) and the **fingerprint** of the PDF delivered, which makes it possible to check afterwards that what was confirmed is exactly what was stored.

- **Receipt**: a PDF with all of the above, to show if it is ever needed.
- **Notify again**: another email, with a new link.
- **Handed over on paper**: for when it is handed over in person; the day is recorded.
- **Refused to sign**: it was delivered and the person did not want to confirm it; who was present is recorded as a witness, as is done on paper.

## 11. The clinical record

In the centres that use it, each customer has a history and cases, and each case its progress notes. It prints to PDF.

**Every time someone opens a clinical record it is logged**: who, when and what they looked at. It is not surveillance, it is what the law requires for health data, and it is what you have to be able to show if anyone asks.

Seeing the clinical record is a separate permission from seeing the customer record. Whoever is at the front desk has no need for it.

### Deleting or annulling an evolution

An evolution is only **deleted** on the same day and by whoever wrote it: it is for removing one noted on the wrong record. After that day, or if someone else wrote it, it is **annulled**, giving the reason. An annulled evolution does not disappear: it stays in the case, struck through, with who annulled it, when and why. It can no longer be changed nor its photos touched, and it is left out when the case is printed. That way the clinical history always says who noted what and when, which is what the law requires.

### Marking where it hurts: the anatomical map

Customer record → Clinical history → New case (or Edit case)

Below the case form is the **anatomical map**: the whole body, front and back, in four layers -- *Muscle*, *Bone*, *Skin (male)* and *Skin (female)*.

- **Moving the mouse** over the body highlights what is underneath and tells you its name and side: «Descending part of trapezius muscle · right side», «Lumbar region · left side». On the skin you get the regions of the body; on the muscle layer, each muscle and the bones that show; on the bone layer, each bone.
- **A click** marks it. The zone is added to the list on the right with its number, and there you note whether it hurts and how much, from 0 to 10, with the same green-to-red bar as the progress notes. On the map, each marked zone is tinted with the colour of its pain.
- Several zones can be marked, on different layers and views. Clicking one in the list takes the map to the layer and view where it was marked.
- The **mouse wheel** or the magnifier buttons zoom in up to six times; with the body enlarged, drag to move it. That way the small muscles of the hand, the foot or the face are easy to mark.
- The side is always the patient's: in the front view, their right side is on the left of the screen.

The zones are saved with the case. When it is opened later, the map shows them already tinted with the list beside it, and the PDF report lists them with their name, side and pain, in the patient's language.

Older cases that had a point on the picture of their line of activity keep it as it was, with their usual picture. The anatomical map is for new cases and for those that had no point.

The images are generated from *Z-Anatomy* (CC BY-SA 4.0), *BodyParts3D* from the Database Center for Life Science (CC BY 4.0) and the *Human Reference Atlas* by HuBMAP (CC BY 4.0), and are published under the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence.

### Printing a case

On the case page, *PDF report* produces the whole case to give to the patient or send to another specialist: the medical background, the case details and each progress note in its own block, from oldest to newest, with the date, who saw the patient, symptoms, examination, diagnosis, treatment, pain, notes and its photos. Every page carries a footer saying it is a confidential document with health data, and its number.

Printing is a separate permission, *Print the clinical record*, which the *Professional* role and the administrator have out of the box; reception does not. Whoever does not have it does not see the button. Every print is recorded in the access log.

### The pain of each session

When a case is opened and in each progress note you can tick **There is pain**. Ticking it shows a bar from 0 to 10, from green to red: 0 is no pain and 10 the worst imaginable, the scale doctors and physiotherapists use. If it is not ticked, no pain is noted, which is not the same as a zero: a zero says it was asked and it did not hurt. On the case page each progress note carries its pain in a label of the same colour, so you can see at a glance whether it is easing, and it also appears in the PDF.

### Photos in the progress notes

Each progress note takes **up to three photos**, to follow by eye how an injury, a scar or a posture changes. They are added with *Add photo* in the progress note itself; from a tablet or a phone the photo can be taken there and then. They are uploaded when you press *Save*, and removed with the cross on each one.

- Turnera reduces each photo before uploading it, and strips what the camera writes inside, such as where it was taken.
- They are health data: only those who can see the clinical record see them, and they travel with the backup and with the export of the centre's data.
- On the case page they appear small under each progress note; click one to see it full size.

### Medicines in the treatment

Above the treatment, in the case and in each progress note, there is *Add a medicine to the treatment*. With three letters (*ibupro*, *enantyum*) it suggests the medicines marketed in Spain that start that way, with their active ingredient, the laboratory and whether they need a prescription. Picking one adds it on a line of the treatment, ready to write the dosage after it.

It is a writing aid, not a prescription: the treatment is still free text. The list comes from CIMA, the medicines information centre of the AEMPS (the Spanish medicines agency), checked on the spot, so it is always up to date. Only what is typed of the medicine's name reaches the AEMPS, nothing about the patient. If one day it does not answer, Turnera says so and the treatment is written by hand as always.

### In a training centre: following the student

A club, an academy or a gym does not keep clinical records, so Turnera does not show them. In each student's record there is their **follow-up**: the *programmes* they have open, each with its *goal*, the *starting point*, the *physical assessment* and the *training plan*, and the coach in charge.

Inside each programme you write the **session notes**: what was worked on and how it went, with up to three photos or screenshots if they help (a posture, a technical gesture). When the goal is met, the programme is marked as *finished*. There is no medical background, no body map, no pain scale and no medicines: that belongs to clinics. The permission is called *See the follow-up* and the programme prints to PDF the same way.

## 12. Maintenance

If the centre has the **Maintenance** module, it appears in the menu, inside *Catalogue*. Each fault or job is an **order**: with a title, objective, description, zone and equipment, type, priority and due date. An order goes through *open, assigned, in progress, waiting, resolved and closed*, or is annulled with a reason. Everything that happens is kept in the **history** of the order, with its author and time, and it cannot be deleted or changed.

Each order gets **photos per phase** (problem, before, during, after and document), up to ten per phase; each photo keeps who uploaded it, when, and its fingerprint, so nobody can claim it was changed. In addition, **Attach document** uploads a file as it is (PDF, Word, Excel, a spreadsheet, a text, an image or a ZIP, up to 15 MB and up to ten per order): it is for the **work report of an outside contractor**, a quote or a certificate. It downloads when clicked, appears in the order's history and the inspectorate can also see it with its link. There are eight permissions (view, open orders, manage, close, cancel, zones and equipment, export and links). It comes with its own role, **Maintenance**, which handles orders, their zones and their equipment but does not annul orders or give links to the Inspectorate; *Management* has everything except the links, and the *System administrator* has those too. Reception, Practitioner and HR can view and report.

**Links for the Inspectorate:** from the screen itself you create a read-only link (1 to 90 days) that shows the orders, their history and photos and lets you download CSV, with no user account. It can be revoked whenever you like and every access is logged. Orders can also be exported to CSV from the list.

### Who handles an order, and releasing it

An order is handled by one person (or one external provider) at a time. Whoever manages the orders **assigns it** from the order; and whoever works in Maintenance can **take it** one that is unassigned, without waiting to be given it. By taking it, it becomes *assigned*; it goes on its way with *In progress*, *On hold* and *Resolve*.

If someone cannot carry on (the shift ends, they fall ill, the job needs someone else), they do not leave it forgotten: they open the order’s **More** menu, choose **Release order** and explain **where the work stands**. The reason is mandatory. The order keeps its history, its photos and its status, is left **without anyone responsible** and someone else can take it, or the manager can assign it again. An order that was *assigned* goes back to *open*; one *in progress* or *on hold* stays as it was, so that it is clear work has already been done. In the history it is noted as *Released*, with who was handling it and what they wrote. Only a pending order that has someone responsible can be released: everyone releases their own, and whoever has the permission *Manage* can release someone else’s.

### From the phone: the professionals’ app

Maintenance staff do not have to sit in front of the computer. In the professionals’ **app** (Android), under the *Maintenance*option, each person taps their name and types their **PIN**, the same one they clock in with. They see the list of orders with their priority, due date and who is handling them (overdue ones in red); in each order they see the history and the photos, **take photos by stage** directly with the camera, take an unassigned order and release it with a reason if they cannot finish it. What is done in the app goes into the same history as what is done on the web, with the person who typed their PIN as the author. Next to it, in brackets, the history also records **the user the session was open with** (“from user …”) when it is not that person’s own, so you know which phone it was done from. A user linked to a member of staff only acts as that person: they cannot take, move forward, comment on or photograph an order on someone else’s behalf, nor release someone else’s without the permission to *Manage*. The app’s manual, screen by screen, is in section 14.

## 12 bis. Activations: doors and devices with a QR code

Designed for what is closed or switched off and someone needs a person on the team to open or switch it on: a sports hall door in winter, the lights of a court, a device. It is a module that is **switched on per centre** (it comes switched off); with it off there is no menu or public page. It appears in the menu as *Activations*.

### How it works

1. A **point** (for example “Sports hall door”) is registered and its **QR** code is printed to stick on the door.
2. Whoever scans it sees the centre’s logo and name, to know they are in the right place, and writes their **name** and a **code**. The sentence explaining which code is requested (for example, “your owner file number”) is configured by the centre on the point.
3. The team receives an alert on their phone, with an **On my way**button. Whoever presses it is not identified: the others see “Someone from the team is on the way”.
4. Whoever is waiting sees “Waiting for a reply” until someone presses On my way. If **5 minutes** (can be changed) go by without a reply, another alert is sent with **top priority**, and the person waiting is shown the point’s alternative text (for example, a concierge phone number).

### Alerts: ntfy

Alerts go through **ntfy**, to a **channel**. The channel name is the only key: whoever knows it can read the alerts without any account. That is why it **is not typed in**: Turnera generates it from the centre’s short name and 22 random characters (for example `mareny-` followed by letters, numbers, hyphens and underscores), impossible to guess and different in each centre. Each team member installs the ntfy app and subscribes to that channel. Only whoever has the permission *See the notification channels* (by default, Management and the administrator) sees it in full; everyone else sees only its end and a notice saying that permission is needed. The alert server and its key are configured by whoever administers the installation.

To subscribe the team there is no need to copy the channel by hand: in *Activations*, the button **Centre channel** at the top and the button **Subscribe** on each point open its channel with **Copy channel**, **Copy subscription link** and a **QR**. The link and the QR lead to that channel on the alert server: on the phone they open the ntfy app or, if it is not installed, its website. It can be opened at any time, not only when the channel is created, and seeing it does not allow changing it: requesting another one or removing it is for *Manage activation points*. Whoever manages without seeing the channels can request another one, but cannot read it.

The channel is requested **once for the whole centre** in *Preferences*, in the group *Activations*, with **Generate channel**: points without their own channel alert through that one. If a point has to alert other people, switch on **Own alert channel** on the point and Turnera generates one just for it, which whoever sees the channels has at hand when saving. If the name leaks, **Generate another channel** gives a new one: the old one stops receiving alerts and the team has to subscribe again.

Channels that were typed in by hand before Turnera generated them **still work**, so that nobody is left without alerts, but they carry the mark *Guessable* and a warning: “This channel was typed in by hand and can be guessed: generate a new one and subscribe again”. It is best to do it as soon as possible.

### What protects the point

The code **is not checked** against any list: it only stops anyone with a phone from sending random requests. To prevent abuse, a point only has **one open request** at a time (whoever arrives later looks at the same one and does not open another; the team receives a silent alert with their name and code, “Also waiting: …”, so they know who they will find), one connection cannot request more than **3 times per hour**, and a request left unattended expires after an hour. The *Test* button of each point sends a test alert to the channel.

### Who carries it

The permissions are *See activation requests*, *Manage activation points* and *See the notification channels*. The factory role **Activations** brings together the first two, to give them to a person at once; Management has all three. Seeing the channels is given separately, to whoever has to hand them out, and also needs seeing the requests to get into the screen. The list shows each request: who made it, with what code, whether an alert was sent, whether it was re-alerted and whether it was attended.

### Withdrawing or deleting a point

The usual thing is to **withdraw** a point with the switch on its row: it stops accepting requests and its QR stops working, but its old requests stay and still say where they came from. It can be put back into service.

At start-up there are often test points left over, misconfigured or with the wrong channel. That is what **Delete**is for, on each point's row, and only with the permission *Delete activation points*, which by default only the administrator has. Before doing it, it says how many requests go with it («The point and its N requests will be deleted, and its QR will stop working. This cannot be undone.») and asks for confirmation. It works the same for an active point as for a withdrawn one. Once deleted, its QR leads nowhere and cannot be recovered, and the application log records who deleted it and with how many requests.

## 12 ter. Support: talking to the Turnera team

If something doesn't work or you need help, open a ticket from *Support*, in the menu. There you see the team’s replies, without going through email. If you prefer to write by email, that works too: your message reaches the same ticket. Replies appear by themselves on screen within seconds: there is no need to reload. They may come with formatting (bold, lists, tables and links) when that explains things better.

### Who sees what

- **View my support tickets**: you see the ones you have opened yourself.
- **Open support tickets and reply**: you can open tickets and reply to your own.
- **View the support tickets of the whole centre**: you see those of the whole centre and who opened each one. To reply to or resolve someone else's you also need the permission to open and reply.

The system administrator and Management have them by default. If you are missing one, the screen tells you which.

### Opening a ticket

1. Press **New ticket**.
2. Write the **subject** and what is happening. If it helps you explain yourself, you can use **bold**, **italics** and **lists** (bulleted or numbered). You can **drag files in** (up to 5 and 40 MB in total: images up to 10 MB or short MP4, MOV or WebM videos up to 30 MB each) or choose them.
3. Tick **It stops me working** only if you cannot carry on: the ticket is dealt with sooner.
4. Press **Send ticket**. Your centre, your user and the Turnera version travel with it automatically.

### Report an incident with the lifebuoy

The button with the **lifebuoy**, at the bottom right on every screen for whoever has the permission *Notify support*, opens a ticket without leaving where you are: you tell what happened, choose the urgency and, if it helps, attach a screenshot (images only). With permission to open and view tickets, the result is one more Support ticket, with its number *T-n*, its status and the team's replies; the notice in the window has a button *Open in Support*. The screen you were on, your branch, the last error and your phone number (if you entered it) are written at the end of the first message. Anyone without those permissions still reports the incident by email, and if the ticket service is not available at that moment the app tells you that it went by email and that you will not see it in Support. Here and when writing in a ticket, the smiley button adds an emoji where the cursor is, in case you also want to tell us how you feel about it.

### Following a ticket

The list shows where each ticket stands:

- **New reply**: the team has replied and you have not read it yet. The menu shows how many there are.
- **In progress**: we are working on it.
- **Resolved**: it is done. If you reply, it opens again.
- **Closed**: resolved more than two weeks ago.

Inside a ticket you see the conversation (yours on the right, the team's on the left), you can download the files, **Attach file** (images, videos or Word, Excel or OpenDocument documents —docx, xlsx, odt, ods—, with the same limits; the support team can send you files of any type), **Send reply** and, once it is solved, **Mark as resolved**. Each person can send up to 5 new tickets per hour.

The boxes for *New ticket* and for **Send reply** have four buttons at the top: **bold**, **italics**, **bulleted list** and **numbered list**. You can also use `Ctrl`+`B` and `Ctrl`+`I`. There are no colours, sizes, links or images, and if you paste text from somewhere else, only what those four buttons understand is kept. Earlier messages still look the same.

While Support is open, the number in the menu, the list and the ticket update on their own when the team replies; what you are typing in the reply box is left untouched. If you lose the connection for a moment, they catch up anyway when it comes back.

When the team replies to a ticket you opened from Support, the reply stays here and you receive a **notification email** with the link to the ticket. The email does not include what they wrote to you or the attachments: to read it and reply, you go into Turnera using the link. If you reply to that email, we also receive it and it lands in the same ticket, but the most convenient way is to do it from here. If there is no email on your employee record, you will be able to open tickets and read the replies in Turnera, but the team will not be able to notify you by email: it is a good idea to have one filled in.

## 12 quater. Stays: residents, rooms and floor records

The **Stays** module is for centres that accommodate people: care homes and hospitals. It is optional: if the centre has not switched it on, Turnera works exactly as it does without it and nothing appears in the menu. When it is switched on, the **Stays** in the menu (with the day-to-day: residents, rooms, diets, incidents, logbook and supervision), and the rest goes where it goes in the rest of Turnera: its **reports** in *Reports*, its **settings** in *Administration › Stays* and its figures in the **Stays** section of the scorecard. That way everyone sees what their roles allow. Each customer can have **one open stay** in a place; their customer record shows it with a tag *Resident · Room 204*. As in the rest of Turnera, **nothing is deleted**: what is done wrongly is corrected, annulled with its reason or retired, and it is recorded who did it and when.

### Rooms and places

In *Stays → Rooms* shows the centre by floor, with each room, its places and who occupies each one. To start, you do not need to create them one by one: **Create rooms** asks how many floors, how many rooms per floor and how many places per room, numbers them as in a hotel (101 is the first room on floor 1), shows the result before creating anything and does not touch rooms that already exist. Afterwards you can change one room, or **several at once** (floor, zone, number of places, retire them or bring them back into use). Each room can have a **type** (single, double, en suite…, in the centre's own words) and a **zone**; the zones are the same work locations as in the roster.

A room that has ever been occupied **is never deleted**: it is retired and stops being offered. An occupied room cannot be retired until its occupant changes place or is discharged.

*[Captura] Rooms: the centre by floor, with each place and who occupies it.*

### Admission, room change and discharge

**Admission** (in *Stays → Residents*): you choose the customer (they must already have a customer record), the free place and the day. The day can be in the past, to record someone who was already in the centre, but not in the future. If they come for a limited time, the **expected end**is entered; empty means an open-ended stay. Someone who is already staying cannot be admitted again.

**Change room**: you choose the new place, and the free ones are offered *and also those occupied by another person*. If you choose another person's place (marked with a dashed line), **they swap**: each moves to the other's place and it is recorded for both.

**Discharge**: closes the stay with its day and reason, and frees the place. If the discharge was recorded by mistake, **Undo the discharge** reopens the stay in the place they left, and the undone discharge stays written in the history. An admission recorded by mistake is **annulled** with its reason. Dates cannot be in the future or earlier than the latest event on record for the stay.

*Residents* shows who is staying now and, with **On a date**, who was staying on any day in the past. It also lists ended stays. Each stay has its own record with the history of the places the person has been in.

*[Captura] Residents: who is staying now, with room changes and discharge at hand.*

### Reservations, absences and temporary stays

**Reservations** (in *Stays → Rooms*, at the top, between *Rooms* and *Reservations*; seen by whoever handles admissions): who is going to be admitted and when, with their phone number. A reservation can **hold a place**: meanwhile nobody else can occupy it, and when that person is admitted the reservation is considered fulfilled. Those that should already have arrived are marked; a reservation is cancelled by saying why the person is not coming.

**Absences**: if a person goes away for a few days (hospital, with family), it is recorded from their stay with the reason, the first day and, if known, the last. While they are away they are marked as such in the lists and do not count as a meal that day. When they return, press *Has returned*; an absence recorded by mistake is annulled. The **temporary stays** are those with an expected end: you can see which ones end within the next 30 days.

### Rates and figures of the care home

The **rates** are named monthly amounts (in *Administration › Stays*). Each stay is given a rate, or a manual amount, **from a given day**; changing a centre rate does not change what anyone already pays. **Amounts are only seen by those who have the permission *See and set rates***, which by default only the administrator and Management have: without it no amount is shown anywhere.

**The figures of the care home** are in the **Stays** section of the scorecard section of the scorecard: occupancy today and expected, revenue, incidents and falls, floor records compliance, admissions and discharges, residents away, people without a rate, temporary stays ending and the forecast for the coming weeks. With *Customise* everyone chooses which cards they see and in what order. What used to be a separate Stays scorecard no longer exists: it is all there.

### Stays reports

In *Reports*, in its section **Stays**, for management and administration, on screen, in CSV and in PDF: **Invoicing** (what each resident owes for the month, with their rate prorated by days; only with the rates permission), **Admissions and discharges** (every admission, change of place, absence, return and discharge in the period, with its reason and who recorded it), **Occupancy** (month by month: places, average occupancy, admissions, discharges, turnover and average stay) and **Records** (per resident, the floor records expected in the period and those that were made, up to 62 days).

*[Captura] The Stays section of the scorecard: occupancy, revenue, incidents, floor records and more.*

### Diets

In *Administration › Stays* you define the centre's **diet types** (diabetic, pureed, salt-free…). Then, from the list *Stays → Diets* each person is given their diet and a note of their own (allergies, textures, what they do not take). The list is meant for the kitchen and the floor: it can be seen by room or by diet, with how many people eat in the centre today. At the top, **Today's portions** gives one card per diet with how many portions to prepare and how many have instructions; in the *By diet* view, each diet opens its group with its count. Menus and ingredients are the kitchen's business; Turnera does not manage them.

*[Captura] The kitchen list: how many of each diet and what is specific to each person.*

### The floor screen and the room logbook

**The floor screen** is what staff use during the shift. Each *record* (meal, hygiene, medication, whatever the centre decides to keep track of) is a large button per person: one tap and it is recorded with the time and who did it. Whoever records identifies themselves with their **staff record and PIN**, as when clocking in, so the record carries the name of the person and not that of whoever left the session open. A record can be *something that has been done*, *an option* among several, *a quantity* or *a few words*, and it can state how many times a day it is expected: the screen then shows what is still missing today.

What is recorded wrongly **is never deleted**: it is *corrected* with another entry (the previous one stays visible, struck through) or *annulled* with its reason. Anyone who has a **usual zone** on their staff record only sees the people of that zone, unless they have the permission *See all zones*. A centre device (a tablet) can be **tied to a zone** in *Administration › Stays → Zone tablets*: on it, only what belongs to that zone will be seen and recorded, whoever signs in. Devices are registered beforehand in *Administration › Devices*, which appears when shared devices are turned on in *Preferences*.

**The room logbook** is each zone’s diary: what the incoming shift has to read before starting. Important entries are marked for handover and can be filtered. Its entries are corrected in the same way as records.

*[Captura] The floor screen: one tap per record; what is still missing today is highlighted.*

### Incidents and shift supervision

**An incident** is something that happens and has to be put in writing: a fall, an argument, a refused medication. It is opened from the person, the zone or the centre, with who, when, what happened and what was done. The centre defines its own **incident types** (the program comes with none) and for each one decides whether it *requires closure*, whether it requires saying whose it is and whether it requires saying what was done. Those that require closure stay open until whoever supervises reviews them and closes them with their conclusion. In the *Report* can be filtered by dates and downloaded as CSV and, for those who can see the Stays reports (by default, the administrator and Management), also as **PDF**, with the centre's letterhead, ready to print or file.

Incidents opened from the mobile app can carry **photos** (up to six). They show small in the incident; tapping one shows it full size, with who took it and when.

**The shift supervision** brings together on one screen what is still to be recorded today in each area, the incidents awaiting closure and the important points from the logbooks. For each person with something pending, each record appears as «Meal 1 of 3»: the times it has been recorded today out of those expected, with the time of the last one. Anything not yet recorded at all is shown in orange; hovering shows what was last recorded and by whom.

*[Captura] Incidents: open ones wait for supervision to close them.*

*[Captura] Shift supervision: what is still to be recorded today, zone by zone.*

### The record of a person staying

From *Residents*, **See their progress** opens the person's record: where they are and since when, charts of what was recorded in recent days (with the daily average and how often each thing happened), their incidents, and their **contact people**. Viewing the records of another day or of a specific person is a **query of health data** and requires the permission *See the record history*; each consultation is noted in the *access trail* in Administration, like those of the clinical history.

*[Captura] The record of a person staying: diet, rate, absences and contact people; below, what was recorded in recent days.*

### Contact people

In the record of the person staying, and in their customer record, **Contact people** keeps who to call: an older person often depends on a son, a niece or their legal guardian. For each one, the name, the relationship, the phone, the email and some notes (when to call, what errands they handle). There can be several, and one is the **main contact**, shown first: the first one entered is main without saying so, and marking another removes the mark from the previous one. They belong to the person, not to the admission, so they are still there if they are admitted again. They are changed by whoever handles admissions or edits customer records. It is worth writing the email carefully: it will be the one that gives access to the future family portal.

### Key staff

The **key staff** are the people who know the resident best and whom the family turns to: their care assistant, their nurse, their psychologist. They are not the same as the contact people, who are their family. On the resident's record, **Add** lets you pick a professional of the centre from the list or, if it is someone from outside, type their name; the role (care assistant, nursing…) is optional. There can be several. It goes with the admission: in another admission it can be someone else. Removing someone does not delete them; who removed them and when is kept. It is changed by whoever handles admissions and seen by whoever sees stays.

When typing the role, the ones already used in the centre are suggested, so as not to have «Physio» and «Physiotherapy» as if they were two different things. In the **agenda**, when booking an appointment for a resident, the professional field shows their reference professionals first with their role («Miguel · Doctor») and then the rest; any of them can be chosen. The report **Reference professionals** (Reports → Stays) lists, as of today, who looks after each resident and in what role, and also who has nobody assigned; it can be downloaded as CSV or PDF.

### Allergies

The **allergies** are recorded one by one: whether it is to a medicine, a food or something else, to what (penicillin, shellfish, latex…), the reaction and the severity. Severe ones show in red and first. They are on the resident's record and in the clinical history, because they go with the person and not with the admission. Floor staff see them too, since what is given to eat and take depends on them; they are recorded by whoever writes in the clinical history or has the permission *Allergies, assessments and restraints*. Removing one does not delete it: who removed it and when is kept. Every look is kept in the access log, like the clinical history.

### Assessments with scales

A **assessment** measures something about the person with a recognised scale and repeats it every so often to see how it evolves. Turnera comes with six: **Barthel** (how independent they are to eat, dress, wash or walk), **Lawton and Brody** (activities such as the telephone, medication or money), **Pfeiffer** (orientation and memory), **Tinetti** (balance and gait), **Norton** (risk of pressure ulcers) and **Downton** (risk of falls). **New assessment** asks for the scale and the date, shows its questions and adds up the score with its reading as it goes (for example, “Moderate dependence”). Turnera works out the score; it is not typed in by hand. You see the latest of each scale and, with *See all*, the full history. One made by mistake is cancelled with its reason and stays visible, crossed out. Other widely used scales, such as Braden or MNA, require a licence from their authors to be included in a program, which is why they are not there.

### Medication

On the resident's record, **Medication** is their medication plan. **Prescribe a medicine** lets you look it up in the catalogue of medicines marketed in Spain (CIMA, from the AEMPS) or type it, and asks for the dose (1 tablet, 5 ml…), the route, the times of the doses (08:00, 14:00, 21:00), who prescribes it and since when, and whether it ends on a date. One given only when needed is marked *As needed*, with when it is given (if in pain, if feverish…). Before saving it is checked against the recorded **allergies** : if the medicine or its active ingredient matches one, a warning appears and it can only be prescribed with *Prescribe anyway*, which stays flagged on the prescription. A prescription is not deleted: it is **stopped** with its reason.

The **Medication** in the Stays menu is the one for the shift: the day's doses grouped by time, for whom (with their room) and what. Each dose is marked **Given** or **Not given** with the reason (refuses it, not there, fasting, asleep, out of stock, vomits it or other) and a note, and it is recorded in the name of whoever gives it, also from an area's tablet with the PIN. A dose is recorded only once and at most two hours before its time. Anyone who is away that day is flagged. Below are the prescriptions *as needed* with **Give now** and what has already been given that way. Previous days can be looked up. Whoever only sees their area only sees the doses of their area. It is used by whoever records the day to day or has the health permission; prescribing and stopping, only the latter.

### Visits

The **Visits** in the Stays menu says who is visiting right now and whom they have come to see. **New visit** asks for the resident and who is coming: you choose from their contact people, which brings the relationship, or type the name. When they leave, **Has left** records the time. One recorded by mistake is cancelled and stays visible. Previous days can be looked up, and the resident's record shows their latest visits. They are recorded by whoever handles admissions or records on the floor; whoever sees stays sees them too.

### Transfers

Menu → Stays → Transfers

The **Transfers** shows who is out right now, where they have gone and with whom. **New transfer** asks for the resident and the **destination** (a hospital, the health centre); the reason, the **driver**, the **vehicle** and the **companion** are optional. The driver is chosen from the staff; so is the companion, or their name is written if it is a relative. On return, **Has returned** records the time. A resident cannot leave on a transfer while still out on another, and one recorded by mistake is annulled and stays visible.

While the transfer lasts, the resident shows as **out** on the floor, in their record («Transfer: destination») and in medication, just like an absence, so that nobody prepares a dose for someone who will not be there to receive it. The record shows their latest transfers and earlier days can be checked. They are recorded by whoever does admissions or records on the floor. The **centre's vehicles** (name and number plate) are added at the bottom of the same screen, by whoever manages the Stays settings; a vehicle that has already been used is not deleted, it is retired.

### Pocket money

On the resident's record, **Pocket money** keeps what the family leaves at the residence for their expenses and what is spent for them (hairdresser, shopping, outings), with the **balance** at the top. Each movement has whether it comes in or goes out, the amount, the concept and the date. You cannot spend more than there is. One recorded by mistake is cancelled and stays visible; an entry that has already been spent cannot be cancelled without first cancelling the outgoings. It is kept by whoever handles admissions and discharges.

### Billing and remittances

**Stays → Billing** works out what each resident pays per month and prepares the direct debit file (SEPA) to upload to the bank. It is for whoever sees and sets the rates.

1. At the top, in **Who collects**, you enter once the account holder, the **SEPA creditor identifier** (given by the bank, it looks like ES12ZZZB12345678), the account the money arrives in and the **remittance file**: *SEPA XML*, the one most banks accept, or *Cuaderno 19 (text)*, the AEB 19.14 format some still ask for. If the bank rejects one, change it here and generate it again.
2. In the rate of each resident with a publicly funded place, **Paid by the public authority** holds the part they do not pay; the resident pays the rest. That way what comes in from the bank matches the resident's part.
3. On their record, **Direct debit** keeps the signed mandate: holder, IBAN and date of signature. Only the last four digits of the account are shown; a new one replaces the previous one. Above it is the **accounting account** the accountants use for that person (it usually starts with 430, but it is free): it goes with the person and not with the stay, and it is the one that will appear in what is exported for the accounts.
4. Each month, **Work out the charges** works out each stay's amount by calendar days according to its rate. Pressing it again only adds the missing ones.
5. **Generate the remittance**, with the collection date (at least two days ahead), gathers the pending ones of whoever has a mandate. **Download the file** gives the XML or the cuaderno 19 text, as chosen, to upload in business online banking.
6. When the bank pays in, **Paid in by the bank** leaves the receipts as collected. One the bank returns is marked **Returned** with the reason and goes into the next remittance; one that is not going to be collected is marked **Not collected**.

### Stay invoices

On the same screen, **Invoices of the month** prepares the stay invoices as **drafts**: accounting reviews them and issues the correct ones, one by one or by ticking several and pressing **Issue selected**. Until issued they have no number and do not go to VeriFactu, and a draft can be **discarded**; an issued invoice is never deleted: it is credited. Once issued, they go into the centre's series of full invoices and can be downloaded as PDF.

- **How each resident is invoiced** is chosen in their record, next to the accounting account: *Month in advance* (the whole month when it starts), *Month in arrears* (the previous month for the days they were there) or *C8 place* (the regional government pays it all).
- If someone billed a month in advance arrives or leaves mid-month, they are invoiced the full month and, when preparing the following month, a **part payment** is issued for the days they were not there (based on when their rate ends).
- **Fixed supplements** (high-protein diet, single room…): in the record, next to how they are invoiced, with their monthly amount, their VAT (or the stays one) and from which day. They go in the monthly invoice with the stay, the whole month in advance or, in arrears, for the days they had them; anything left over goes in the credit note. In a C8 place the resident pays them, in their own invoice for the previous month. *Remove* stops charging it from that day; what has already been invoiced does not change.
- The **C8 places** go in a single invoice to the regional government, with one line per resident and month (name, tax ID and days). In **VAT and regional government** its name, tax ID and address are entered, and whether the invoice is *monthly* or *quarterly* (the quarterly one comes out in January, April, July and October).
- Rate amounts **include VAT**. The VAT is the centre's stays VAT (10% by default) unless the rate has its own, set when editing it in the Stays settings.
- A full invoice needs the payer's tax ID: if it is missing, the draft says so and cannot be issued until it is added to the record.
- Preparing again duplicates nothing: it only adds what is missing.

#### Extras of the month

What is done for a resident apart from the place itself (hairdressing, podiatry, clothing repairs and name labels…) is recorded in **Extras of the month**, on the same screen: the day, the resident, the **service** from the catalogue (it brings its price and VAT) or a free description, the quantity and the price. The day and the service stay filled in after each entry, so you can go straight through the hairdressing or podiatry sheet for many residents. What is recorded but not charged is marked **Do not invoice**.

- When you **prepare the invoices for the month**, the pending extras up to the **cutoff day** go into each resident's invoice: into their draft for the month if they have one or, if not, into a separate invoice. The cutoff is set in *Cutoff and escorts* (for example, the 25th: from the 26th of the previous month to the 25th go in); empty, the calendar month. What is recorded after the cutoff goes to the next month.
- The **escorts** come by themselves from the transfers with an escort and a return time: they are charged by the hour, **rounded down to the quarter hour** (5 h 10 min is 5 hours; 3 h 40 min, 3.5), at the hourly price set in *Cutoff and escorts*. Without a price they are not charged. In C8 places they are never charged, and on a resident's record you can tick *Escorts are not charged*.
- An extra that is already in a draft or an invoice is not changed or cancelled: the draft is discarded (and the extra becomes pending again) or a credit note is issued for the invoice.

### Quality and family portal

**Stays → Quality** is the record of **compliments, suggestions, complaints and non-conformities**. Each one says where it comes from (the family, the centre itself or an inspection), whom it concerns (a resident or the whole centre), who reports it and what has happened. Open ones come first, from oldest to newest; they are closed by recording **what was done**, which is compulsory for a complaint or a non-conformity. Nothing is deleted. The official complaints form is still separate: this is for finding out in time and keeping a record of what is done.

On the same screen is the **family portal**, closed until it is opened. The family goes to */familia*, picks the residence if there is more than one and types their email; if that email is in the **contact people** of someone staying there, they get a link valid for 30 days. With it they see the room, the diet, the key staff and the latest visits, and can **write to the residence**: what they write goes into Quality with their name and email, and Management gets a notice. Anyone who is not there gets the same reply and is sent nothing. If that email is removed from the record, the link stops showing anything.

Incidents from the last 30 days and care records from the last week are only shown if ticked. The colours, the font and a welcome text make the portal look like the residence's website; the logo is the centre's. To put it on the website there is a link without its own styles that whoever runs the website places wherever they like. All of this belongs to the permission *Quality and family portal*.

### Restraints

A **restraint** is any means that limits the person's movements: bed rails, belts, wrist straps, a table on the chair or a medicine given to restrain them. It is only recorded with **who prescribes it** and when, **who gives consent** (the person or their representative) and when, the reason, when it is applied and **every how many days it is reviewed** (30 by default). One past its review date shows in red. *Review* records who reviews it and what is concluded; *Withdraw*, the day and the reason. Nothing is deleted. They are seen by whoever sees stays or works on the floor; they are recorded by whoever has the permission *Allergies, assessments and restraints*. Turnera records what the centre decides: which restraint protocol it follows and what its regional regulations require are set by the centre.

### Permissions and roles

There are sixteen Stays permissions, which each centre shares out as it likes: *Allergies, assessments and restraints*, *Quality and family portal*, *See the stays*, *Admissions, room changes and discharges*, *Configure the rooms*, *Configure Stays*, *Record day-to-day care*, *See the record history*, *Correct other people's records*, *Open incidents*, *Close incidents*, *Write in the room logbook*, *See and set rates*, *See Stays in the scorecard*, *See the diets* and *See all zones*. When the module is switched on in a centre, two starting roles are created, which the centre can change: **Estancias · planta** (view stays, record, open incidents, write in the logbook and view diets) and **Estancias · supervisión** (the above plus admissions and discharges, close incidents, see the record history, correct and see all zones). *Management* has them all; amounts are only for those who have the rates permission.

## 12 quinquies. Financial management: pay, settlements, fixed costs and cash forecast

The **Financial management** is for knowing what the centre costs: how each professional is paid, what has to be paid each month to collaborators, and the fixed costs. It is optional: if the centre does not have it switched on, it does not appear anywhere, neither in the menu nor in the permissions. It needs Till, because what a collaborator earns comes from what was charged.

Its two permissions are **delicate** and by default only the *System administrator*: *Costs and pay* (each professional’s pay and the fixed costs) and *Collaborator settlements*. The professional themselves sees none of this.

### How each professional is paid

In *Staff*, the actions menu of each row has **Pay**. There are three ways of paying:

- **Fixed monthly cost**, for salaried staff: what they cost the company per month (gross pay, the company’s Social Security and prorated extra payments). Nothing is settled for a salaried person.
- **Percentage of what they bill**, on what is billed excluding VAT.
- **Amount per act or hour**: a list of services, each with its rule. It can be an amount *per act* (€120 for each toxin), *per appointment hour* (€80/h for assessment consultations) or a *percentage* of what is billed excluding VAT (40% of the threads). What is not on the list is not paid. This is the usual arrangement with a doctor who comes to do certain procedures.

In the last two you choose whether they are paid for what was **done** (even if the customer still owes it) or only what was **charged**. The appointment hour is how long the appointment lasted; if the sale does not have a single appointment of that professional, the service duration is counted.

Each change is saved with its **“Valid from” date** and applies until the next one, so a raise is a new version and the previous one stays in the **history**. Saving with a date that already exists changes that version. Removing a version makes the previous one apply again.

### Collaborator settlements

In *Financial management → Settlements* you choose the month and the collaborators of the site appear: those paid a percentage or an amount per act or hour. Choosing one shows line by line what they did, with the customer, the service, the rule applied and what they are owed. A session paid with a voucher counts as an act done; in a percentage it adds nothing, because the money came in when the voucher was sold. A **refund** is deducted in the month it is made.

**Download Excel** exports the detail to check it against the collaborator’s invoice. Income tax withholding and VAT go on that invoice: Turnera does not calculate them. **Mark as paid** fixes the amount: if something in that month changes afterwards, what was paid does not move and the screen warns what it would come to now. If it was marked by mistake, *Undo payment* sets it as pending again.

### Fixed costs

In *Financial management → Fixed costs* you record what the site pays each month, quarter or year, whether it sells or not: rent, utilities, insurance, loans, advisers. Each cost has its start date and, if it ends, its end date. Quarterly and yearly ones are spread over the months in the column *Per month*. A cost that goes up is closed with its “Until” date and another is added with the new amount, so each month counts the right one. Removing a cost is for one entered by mistake.

### Cash forecast

In *Financial management → Cash forecast* you choose the period (this month, this quarter, this year or up to a given month) and you see the result the site will leave: what has already happened from the start of the period until today, plus what is expected from today to the end. Everything is excluding VAT. At the top, three cards: the expected result, how much of the income is already secured (what was sold and the appointments already booked) and the worst month of the period.

How each line is forecast:

- **Services and products.** First come the appointments already booked and not yet charged, at list price. If what was sold in the same period last year comes to more, the gap is filled with that pace; with no previous year, with the daily average of the last three months.
- **Salaried staff.** The company cost of each professional with monthly pay, day by day, according to what is recorded in their pay.
- **Collaborators.** Up to today, what they have earned under their pay scheme, the same as in the settlement. In the forecast, the same share of income they have had over the last three months.
- **Fixed costs.** Their monthly equivalent, spread over the days.

It does not start from any balance: it says what the centre generates in the period, not the money in the bank. Instalments of deferred payments do not appear separately, because those sales already count as income on the day they were made.

If the centre has Financial management, whoever can see the costs also finds in the *Scorecard* a section with the expected result for the month and the year, staff cost over income, the break-even point, collaborator settlements, fixed costs and a chart of the result month by month (months with a forecast are dashed). Whoever cannot see the costs does not see that section.

## 13. When something goes wrong

### “It will not let me book the appointment”

If it says the slot is taken, there is another appointment in that room or with that member of staff at that time; the warning says which. If it says the device is in use, there is another appointment at that time that needs the same one, even if it is in another room.

### “Which version are you on?”

At the bottom of the left-hand menu, under the active delegation. It is the first thing to look at before writing in, because a fault already fixed in a later version is solved by updating.

### “A menu option is missing”

It is a permission your user account does not have. Whoever administers the centre can give it to you.

### “The customer is not receiving the portal code”

Check that the address on their record is the one you are typing, that the email gateway is set up, and that it has not landed in their junk folder. The code expires after fifteen minutes: once that time is up you have to ask for another.

### “The end-of-day till does not balance”

Look first at the payments split across several payment methods and the visits that were left owing. An unpaid visit is not in the till: it is in *Unpaid visits*.

### “I cancelled an appointment by mistake”

It cannot be recovered: you have to create it again with the same details.

### «An appointment has disappeared from the diary»

The most common cause is that it was cancelled and the diary has ticked *Hide cancelled*, which each workstation remembers. To be sure, open the cancellations report with a range that includes the day of the appointment and type the customer's name: if it was cancelled, it shows when, with which reason and by which user. If each person logs in with their own user, you also know who it was.

## 14. The professionals’ native app (Android)

**This section is the manual of the native Android app, not of the web.** It is a separate application, installed on a phone or a tablet, designed so that staff can clock in, see their own information and handle maintenance orders without sitting in front of the computer. Everything else in this manual is about the Turnera web.

What is done in the app ends up in the same place as what is done on the web: clockings in *Clocking in and working time* and orders in *Maintenance*.

### Downloading and installing the app

Menu, at the very bottom → App for professionals

The app is downloaded from Turnera's own website, in the entry *App for professionals* of the menu (seen by whoever works with the staff module). The page states the available version and its size, and has the button **Download the app**. **It only works on Android devices**, whether phones or tablets; there is no version for iPhone or iPad.

1. Open that page on the centre's device, signed in, and press *Download the app*.
2. When opening the file, Android asks for permission to install apps from the browser: it is granted for this installation only.
3. Press *Install*, open it and sign in with the centre's user.

To update it, download and install the new version over the previous one; nothing is lost. If no version has been published on the server yet, the page says so.

### Signing in and choosing the site

The app is set up **once per device**: the centre’s management signs in with its user and chooses the site where it will be. From then on the device keeps that shared session and each professional identifies themselves with their **PIN**, the same one they clock in with on the web.

### In a care home: my shift, floor, medication and incidents

In centres with the Stays, the personal app (for whoever logs in with their own user) adds to the menu what is needed on the floor. Each option shows only to those with the matching permission, the same as on the web, and what is recorded goes under the name of whoever logged in. On a shared phone they do not appear.

- **My shift today**: your own shift and zone, who else works that shift (with a green dot if they have already clocked in) and the zone's handover notebook since yesterday. From here you go straight to the floor, medication or opening an incident. A night shift is still «today» until it ends.
- **Floor**: the residents of the zone with what is still to record today. Tapping one records their entries (meals, changes, vital signs…), just like the floor screen on the web. It works **without coverage**: what is recorded is saved on the phone and sent automatically as soon as there is signal. At the top you can see how many entries are waiting to be sent and whether the centre rejected any.
- **Medication**: the day's doses ordered by time, with *Given* or *Not given*; when marking «Not given» the reason is chosen (refuses it, not there, fasting…). This one does need a connection, so that two doses are never confirmed from two phones.
- **New incident**: the type, who it concerns, what happened and what was done, with up to **six photos** taken with the camera. Photos are sent reduced and without location data, and can be seen in the incident from the web. Photos can be added up to a day after opening it even if it is already closed.
